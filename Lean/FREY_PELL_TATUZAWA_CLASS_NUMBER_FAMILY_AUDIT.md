# Tatuzawa and the Pell squarefree-parameter family

## Abstract

For the squarefree-parameter family already constructed in the Pell radical
reduction, write

\[
 c_n=A_ny_n^2,\qquad D_n=4A_n,\qquad
 R_n=\log\varepsilon_{A_n}=H_n+O(1),
 \tag{0.1}
\]

where \(A_n\equiv23\pmod {24}\), the positive fundamental discriminants
\(D_n\) are pairwise distinct, and

\[
 h_nR_n=\sqrt {A_n}\,L(1,\chi_{D_n}).
 \tag{0.2}
\]

Tatuzawa's theorem can be applied to this family with its exact exceptional
quantifier.  For every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\), apart from at most one index
whose conductor is above the explicit threshold,

\[
 L(1,\chi_{D_n})>
 0.655\,\delta D_n^{-\delta}.
 \tag{0.3}
\]

The injectivity of \(A_n\) is exactly what transports "one possible
character" to "one possible index".  It follows, after passing beyond the
possibly exceptional index for each fixed \(\delta\), that

\[
 \log L(1,\chi_{D_n})=o(\log A_n),
 \qquad
 \log(h_nR_n)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\log A_n.
 \tag{0.4}
\]

The threshold in this full-tail formulation is ineffective, since the
possible exceptional character is not identified.  The statement (0.3)
itself remains effective outside one character.

This is the strongest genuine consequence of the exceptional-discriminant
route.  Its direction does not close the radical problem.  Tatuzawa supplies
a **lower** bound for \(L(1,\chi)\); a lower bound for \(A_n\) from (0.2)
uses an **upper** bound for \(L(1,\chi)\) together with a lower bound for the
class number.  In particular, (0.4) and \(h_n\ge1\) give only

\[
 \log A_n\ge(2-o(1))\log H_n=o(H_n),
 \tag{0.5}
\]

not the required \(\log A_n\ge(1-o(1))H_n\).  The distinction from the
Heilbronn--Linfoot imaginary-quadratic application is structural: the
imaginary class-number formula has no regulator, whereas in (0.2) the known
large regulator absorbs Tatuzawa's lower bound.

The additional norm-two structure can also be evaluated exactly.  Put

\[
 \alpha_n=s_n+y_n\sqrt {A_n}.
\]

Then \(N(\alpha_n)=2\) and
\(\varepsilon_{A_n}=\alpha_n^2/2\).  Thus the ramified prime above \(2\)
is generated by the totally positive element \(\alpha_n\), even in the
narrow ideal group.  This does **not** remove another genus factor: it only
identifies which ramified generator is trivial, while the product of the odd
ramified primes represents the nontrivial narrow sign class.  The previously
proved divisibility \(2^{\omega(A_n)-1}\mid h_n\) is unchanged.

Yokoi's invariant makes the remaining obstruction particularly transparent.
For this family it is exactly

\[
 m_{A_n}=\left\lfloor{\varepsilon_{A_n}\over A_n}\right\rfloor
        =2y_n^2.
\]

Consequently Yokoi's effective lower bound is
\(h_n\gg_s A_n^{1/2-1/s}/\log(A_n(2y_n^2+1))\), outside one possible
discriminant.  Its denominator is \(R_n+O(1)\), so this is precisely the
Tatuzawa bound (3.7), not an independent estimate.  The Richaud--Degert
case with remainder \(-2\) occurs here exactly when \(y_n=1\), the case in
which \(A_n=c_n\) and the radical target is already closed.

## 1. Inputs already proved for the family

The preceding fundamental-unit audit proves, for \(n\ge1\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s_n^2-3r_n^2&=1,\\
 c_n=s_n^2-2&=A_ny_n^2,\\
 \varepsilon_{A_n}
  &=(c_n+1)+y_ns_n\sqrt {A_n},\\
 A_n&\equiv23\pmod {24}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Here \(A_n>1\) is squarefree, \(\varepsilon_{A_n}\) is the fundamental
unit of the maximal order of \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt {A_n})\), and the \(A_n\)
are pairwise distinct.  Thus

\[
 D_n=4A_n
 \tag{1.2}
\]

is a positive fundamental discriminant, and
\(\chi_{D_n}=(D_n/\mathord\cdot)\) is a nonprincipal primitive real even
Dirichlet character of conductor \(D_n\).  Pairwise distinctness also implies
\(A_n\to\infty\): below any fixed bound there are only finitely many positive
integers, and no one of them can occur twice.

There is no unit of norm \(-1\) in these fields.  With \(h_n\) the ordinary
class number, the real quadratic analytic class-number formula therefore has
the already-audited normalization

\[
 h_nR_n=\sqrt {A_n}\,L_n,
 \qquad L_n:=L(1,\chi_{D_n})>0.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The explicit local data are

\[
 \chi_{D_n}(2)=0,\qquad \chi_{D_n}(3)=-1,
 \tag{1.4}
\]

and Louboutin's pointwise estimate gives

\[
 L_n\le {\log D_n+6\over8}.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

## 1A. The norm-two element and the ramified prime at two

The squarefree decomposition contains a second exact Pell equation:

\[
 s_n^2-A_ny_n^2=2.
 \tag{1A.1}
\]

Set \(\alpha_n=s_n+y_n\sqrt {A_n}\).  Both real embeddings of
\(\alpha_n\) are positive: its first embedding is positive and its conjugate
is \(2/\alpha_n>0\).  Moreover,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \alpha_n^2
  &=s_n^2+A_ny_n^2+2y_ns_n\sqrt {A_n}\\
  &=2(c_n+1)+2y_ns_n\sqrt {A_n}
   =2\varepsilon_{A_n}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1A.2}
\]

Since \(A_n\equiv7\pmod8\), the rational prime \(2\) is ramified in
\(K_n=\mathbf Q(\sqrt {A_n})\).  In
\(\mathcal O_{K_n}=\mathbf Z[\sqrt {A_n}]\), its unique prime is

\[
 \mathfrak p_2=(2,1+\sqrt {A_n}),
 \qquad (2)=\mathfrak p_2^2.
 \tag{1A.3}
\]

The integers \(s_n\) and \(y_n\) are odd, so
\(\alpha_n\in\mathfrak p_2\).  Since
\(|N_{K_n/\mathbf Q}(\alpha_n)|=2\), comparison of ideal norms gives

\[
 (\alpha_n)=\mathfrak p_2.
 \tag{1A.4}
\]

Because \(\alpha_n\) is totally positive, (1A.4) holds as a narrow
principalization, not just as an ordinary principalization.

It is tempting to subtract one more bit from the genus lower bound, but that
would double-count the same sign relation.  Let the odd prime divisors of
\(A_n\) be \(q\), and put
\(\mathfrak p_q=(q,\sqrt {A_n})\).  Then

\[
 \prod_{q\mid A_n}\mathfrak p_q=(\sqrt {A_n}).
 \tag{1A.5}
\]

The right side is ordinary principal but not narrow principal.  Indeed
\(\sqrt {A_n}\) has signs \((+,-)\), and the absence of a norm-minus-one
unit prevents multiplication by a unit from making both signs positive.
Thus (1A.5) is the relation among the odd ramified classes in the ordinary
class group, while in the narrow class group its product is the nontrivial
kernel element of
\(\mathrm {Cl}^+(K_n)\to\mathrm {Cl}(K_n)\).

There are \(t=\omega(A_n)+1\) prime-discriminant factors in \(4A_n\).
Classical genus theory still gives

\[
 \#\bigl(\mathrm {Cl}^+(K_n)/\mathrm {Cl}^+(K_n)^2\bigr)
   =2^{t-1}=2^{\omega(A_n)}.
 \tag{1A.6}
\]

Since \(h_n^+=2h_n\), this remains exactly

\[
 2^{\omega(A_n)-1}\mid h_n.
 \tag{1A.7}
\]

The norm-two solution therefore locates the narrow ambiguous classes but
does not increase the genus lower bound for the ordinary class number.

## 2. Tatuzawa with the correct quantifiers

Tatuzawa's Theorem 2 states the following.  Fix
\(0<\delta<1/2\).  Among the nonprincipal primitive real characters of
conductors

\[
 D\ge \max\{e^{1/\delta},e^{11.2}\},
 \tag{2.1}
\]

there is at most one exception to

\[
 L(1,\chi)>0.655\,\delta D^{-\delta}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The phrase "at most one" belongs to a fixed choice of \(\delta\).  The
possible exceptional character may depend on \(\delta\).  It is therefore
invalid to choose \(\delta=1/\log D\) separately for every conductor and
then retain one global exceptional character.

For a fixed \(\delta\), define

\[
 \mathcal E_\delta=
 \left\{n:
 D_n\ge\max\{e^{1/\delta},e^{11.2}\},\quad
 L_n\le0.655\,\delta D_n^{-\delta}
 \right\}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

If \(m,n\in\mathcal E_\delta\), Tatuzawa gives
\(\chi_{D_m}=\chi_{D_n}\), hence \(D_m=D_n\).  Injectivity then gives
\(m=n\).  Consequently

\[
 \#\mathcal E_\delta\le1.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

This is stronger than a density-one conclusion and is the exact useful role
of the Bennett--Walsh injectivity theorem in the analytic route.

Because \(D_n\to\infty\), for every fixed \(\delta\) one may pass beyond
both the conductor threshold and the one possible exceptional index.  Hence

\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}{\log L_n\over\log D_n}\ge-\delta.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

On the other hand, (1.5) gives

\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}{\log L_n\over\log D_n}\le0.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Letting fixed \(\delta>0\) tend to zero proves

\[
 L_n=D_n^{o(1)}=A_n^{o(1)}.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

There is no quantifier error in this last passage.  The definition of a
limit permits a different tail threshold for each fixed \(\delta\).  What is
lost is effectivity: the threshold must lie beyond an unknown exceptional
index, and the exceptional index can vary with \(\delta\).

## 3. The real-quadratic Brauer--Siegel balance

Substituting (2.2) into (1.3) gives, outside the one possible index,

\[
 h_nR_n>
 0.655\,\delta\,4^{-\delta}
 A_n^{1/2-\delta}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The pointwise upper bound (1.5) gives for every index

\[
 h_nR_n\le
 {\sqrt {A_n}\bigl(\log(4A_n)+6\bigr)\over8}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Equations (3.1)--(3.2), for every fixed \(\delta\), are equivalent to the
full-tail logarithmic statement

\[
 \log(h_nR_n)
 =\frac12\log A_n+o(\log A_n).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

This is the fixed-degree Brauer--Siegel balance specialized to the actual
family, with the lower side obtained from Tatuzawa and the upper side from
the accepted explicit character estimate.  Since \(R_n=H_n+O(1)\), it also
determines the possible class-number scale:

\[
 \log h_n
 =\frac12\log A_n-\log H_n+o(\log A_n).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Formula (3.4) is an identity of scales, not an independent lower bound for
\(A_n\).  Using only \(h_n\ge1\) in (3.3) gives

\[
 \log R_n\le\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\log A_n,
 \tag{3.5}
\]

and hence (0.5).  The explicit upper estimate (1.5) sharpens this to the
already proved polynomial bound

\[
 A_n\gg {R_n^2\over(\log R_n)^2},
 \tag{3.6}
\]

with the stronger audited constants.  Tatuzawa does not change its order.

The polarity can be seen without asymptotics.  From a lower bound
\(L_n\ge\ell_n\), (1.3) gives

\[
 h_nR_n\ge\sqrt {A_n}\,\ell_n,
 \qquad
 h_n\ge {\sqrt {A_n}\,\ell_n\over R_n}.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

This says that a field with large \(A_n\) and the prescribed regulator must
have enough ideal classes.  It does not say that \(A_n\) is large.  If one
adds a generic class-number upper bound \(h_n\le M(A_n)\), then (3.7) yields

\[
 R_n\ge {\sqrt {A_n}\,\ell_n\over M(A_n)},
 \tag{3.8}
\]

a lower bound for the regulator (or, for a sufficiently strong \(M\), an
upper bound for \(A_n\)).  This is the opposite direction from the radical
closure.  With a Minkowski/character-sum scale
\(M(A)\ll\sqrt A\log A\), (3.8) reduces to
\(R\gg A^{-\delta}/\log A\), which is vacuous here.

By contrast, an upper bound \(L_n\le U_n\) and the lower bound \(h_n\ge1\)
give

\[
 R_n\le\sqrt {A_n}\,U_n,
 \tag{3.9}
\]

which is why Louboutin's theorem, rather than Tatuzawa's, produces (3.6).

## 4. Why the Heilbronn mechanism does not transfer

Tatuzawa immediately applies his theorem to the Heilbronn--Linfoot problem
for imaginary quadratic fields.  For a negative fundamental discriminant,
the analytic class-number formula has the shape

\[
 h(-D)=C_D\sqrt D\,L(1,\chi_{-D}),
 \tag{4.1}
\]

where \(C_D\) is an explicit roots-of-unity constant.  There is no
regulator, so a lower bound for \(L(1,\chi)\) is directly a lower bound for
the class number.

For the positive discriminants in this Pell family, the corresponding
formula is (1.3).  The same lower bound controls only the product \(h_nR_n\).
Here \(R_n\asymp H_n\) is precisely the large quantity under study, and it
absorbs the lower estimate.  Zero repulsion and the "one exceptional
character" theorem improve lower bounds for the other \(L\)-values; neither
removes the regulator from (1.3).  Thus the classical imaginary-quadratic
class-number argument cannot be imported by changing signs.

The fixed local facts \(\chi_{D_n}(2)=0\) and
\(\chi_{D_n}(3)=-1\) do not, in any accepted theorem used here, rule out the
one possible exceptional real character.  Treating them as such an
exclusion would be an additional zero-free theorem, not a consequence of
Tatuzawa.

## 5. Average theorems and sparse selection

Average and density-one results encounter two separate barriers.

First, injectivity alone does not make the family equidistributed among
fundamental discriminants.  A set of exceptional discriminants of size
\(o(X)\), or even \(O(X^\theta)\) for a fixed \(\theta>0\), can contain an
arbitrary logarithmically sparse injective sequence.  The Pell growth gives
at least logarithmically many family indices with \(A_n\le X\), but no lower
density among all discriminants.  Therefore an average theorem cannot be
restricted to \(D_n=4A_n\) without an independent selection or
equidistribution theorem for this particular squarefree-kernel sequence.

Second, even a hypothetical uniform bound \(L_n=O(1)\) for every member of
the family would give from \(h_n\ge1\) only

\[
 A_n\gg R_n^2.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

This still has \(\log A_n=O(\log R_n)=o(R_n)\).  Thus typical-size results
for \(L(1,\chi)\) fail at the required scale even before the sparse-selection
problem is addressed.

Genus theory has the same limitation.  Its audited divisibility
\(2^{\omega(A_n)-1}\mid h_n\), combined with an upper bound for \(L_n\),
improves (3.9) by the factor \(2^{\omega(A_n)-1}\).  But
\(2^{\omega(A)}=A^{o(1)}\) uniformly for squarefree \(A\), and there is no
proved family-specific lower bound forcing enough distinct prime factors to
turn this into an exponential-in-\(R_n\) estimate.  Tatuzawa supplies another
lower bound for the same class-number variable; the two lower bounds do not
multiply.

## 6. Continued fractions, regulator spacing, and a scalar model

The generic continued-fraction estimate

\[
 R(A)\ll\sqrt A\log A
 \tag{6.1}
\]

again inverts only to a polynomial lower bound for \(A\).  Conversely, the
elementary size of a Pell unit gives \(R(A)\ge\tfrac12\log A+O(1)\), which
is an upper bound for \(A\) in terms of \(R\), not the desired lower bound.
The facts that the \(A_n\) are distinct and that \(R_n=H_n+O(1)\) is linearly
spaced in \(n\) do not order the \(A_n\) numerically.  No accepted generic
spacing theorem turns those facts into
\(A_n\ge\exp((1-o(1))R_n)\).

There is a strict scalar compatibility model for all the inequalities above.
Let \(t\to\infty\) and set

\[
 \sqrt A=t,\qquad R=t,\qquad h=1,\qquad L=1.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Then the class-number scalar identity \(hR=\sqrt A\,L\) is exact.  Every
fixed-power Tatuzawa lower profile \(L\gg A^{-\delta}\), every logarithmic
upper profile \(L\ll\log A\), and the genus lower bound with one prime factor
are compatible with (6.2).  Nevertheless

\[
 \log A=2\log t=o(t)=o(R).
 \tag{6.3}
\]

This is not asserted to be a sequence of squarefree Pell discriminants.  It
is a logical countermodel showing that the scalar analytic inequalities,
even taken simultaneously, do not entail the coefficient-one conclusion.
Any closure must use additional arithmetic of (1.1), not another algebraic
rearrangement of the same class-number formula.

## 6A. Yokoi and Richaud--Degert do not improve the scale

Yokoi writes the fundamental unit as

\[
 \varepsilon_A={t_A+u_A\sqrt A\over2}.
 \tag{6A.1}
\]

In the present family,

\[
 t_A=2(c+1)=2Ay^2+2,
 \qquad u_A=2ys.
 \tag{6A.2}
\]

Yokoi's Theorem 1.1, valid here because \(A\ge23\), identifies his invariant
as \(m_A=\lfloor t_A/A\rfloor=\lfloor\varepsilon_A/A\rfloor\).  Therefore

\[
 m_A=2y^2,
 \qquad t_A=A m_A+2.
 \tag{6A.3}
\]

In the notation of his Theorem 1.2 this also gives
\((a_A,b_A)=(2,0)\).  Proposition 4.1(1), for every fixed
\(z\ge11.2\) and \(A\ge e^z\), consequently gives, with at most one
exceptional discriminant,

\[
 h_A>
 {0.3275\,4^{(z-2)/(2z)}\over z}\,
 {A^{(z-2)/(2z)}\over\log((2y^2+1)A)}.
 \tag{6A.4}
\]

Here the exponent of \(4\) uses \(A\equiv3\pmod4\).  The exact estimate
\(2Ay^2+1<\varepsilon_A<2Ay^2+2\) shows uniformly that

\[
 \log((2y^2+1)A)=R_A+O(1).
 \tag{6A.5}
\]

Thus (6A.4) is only

\[
 h_A\gg_z {A^{1/2-1/z}\over R_A},
 \tag{6A.6}
\]

which is Tatuzawa's product bound divided by the known regulator.  It is not
an independent lower bound of size \(\sqrt A/R_A\).  Choosing \(z\) as a
function of \(A\) and retaining one exceptional discriminant would repeat
the quantifier error from Section 2; the theorem is stated for each fixed
\(z\).

The standard Richaud--Degert definition is
\(A=k^2+r\), \(-k<r\le k\), with \(r\mid4k\).  Yokoi's Proposition
3.3(2) says that the remainder \(r=-2\) case is equivalent to

\[
 m_A=2,\qquad (a_A,b_A)=(2,0).
 \tag{6A.7}
\]

By (6A.3), this occurs in the family exactly when \(y=1\).  But then
\(A=c\), so the radical target is already immediate.  The norm-two equation
for a moving coefficient \(y>1\) does not make the family Richaud--Degert.

Finally, Yokoi's Theorem 4.1 says that, for fixed \(h_0\) and fixed
\(q>2\), only finitely many fields satisfy both
\(\varepsilon_A<A\exp(A^{1/q})\) and \(h_A\le h_0\).  Its regulator
hypothesis is \(R_A<\log A+A^{1/q}\), which already imposes a power-size
lower relation between \(A\) and \(R_A\).  It cannot be used to derive that
relation for this family.  No unconditional pointwise Yokoi or
Richaud--Degert theorem audited here supplies the missing estimate
\(h_A\gg\sqrt A/R_A\) for all family members.

## 7. Exact missing input

The desired sufficient statement remains

\[
 \boxed{\ \log A_n\ge(1-o(1))R_n
        =(1-o(1))H_n.\ }
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Within the class-number identity, (7.1) would follow from a genuinely new
family-specific theorem forcing

\[
 R_n\le(1+o(1))\log A_n.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

Equivalently, one would need an independent class-number lower bound strong
enough that

\[
 h_n\ge
 {\sqrt {A_n}\,L_n\over(1+o(1))\log A_n}.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Writing (7.3) down using (1.3) merely restates (7.2); it is not a proof.
Tatuzawa gives instead the weaker, regulator-dependent lower bound (3.7).
Average class-number results do not apply pointwise to this sparse family,
and generic continued fractions stop at (6.1).

The minimum new theorem must exploit the simultaneous special equations

\[
 \varepsilon_A=3r^2+ys\sqrt A,
 \qquad s^2-3r^2=1,
 \tag{7.4}
\]

or directly control the square divisor \(y_n^2\mid c_n\).  The accepted
Tatuzawa/Siegel, class-number, genus, average-value, and generic regulator
package does not prove (7.1).

## 8. Formal companion

`IUTThreeClosures/FreyPellTatuzawaClassNumberFamilyAudit.lean` verifies the
division-free scalar core:

1. an injective discriminant map pulls an at-most-one exceptional
   discriminant back to an at-most-one exceptional index;
2. a lower \(L\)-bound gives a lower bound for \(hR\);
3. an upper \(L\)-bound together with \(h\ge1\) gives an upper bound for
   \(R\);
4. a lower \(L\)-bound together with an upper bound for \(h\) gives the
   opposite-direction inequality; and
5. the norm-two and Yokoi trace-coordinate identities (1A.1) and (6A.3); and
6. the scalar profile (6.2) satisfies both directions while remaining
   subcritical.

It does not formalize Dirichlet characters, Tatuzawa's theorem, the analytic
class-number formula, or asymptotic notation.
