# The prime-eleven Chebyshev curve: complete rational points by Coleman--Chabauty

## 1. Theorem and trust boundary

Use the first-kind Chebyshev convention

\[
T_0(X)=1,\qquad T_1(X)=X,\qquad
T_{n+2}(X)=2X T_{n+1}(X)-T_n(X).
\]

Then

\[
T_{11}(X)=1024X^{11}-2816X^9+2816X^7-1232X^5+220X^3-11X.
\]

This note gives an accepted-computation proof of the following unconditional
classification.

**Theorem.** If \(T,y\in\mathbf Q\) and

\[
y^2=4T_{11}(T)+5,
\]

then

\[
(T,y)\in
\{(-1,1),(-1,-1),(1,3),(1,-3)\}.
\]

In particular, there is no rational, and hence no integral, solution with
\(T>1\).

No point-search or height cutoff, conjectural analytic rank, GRH, BSD,
`abc`, Szpiro, or an equivalent open conjecture is used.  The non-Lean trust
boundary is stated exactly: Magma V2.29-9 performs an exact arithmetic
2-Selmer computation and exact divisor arithmetic; SageMath 10.9 performs
the certified 5-adic Coleman computation.  Sections 4--6 give the complete
mathematical bridge from those reported values to the rational-point list.
The companion Lean file checks only the scalar algebra and exposes the CAS
classification as an explicit hypothesis; it does not pretend to reimplement
either CAS computation in the Lean kernel.

## 2. The genus-five curve and good reduction at 5

Put

\[
F(X)=4096X^{11}-11264X^9+11264X^7-4928X^5+880X^3-44X+5.
\]

The original equation is \(y^2=F(T)\).  For the 5-adic calculation use the
isomorphic monic model

\[
H:\quad v^2=h(X):=\frac{F(X)}{4096},\qquad v=\frac y{64}.
\]

Sage computes

\[
\operatorname{disc}(h)
=-\frac{16847368837908939}
        {1329227995784915872903807060280344576}
=-\frac{3^{10}11^{11}}{2^{120}}.
\]

Thus \(h\) is monic of degree 11, the curve is nonsingular of genus 5, all
coefficients are 5-integral, and its discriminant is a 5-adic unit.  The
proper smooth odd-degree model has good reduction at 5.

Exact finite-field enumeration gives

\[
H(\mathbf F_5)=
\{\bar O,(0,0),(1,2),(1,3),(4,1),(4,4)\},
\]

and \(\bar h\) has the single simple root 0.  This is an enumeration of a
finite set, not a bounded rational-point search.

The five visible rational points on \(H\) are

\[
O,\qquad (-1,\pm1/64),\qquad(1,\pm3/64).
\]

They reduce to five of the six displayed points.  The only additional
residue disc is the one above the finite Weierstrass point \((0,0)\).

## 3. Exact rank two and explicit half-divisors

For the exact descent Magma uses the reduced isomorphic model

\[
C_q:\quad Y^2=q(X),
\]

where

\[
q(X)=-2X^{11}+22X^9-88X^7+154X^5-110X^3+22X+5
     =F(-X/2).
\]

The change is \(X=-2T\), with the same \(y\)-coordinate.  Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1={}&X^5+X^4-4X^3-3X^2+3X+1,\\
u_9={}&X^5-X^4-4X^3+3X^2+3X-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Magma checks exactly that

\[
q-1=-2(X-2)u_1^2,\qquad
q-9=-2(X+2)u_9^2.
\]

In the Jacobian \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(C_q)\), let the pairs below denote
Mumford divisors:

\[
H_1=(u_1,-1),\quad H_9=(u_9,-3),\quad
D_1=(X-2,1),\quad D_9=(X+2,3).
\]

The exact Jacobian arithmetic returns

\[
2H_1=D_1,\qquad2H_9=D_9.
\]

Under \(X=-2T\), the point divisors \(D_1,D_9\) correspond respectively to
\([(-1,1)-O]\) and \([(1,3)-O]\) on the original curve.

`TwoTorsionSubgroup(J)` has order one.  The unconditional call
`PhiSelmerGroup(q,2 : ReturnRawData)` returns

\[
\operatorname{Sel}^{(2)}(J/\mathbf Q)\simeq(\mathbf Z/2)^2.
\]

For the monic change used internally by the descent, the two transformed
Mumford polynomials are

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_1={}&X^5-2X^4-16X^3+24X^2+48X-32,\\
U_9={}&X^5+2X^4-16X^3-24X^2+48X+32.
\end{aligned}
\]

Because these polynomials have odd degree, their standard Mumford
squareclasses are represented by \(-U_i(\theta)\), not by
\(U_i(\theta)\).  The transcript checks that both classes belong to the
reported Selmer subgroup, are nonzero and distinct, and span a subgroup of
order four.  This sign is not cosmetic: omitting it makes both membership
tests fail by the same \([-1]\) class.

The Kummer injection

\[
J(\mathbf Q)/2J(\mathbf Q)\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{Sel}^{(2)}(J/\mathbf Q)
\]

and the two exhibited independent images show that the Kummer image itself
has order four.  Since \(J(\mathbf Q)[2]=0\),

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}J(\mathbf Q)/2J(\mathbf Q)
=\operatorname{rank}J(\mathbf Q)=2.
\]

This uses neither a parity assertion nor finiteness of a Tate--Shafarevich
group.  In particular, \(H_1,H_9\), and hence \(D_1=2H_1,D_9=2H_9\), are
independent.  The subgroup generated by \(D_1,D_9\) has finite index in
\(J(\mathbf Q)\).

## 4. A nonvanishing annihilating differential

Use the holomorphic basis

\[
\omega_i=X^i\frac{dX}{2v},\qquad 0\leq i\leq4
\]

on the monic good-reduction model \(H\).  Sage computes the Coleman logarithm
vectors \(\ell_-\) and \(\ell_+\) of
\([(-1,1/64)-O]\) and \([(1,3/64)-O]\).  Their coordinatewise valuations
are

\[
(1,1,1,1,1),\qquad(1,1,1,1,1),
\]

and the logarithm matrix has rank two.  Its integral right-kernel lattice has
dimension three.  More explicitly, division of both logarithm rows by 5 and
reduction modulo 5 gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&2&4&1\\
2&2&1&2&2
\end{pmatrix},
\]

which has rank two over \(\mathbf F_5\).  Thus the kernel reduction is
precision-stable and every vector in it lifts to an exact 5-adic annihilator.
Primitive normalization gives the kernel basis

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(1,0,0,0,4),\\
&(0,1,0,3,1),\\
&(0,0,1,2,0).
\end{aligned}
\]

The script then solves explicitly for a kernel lift whose reduced coefficient
vector is

\[
\bar c=(1,0,1,2,4).
\]

The two annihilating dot products have valuation 39 at input precision 40.
Equivalently, the precision-stable kernel calculation certifies an actual
primitive 5-adic kernel vector \(c=(c_0,\ldots,c_4)\) with this reduction.
Put

\[
\omega=(c_0+c_1X+\cdots+c_4X^4)\frac{dX}{2v},\qquad
\lambda(P)=\int_O^P\omega.
\]

The logarithms of the two independent point divisors span the Mordell--Weil
logarithm space, so \(\omega\) annihilates all of \(J(\mathbf Q)\).  One does
not need saturation of the displayed subgroup: if \(mQ\) belongs to it for
some nonzero integer \(m\), then in the field \(\mathbf Q_5\),

\[
\log(Q)=m^{-1}\log(mQ).
\]

Consequently

\[
\lambda(P)=0\qquad(P\in H(\mathbf Q)).
\]

The numerator of the reduced differential has values

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
X&0&1&4\\ \hline
1+X^2+2X^3+4X^4&1&3&4.
\end{array}
\]

At infinity the differential has nonzero value because its top coefficient
is \(\bar c_4=4\).  At the Weierstrass point, take \(v\) as local parameter.
Since

\[
2v\,dv=h'(X)\,dX,\qquad h'(0)\equiv1\pmod5,
\]

the local coefficient of \(\omega\) is again a unit.  Thus the reduction of
\(\omega\) is nonzero at every one of the six points of \(H(\mathbf F_5)\).

## 5. A local one-zero lemma at 5

The usual global Coleman estimate is sometimes quoted with a condition such
as \(p>2g\).  That condition is unnecessary here because the chosen
differential is nonvanishing in every residue disc.

**Lemma.** Let \({\cal D}\) be a residue disc of a smooth curve over
\(\mathbf Z_5\).  If a regular differential \(\eta\) has nonzero reduction
at the point below \({\cal D}\), then a Coleman primitive of \(\eta\) has at
most one zero in \({\cal D}\).

**Proof.** Translate an integral local parameter so that one zero has
parameter \(t=0\).  A second point in the same disc has
\(t\in5\mathbf Z_5\); if it is distinct, put \(m=v_5(t)\geq1\).  Write

\[
\eta=(a_0+a_1t+a_2t^2+\cdots)dt,
\qquad a_0\in\mathbf Z_5^\times.
\]

The difference of primitive values is

\[
a_0t+\frac{a_1}{2}t^2+\frac{a_2}{3}t^3+\cdots.
\]

The first term has valuation \(m\).  Every term of degree \(n\geq2\) has
valuation at least

\[
nm-v_5(n)>m,
\]

because \((n-1)m>v_5(n)\).  The first term cannot cancel, so two distinct
zeros are impossible. \(\square\)

Applying the lemma to \(\omega\), every residue disc of \(H(\mathbf Q_5)\)
contains at most one zero of \(\lambda\).

## 6. Six local zeros and all rational points

There is already one zero in each of the six residue discs.

* The point \(O\) is a zero by the choice of base point.
* The four rational affine points \((-1,\pm1/64)\) and
  \((1,\pm3/64)\) are zeros because \(\omega\) annihilates
  \(J(\mathbf Q)\).  They occupy the four discs above \(X=4\) and \(X=1\).
* Modulo 5, \(h\) has the single simple root 0.  Hensel's lemma gives a
  unique \(\alpha\in\mathbf Z_5\) with \(h(\alpha)=0\) and
  \(\alpha\equiv0\pmod5\).  Put \(W=(\alpha,0)\).  Since

  \[
  \operatorname{div}(X-\alpha)=2W-2O,
  \]

  the class \([W-O]\) is 2-torsion.  The 5-adic abelian logarithm kills
  torsion, hence \(\lambda(W)=0\).  Sage independently returns all five
  basis integrals from \(O\) to \(W\) as zero, with valuation `+Infinity`.

The local lemma now proves that these are the only six 5-adic zeros, one in
each disc.  Every rational point is a zero, so

\[
H(\mathbf Q)\subseteq
\{O,(-1,\pm1/64),(1,\pm3/64),W\}.
\]

The point \(W\) is not rational.  Magma verifies that \(q\) is irreducible
over \(\mathbf Q\) and has no rational root; the rational linear changes
relating \(q,F,h\) preserve irreducibility.  Therefore

\[
H(\mathbf Q)=\{O,(-1,\pm1/64),(1,\pm3/64)\}.
\]

Returning via \(y=64v\) proves the theorem in Section 1.
