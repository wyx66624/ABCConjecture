# P29 class-group Galois-module amplification audit

## Scope and notation

This note records a rigorous consequence of a hypothetical nonzero element of
the 2-primary class group of

\[
  K=\mathbf Q(a),\qquad a^{29}=2.
\]

It does **not** prove `Cl(K)[2] = 0`.  Put

\[
  N=\mathbf Q(a,\zeta_{29}),\qquad
  G=\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbf Q)
    \simeq C_{29}\rtimes C_{28},
\]

where `C_28` acts faithfully on `C_29`, and put
`H = Gal(N/K) \simeq C_28`.  Thus `G/H` has 29 elements.  Here `N` is the
Galois closure of `K`; below, `F` denotes a different field, namely the
Galois closure over `N` generated by the conjugates of a hypothetical
quadratic Hilbert class extension of `K`.

The conclusion proved here is:

> If `Cl(K)[2]` is nonzero, then
> `Hom(Cl(N),F_2) = (Cl(N)/2)^vee` has a `G`-stable submodule of dimension
> 28 or 29 over `F_2`.  In particular, the 2-rank of `Cl(N)` is at least 28.

The argument excludes only the one-dimensional trivial possibility.  It does
not exclude the 28-dimensional augmentation constituent.

## Arithmetic input

The following standard facts are used.

1. `X^29 - 2` is 2-Eisenstein, so `[K:Q]=29`.  The pure-field index
   criterion (equivalently, the shifted 29-adic Eisenstein calculation
   below, together with the calculation at 2) gives
   `O_K = Z[a]` and
   `disc(K)=29^29 2^28`.
2. At 29,
   `(X+2)^29-2` is Eisenstein: its constant term is
   `2(2^28-1)`, and `v_29(2^28-1)=1`.  Consequently 29 has a unique prime
   `P_29` in `K`, with `(29)=P_29^29`.  Hence the class of `P_29` has order
   dividing 29 and every quadratic class character vanishes on it.
3. At 2 there is a unique prime `P_2=(a)`, which is principal.  Every
   quadratic class character therefore also vanishes on `P_2`.
4. `K` has signature `(1,14)`.  Since `-1` realizes the nontrivial sign at
   its unique real place, the sign map of the unit group is onto.  The
   narrow-to-wide class group exact sequence consequently gives
   `Cl^+(K)=Cl(K)`.  A quadratic extension associated with a class character
   is therefore split at that real place and has signature `(2,28)`.  It is
   not a totally real field: the fourteen complex places of `K` remain.
5. Direct modular exponentiation gives
   `ord_29(2)=28`.  For example
   `2^14 = -1 (mod 29)` (indeed `2^7=12` and `12^2=144= -1`), while the
   proper divisors of 28 are ruled out by this identity and
   `2^4=16 != 1 (mod 29)`.  Therefore `Phi_29` is irreducible over `F_2`.

Items 1--4 should be explicit hypotheses if the Galois-module theorem is
formalized independently of the local number-field development.  Item 5 is
finite arithmetic and needs no external axiom.

## From one class character to a permutation-module quotient

Assume that a nonzero quadratic class character of `K` exists, and let
`E/K` be its quadratic Hilbert class extension.  Form over `N` the compositum
`F` of the 29 `G`-conjugates of `EN/N`.  Unramifiedness is preserved by base
change, so `F/N` is an everywhere-finite-unramified elementary abelian
2-extension.  Item 4 controls the real place of `E/K`; it does not make
`F` totally real (indeed `F` contains the cyclotomic field inside `N`).

The first base-changed extension `EN/N` is nontrivial.  Indeed, the unique
quadratic intermediate field of the cyclic extension `N/K` is ramified above
29 (locally it is the quadratic part of the totally ramified cyclotomic
extension), whereas `E/K` is unramified there.  Thus `E` is not contained in
`N`, and the character space constructed below is nonzero.

Write

\[
  V=\operatorname{Gal}(F/N),\qquad W=V^\vee.
\]

Class field theory embeds `W` as a `G`-stable subspace of
`Hom(Cl(N),F_2)`.  The conjugates of the original nonzero character span
`W`, so there is a surjective `F_2[G]`-map

\[
  P=\mathbf F_2[G/H]\twoheadrightarrow W.
\]

Let `1` be the constant vector and let

\[
  A=\{(x_s)_{s\in G/H}:\sum_s x_s=0\}.
\]

Because `|G/H|=29` is odd, the summation map splits by the constant vector,
and

\[
  P=\mathbf F_2\,1\oplus A,\qquad \dim A=28.
\]

Restrict `A` to the normal subgroup `C_29`.  It is the nontrivial part of
the regular `F_2[C_29]`-module, hence is isomorphic to
`F_2[X]/(Phi_29(X))`.  Since `ord_29(2)=28`, `Phi_29` is irreducible over
`F_2`; therefore `A` is irreducible already as a `C_29`-module, and hence as
a `G`-module.  It is not isomorphic to the trivial module.  Thus the only
`G`-submodules of `P` are

\[
  0,\quad \mathbf F_2 1,\quad A,\quad P.
\]

(For a mixed submodule, its projections would give a nonzero homomorphism
between the two nonisomorphic simple modules, which is impossible.)  Hence a
nonzero quotient `W` has dimension `1`, `28`, or `29`.  It remains to rule
out dimension 1.

## Exclusion of the one-dimensional central quotient

Suppose `dim_F2 W=1`.  Since `Aut(C_2)` is trivial, the action of `G` on
`V=C_2` is trivial, and the Galois group of `F/Q` fits into a central
extension

\[
  1\longrightarrow C_2\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbf Q)\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1.
\]

### The decomposition-group complement

The field `N/Q` has a unique prime above 29 and its decomposition group is
all of `G`: locally the cyclotomic part has ramification degree 28 and the
Kummer part contributes degree 29.  By item 2 the prime `P_29` splits in
`E/K`; the same holds for every conjugate.  Therefore the unique prime of
`N` above 29 splits in `F/N`.

There are exactly two primes of `F` above 29.  Since
`|Gal(F/Q)|=2|G|`, the decomposition group of either one has order `|G|`.
It intersects the central `C_2=Gal(F/N)` trivially because the relative
prime splits.  Its injection into `G` is therefore an isomorphism, so it is
a complement.  The central extension splits, and hence

\[
  \operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbf Q)\simeq C_2\times G,
  \qquad F=NM

\]

for a quadratic field `M/Q` with `M cap N=Q`.  Notice that the existence of
a complement alone does **not** imply `F=N`; the following local argument is
essential.

### Local square classes at 2 and 29

Because `F/N` is unramified, `M/Q` can ramify only at finite primes already
ramified in `N/Q`, namely 2 and 29.  No assertion about the sign of `M` is
needed.  Listing all quadratic fields whose fundamental discriminant is
supported on these two primes gives, up to a rational square,

\[
  M=\mathbf Q(\sqrt d),\qquad
  d\in\{-1,-2,-29,-58,2,29,58\}.
\]

At 2, `ord_29(2)=28`, so `Q_2(zeta_29)/Q_2` is unramified of degree 28,
whereas adjoining a 29th root of 2 contributes odd totally ramified degree
29.  Hence `N_2/Q_2` has a unique quadratic subfield, its unramified
quadratic subfield.  It is `Q_2(sqrt(29))`, since `29=5 (mod 8)`.
Moreover `P_2=(a)` splits in `E/K`, so the unique prime above 2 also splits
in `F/N`.  It follows that `M_2` must be this quadratic subfield (or be
trivial).

This already removes every candidate except `d=29`.  Indeed, the trivial
2-adic square class is represented by an odd unit congruent to 1 modulo 8,
and the unramified quadratic class by an odd unit congruent to 5 modulo 8.
Here `29=5 (mod 8)`, while `-1=7 (mod 8)` and `-29=3 (mod 8)`; the four
even candidates are ramified.  Thus only `d=29` is locally trivial or equal
to the unique quadratic subfield at 2.

At 29, `N_29/Q_29` likewise has a unique quadratic subfield, the quadratic
subfield of the cyclotomic extension, namely `Q_29(sqrt(29))`.  The local
square class of 2 is the nonsquare unit class because `(2/29)=-1`.
As an independent check, at 29 the positive candidates behave as follows:

* `d=2` gives the unramified nontrivial quadratic class at 29, not the
  ramified class of `sqrt(29)`;
* `d=58` gives the other ramified square class `2*29`, again not the class
  of `sqrt(29)`;
* only `d=29` matches the quadratic subfield at 29.

But `Q(sqrt(29))` is already the unique quadratic subfield of
`Q(zeta_29)`, hence is contained in `N`.  This contradicts `M cap N=Q`.
The one-dimensional quotient is therefore impossible.

Combining this with the permutation-module calculation proves that `W` has
dimension 28 or 29.  Since
`W` is a submodule of `Hom(Cl(N),F_2)=(Cl(N)/2)^vee`, the 2-rank of
`Cl(N)` is at least 28.  This is a statement about the canonical character
space (or, dually, a quotient of `Cl(N)/2`), not a canonical embedding of a
chosen 28-dimensional module into `Cl(N)[2]`.

## Why this does not close the P29 problem

The prime-29 decomposition group gives a complement even when the kernel is
large.  For a concrete group-theoretic model, let `G` act on 29 points and
let `A` be the 28-dimensional augmentation module above.  Then
`A semidirect_product G` contains `G` as a decomposition-style complement,
although `A` is nonzero.  If `H=C_28` fixes the point 0 and
`A_0={v in A : v(0)=0}`, then `A_0 semidirect_product H` has index two in
`A semidirect_product H`; this models a quadratic extension over `K` whose
normal closure still has the full augmentation kernel while the selected
prime splits.

Thus the local argument kills the central/trivial constituent but supplies
no contradiction to the 28-dimensional irreducible constituent.  Closing
the route would require an independent upper bound `dim_F2 Cl(N)[2] < 28`,
or another arithmetic restriction excluding `A` from the unramified class
group.  Neither is proved here.

## Lean formalization boundary

The finite arithmetic foothold is now kernel checked in
`IUTThreeClosures/P29FiniteCore.lean`.  It proves
`orderOf (2 : ZMod 29) = 28` from three exact modular-power tests using
kernel reduction (`decide`), not `native_decide`.  Its `#print axioms` ledger
contains only Lean's standard `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and, for the order
lemma, `Classical.choice`; in particular it contains no `sorryAx` or native
decide axiom.

The remaining finite representation-theoretic core is a reasonable
standalone Lean target:

1. identify the 29-point permutation module with constants plus the kernel
   of summation;
2. use irreducibility of `cyclotomic 29 F_2`, derived from the checked order
   calculation, to classify its quotients as dimensions `1`, `28`, `29`.

The repository currently has no checked bridge from these finite-module
statements to Hilbert class fields, decomposition groups, local square
classes, and the class-field-theoretic embedding of `W`.  The new order file
therefore formalizes only its stated finite arithmetic fact, not the
mathematical class-group conclusion.  Those representation-theoretic,
number-field, and class-field steps remain explicit Lean TODOs rather than
imported axioms.
