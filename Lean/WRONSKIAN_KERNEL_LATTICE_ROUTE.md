# Wronskian kernel lattice: exact limits of the naked geometry-of-numbers route

This note is an offline mathematical analysis of the free prime-weight
derivative route.  Every displayed implication is proved below before its Lean
counterpart is discussed.  No abc estimate, small-vector estimate, or desired
height bound is packaged as input data.

## 1. The two coefficient rows

Let `a+b=c` be a primitive positive triple and let

```text
D_x(n) = sum_{p|n} (n/p) v_p(n) x_p,
```

where the weights `x_p` are integers.  Primitivity makes the prime supports of
`a`, `b`, and `c` disjoint.  On their union, the compatibility form

```text
mathcal A(x) = D_x(a) + D_x(b) - D_x(c)
```

has coefficients

```text
p | a :  alpha_p =  a v_p(a)/p,
q | b :  beta_q  =  b v_q(b)/q,
r | c : -gamma_r = -c v_r(c)/r.                         (1.1)
```

The Wronskian form

```text
mathcal B(x) = a D_x(b) - b D_x(a)
```

has coefficients `-b alpha_p`, `a beta_q`, and `0` on these three blocks.
Thus the remaining selection problem really is

```text
mathcal A(x)=0,   mathcal B(x)!=0,                        (1.2)
```

together with an upper bound for

```text
|D_x(a)|/a + |D_x(b)|/b.                                 (1.3)
```

The last quantity, rather than the raw norm of the weights, is what enters the
proved arithmetic Wronskian inequality.

## 2. Exact minors and Smith normal form

Let `A=(A_1,...,A_s)` and `B=(B_1,...,B_s)` be integral rows, assume that
`A` is not the zero row, let `g=gcd(A_1,...,A_s)>0`, and put

```text
Lambda = {x in Z^s : A.x=0}.
```

For every pair `i,j` with nonzero minor

```text
Delta_ij = B_i A_j - B_j A_i,
```

the vector

```text
x_i=A_j/d_ij,  x_j=-A_i/d_ij,  x_l=0 otherwise,
d_ij=gcd(A_i,A_j),                                       (2.1)
```

lies in `Lambda`, has `B.x=Delta_ij/d_ij != 0`, and satisfies

```text
||x||_infinity <= max(|A_i|,|A_j|)/d_ij.                 (2.2)
```

This is the sharp elementary minor bound.  There is also an exact Smith
normal form statement:

```text
B(Lambda) = (Delta/g) Z,
Delta = gcd_{i<j}(Delta_ij).                              (2.3)
```

Here gcds are taken nonnegative.  To prove (2.3), write `A'=A/g`, so that
`A'` is primitive, and let `Delta'` be the gcd of the minors formed from
`A'` and `B`.  The original minors are `g` times these minors, hence
`Delta=g Delta'`.  Choose integers `u_i` with `sum u_i A'_i=1` and set
`t=sum u_i B_i`.  Every number

```text
B_j-A'_j t = sum_i u_i(A'_i B_j-A'_j B_i)
```

is divisible by `Delta'`.  Hence `A.x=0`, equivalently `A'.x=0`, implies
`Delta' | B.x`.  Conversely, every reduced minor is the `B`-value of the
kernel vector `A'_i e_j-A'_j e_i`; a Bezout combination realizes `Delta'`.
Since `Delta'=Delta/g`, this proves (2.3).  The nonzero-row hypothesis is
essential: for `A=0`, division by `g=0` is meaningless and the image is
instead the ordinary ideal generated by the entries of `B`.

Neither (2.2) nor (2.3) yields a sublinear nondegenerate vector bound in
general.  For every `H>=1`, take

```text
A_H=(H,1,0),   B_H=(1,0,0).                              (2.4)
```

The vector `(0,0,1)` is a norm-one degenerate kernel vector.  But a kernel
vector with `B_H(x)!=0` has `x_2=-H x_1`, hence norm at least `H`.  The vector
`(1,-H,0)` attains `H`.  This infinite family proves that the linear order in
(2.2) cannot be improved for arbitrary pairs of rows merely by invoking
Siegel, Minkowski, or successive minima.

## 3. Why the elementary abc minors are tautological

Choose `p|a` and `r|c`, put `e=v_p(a)`, `f=v_r(c)`, and set

```text
alpha=(a/p)e,   gamma=(c/r)f,   d=gcd(alpha,gamma).
```

The divided two-coordinate witness is

```text
x_p=gamma/d,   x_r=alpha/d.
```

It satisfies (1.2), and (1.3) is exactly

```text
L = c e f/(p r d).                                       (3.1)
```

Because `gcd(a/p,c/r)=1`, every common divisor of `(a/p)e` and `(c/r)f`
divides `ef`; hence `d|ef`.  If `R=rad(abc)`, then

```text
R L/c = (R/(pr)) (ef/d),                                 (3.2)
```

a positive integer.  Therefore the Wronskian conclusion `c<=R L` produced by
this minor is only the assertion that the integer in (3.2) is at least one.
The same calculation applies to an `a`--`b` minor.  Thus explicit two-prime
minors are valid witnesses, but they contain no height gain.

## 4. Projection to the powerful-part lattice

Write

```text
a=A r_a,  b=B r_b,  c=C r_c,
```

where `r_n=rad(n)` and `A=n/r_n` is the powerful part.  The proved local
divisibility says that any compatible derivative values can be written

```text
D(a)=A x,  D(b)=B y,  D(c)=C z,
A x+B y=C z.                                             (4.1)
```

The original relation is

```text
A r_a+B r_b=C r_c.                                       (4.2)
```

Subtracting `r_b` times (4.1) from `y` times (4.2) gives the determinant
identity

```text
A(r_a y-r_b x)=C(r_c y-r_b z).                           (4.3)
```

Since `A` and `C` divide the coprime numbers `a` and `c`, they are coprime.
Consequently there is an integer `k` with

```text
r_a y-r_b x=C k.                                         (4.4)
```

The Wronskian is `AB(r_a y-r_b x)=ABC k`, so `k=0` is exactly the degenerate
direction.  For `k!=0`, divide (4.4) by `r_a r_b` and apply the triangle
inequality:

```text
|x|/r_a+|y|/r_b
  >= |y/r_b-x/r_a|
   = C|k|/(r_a r_b)
  >= C/(r_a r_b)
   = c/R.                                                (4.5)
```

Thus the second/avoiding direction in the projected lattice has an intrinsic
lower bound equal to the abc quality ratio itself.

There is also a precise converse in the *relaxed* lattice, in which every
multiple of `A,B,C` is allowed as a derivative value.  For `k=1`, Cramer's
rule gives

```text
x=(r_a z-B)/r_c,   y=(r_b z+A)/r_c.                      (4.6)
```

The first integrality congruence is `r_a z=B (mod r_c)`.  It is solvable
because `gcd(r_a,r_c)=1`.  Multiplying it by `r_b` and using
`a+b=0 (mod r_c)` proves the second congruence automatically.  All solutions
are obtained by replacing

```text
(x,y,z) by (x+r_a t, y+r_b t, z+r_c t).                  (4.7)
```

If `u=x/r_a`, `v=y/r_b`, then `v-u=c/R`.  Choosing an integer `t` nearest to
`-(u+v)/2` yields

```text
|u+t|+|v+t| <= c/R+1.                                    (4.8)
```

Indeed, with `m=(u+v)/2` and `h=(v-u)/2`, the left side is
`|m+t-h|+|m+t+h|=2 max(|m+t|,|h|)<=1+2|h|`.

If `mu_0(a,b,c)` denotes the least relaxed nondegenerate cost, (4.5)--(4.8)
give the exact interval

```text
c/R <= mu_0(a,b,c) <= c/R+1.                             (4.9)
```

It follows that a uniform estimate `mu_0 <= K_epsilon R^epsilon` is, up to
changing `K_epsilon` by one, equivalent to the abc inequality.  The desired
nondegenerate selection lemma is therefore not a cheap missing lattice lemma:
at the projected level it is a reformulation of the target height estimate.

The word "relaxed" matters.  When one of `a,b` is `1`, or when integral prime
weights do not realize all powerful-part multiples, (4.6) need not come from
actual weights.  The lower bound (4.5), however, applies to every actual
weight vector.

## 5. The exact image of integral prime weights

For `n>1`, set `r=rad(n)`, `Q=n/r`, and

```text
tau(n) = gcd_{p|n} ((r/p) v_p(n)).                        (5.1)
```

Since

```text
(n/p)v_p(n)=Q (r/p)v_p(n),
```

Bezout's identity proves the exact image formula

```text
{D_x(n): x_p in Z} = Q tau(n) Z.                         (5.2)
```

Thus the actual derivative-value lattice is a sublattice of (4.1), obtained
by additionally requiring `tau(a)|x`, `tau(b)|y`, and `tau(c)|z`.  More prime
coordinates do not by themselves create more freedom after projection to the
three values that occur in the Wronskian estimate.

Some checks clarify this factor.  If `n` is squarefree, then `tau(n)=1`: for
each prime divisor `l` of `r`, the term indexed by `p=l` is not divisible by
`l`, and no prime outside `r` can divide all `r/p`.  If `n=p^m`, then
`tau(n)=m`.  The latter produces a genuine logarithmic loss.

For example, for every `m>=1` the primitive triple

```text
(a,b,c)=(1, 2^m-1, 2^m)                                 (5.3)
```

has

```text
D_x(c)=m 2^(m-1) x_2.
```

Compatibility gives `D_x(b)=D_x(c)`, and the Wronskian is `D_x(b)`.  If it is
nonzero, then `x_2!=0`, so every actual compatible vector satisfies

```text
|D_x(b)|/(2^m-1)
  >= m 2^(m-1)/(2^m-1)
   > m/2.                                                (5.4)
```

For every `m>=2`, the exact image formula (5.2) also constructs a nonzero
common derivative value by intersecting the two nonzero image subgroups for
`b` and `c`.  Thus the paper argument gives an unconditional infinite family
strictly disproving any uniform constant bound for the normalized derivative
cost.  The current Lean theorem proves the conditional lower bound but does
not yet formalize this image-intersection existence step.  The family does
not disprove a `c^epsilon` bound, since the loss is only logarithmic; rather,
it shows that the realization factor `tau(n)` cannot be silently discarded.

## 6. Large and small support: the viable hybrid statement

Let `s=omega(abc)>=3`.  A generic one-row Siegel lemma may give a short nonzero
vector in `ker(mathcal A)`, but Section 2 proves that it need not avoid
`ker(mathcal B)`.  If a structure-sensitive theorem did give the optimistic
bound

```text
||x||_infinity <= K_s H_A^(1/(s-1)),                     (6.1)
```

where `H_A` is the maximum coefficient in (1.1), then

```text
H_A <= c log_2(c)/2,
sum_{p|ab} v_p(abc)/p <= s log_2(c)/2.                   (6.2)
```

Equations (6.1)--(6.2) and the Wronskian inequality would give, schematically,

```text
c^(1-1/(s-1)) <= R times explicit logarithmic factors.   (6.3)
```

The main exponent becomes `(s-1)/(s-2)=1+1/(s-2)`.  This explains why large
support could in principle handle a prescribed epsilon.  But (6.1) is
precisely the avoidance estimate invalid for arbitrary rows, and (4.5) shows
that its special abc version cannot ignore the quality ratio.  Its constants
in `s` would also have to be controlled; the lower bound by the product of the
first `s` primes can absorb `exp(O(s))`, but not arbitrary growth.

For bounded `s`, a fixed-`S` Baker or S-unit theorem is insufficient because
the set of primes `S` still varies.  The exact complementary statement needed
for a threshold `K` is

```text
omega(abc)<=K  ==>  log c <= (1+epsilon) log R+C(epsilon,K),  (6.4)
```

uniformly in the primes themselves.  Existing fixed-support finiteness does
not supply (6.4).  A logically sound hybrid program is therefore:

1. prove a radical-sensitive avoidance estimate only for sufficiently large
   support, with explicit dependence on `s`;
2. prove the genuinely uniform bounded-cardinality S-unit estimate (6.4);
3. combine them at a threshold depending on `epsilon`.

Neither component is presently proved.  The determinant calculation narrows
the target: further progress must use arithmetic structure beyond the naked
kernel lattice, for example congruence distribution, archimedean cancellation,
or a uniform S-unit input.  The route is not discarded; the circular
coefficient-only version has been isolated exactly.

## 7. Lean coverage

`IUTThreeClosures/WronskianKernelLattice.lean` formalizes:

- the explicit two-coordinate nondegenerate minor witness and its linear norm
  bound;
- the determinant identity (4.3) and coprime cancellation producing (4.4);
- the exact normalized gap and lower bounds (4.5);
- the degenerate compatible line (4.7) with `k=0`.

The Smith image formula (2.3), the nearest-integer upper bound (4.8), and the
exact integral-weight image (5.2) are proved mathematically here.  They should
be added to Lean only against a stable finite-support API; none is represented
by a hypothesis field or an assumed target estimate.
