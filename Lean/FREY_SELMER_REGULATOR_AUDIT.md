# Frey--2-Selmer, local-height, and regulator route

## 1. Result of the audit

Let

\[
  a,b,c\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\qquad a+b=c,\qquad (a,b)=(b,c)=(c,a)=1,
\]

and attach the full-two-torsion Frey curve

\[
  E=E_{a,b}:y^2=x(x-a)(x+b).
  \tag{1.1}
\]

This route produces a useful new bridge, but not a proof of abc.

* Ordinary `2`-descent and the Cassels--Tate pairing control a finite
  squareclass/support ledger.  They do not control valuation magnitude,
  canonical heights of generators, or the regulator.
* The three visible nonzero rational `2`-torsion points do not supply a
  large canonical point: their Neron--Tate heights are exactly zero even
  when their integral coordinates are arbitrarily large.
* Local Neron heights do see the full multiplicative exponent, but with
  signs that conserve exactly across the three nonzero `2`-torsion points.
  Passing to positive parts destroys the global canonical-height identity.
* The archimedean real period does recover the source height:

  \[
    \log c=2\log(1/\Omega_E)+O(\log(1+\log c)).
    \tag{1.2}
  \]

* Assuming the strong Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer leading-term formula, (1.2)
  turns abc into a precise half-slope estimate for one normalized BSD
  quotient.  That estimate is not supplied by BSD, GRH, Selmer dimension,
  or the Cassels--Tate pairing.

Thus the genuinely new surviving target is

\[
 \log\!\left(
   \frac{\operatorname{Reg}(E)\,|\Sha(E)|\,\prod_p c_p(E)}
        {\Lambda_E\,|E(\mathbf Q)_{\rm tors}|^2}
 \right)
 \le \frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\log N_E+O_\varepsilon(1),
 \tag{1.3}
\]

where

\[
  \Lambda_E=\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}.
\]

Equation (1.3) is a clean analytic--arithmetic balance, not a consequence
of the qualitative BSD statement.  On the Frey locus it is essentially
abc-equivalent, up to the sublinear error in (1.2).  No unconditional abc,
Szpiro, BSD, GRH, or Lang-height assertion is made below or in Lean.

## 2. Exact Frey data and full rational two-torsion

The three nonzero points of order two are

\[
  T_0=(0,0),\qquad T_a=(a,0),\qquad T_b=(-b,0).
  \tag{2.1}
\]

The already audited invariants are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_4(E)&=16(a^2+ab+b^2),\\
 \Delta(E)&=16a^2b^2c^2,\\
 j(E)&=\frac{256(a^2+ab+b^2)^3}{a^2b^2c^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

At every odd prime `p | abc`, primitivity gives `v_p(c_4)=0`, so the
displayed model is minimal and multiplicative there, with

\[
 v_p(\Delta_{\min})=2e_p,
 \qquad e_p=v_p(abc).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

At two, `a^2+ab+b^2` is odd for every primitive positive triple.  Hence
`v_2(c_4)=4`; any possible minimal change can alter the invariant
differential by at most one factor of two.  This bounded two-adic ambiguity
will only change absolute constants in the period comparison.

## 3. What full `2`-descent actually gives

Since `E[2]` is rational,

\[
 H^1(\mathbf Q,E[2])
   \simeq
 (\mathbf Q^\times/\mathbf Q^{\times2})^2.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Let `S_f` be the finite primes dividing `2abc`.  A `2`-Selmer class is
unramified outside `S=S_f union {infinity}`.  Over `Q`, the group of
squareclasses unramified outside `S` is generated by `-1` and the primes in
`S_f`.  Therefore

\[
 \dim_{\mathbf F_2}\operatorname{Sel}_2(E/\mathbf Q)
 \le 2(|S_f|+1).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The Kummer exact sequence gives

\[
 0\longrightarrow E(\mathbf Q)/2E(\mathbf Q)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sel}_2(E/\mathbf Q)
 \longrightarrow \Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[2]
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Because `dim E(Q)[2]=2`, (3.2)--(3.3) imply only

\[
  \operatorname{rank}E(\mathbf Q)\le 2|S_f|.
  \tag{3.4}
\]

This is a support-count estimate.  In particular, it has no coefficient
`log p` and no valuation weight `e_p`.

For a local squareclass, the valuation survives only modulo two.  The
support/parity profile used in the companion Lean module is

\[
  e\longmapsto
  \bigl(\mathbf 1_{e>0},e\bmod2\bigr).
  \tag{3.5}
\]

The unit squareclass is additional finite data, but it does not restore the
integer `e`.

## 4. A strict actual-family obstruction

Consider the primitive subfamily

\[
 (a_n,b_n,c_n)
   =\left(3^{2n+2},2,3^{2n+2}+2\right).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

At `p=3`,

\[
 v_3(a_nb_nc_n)=2n+2,
 \qquad
 v_3(\Delta(E_n))=4n+4.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The support/parity profile (3.5) is `(1,0)` for every `n`.  Moreover, the
unit entries are fixed in `Q_3^*/Q_3^{*2}`: `b_n=2`,
`c_n \equiv 2 \pmod 3`, and `a_n` is a square.  Thus the elementary
squareclass channel at three is constant while the multiplicative depth is
unbounded.

The formal theorem is stronger than the statement that one convenient
linear bound fails.  For every function

\[
  F:\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\longrightarrow\mathbf N,
\]

some member of (4.1) satisfies

\[
  F(1,0)<v_3(\Delta(E_n)).
  \tag{4.3}
\]

This is a genuine quantifier obstruction to bounding local exponent depth
from the coarse `2`-descent profile alone.  It does not assert that the
entire global Selmer group is constant along (4.1).

The same family has nonsplit multiplicative type `I_(4n+4)` at three and
rational Tamagawa number `c_3(E_n)=2`; that independent calculation was
audited in `HIGHER_CONGRUENCE_DEPTH_BARRIER.md`.  Hence even adding the
rational Tamagawa number to (3.5) does not bound the local exponent.

## 5. Why the Cassels--Tate refinement still has no height scale

The Cassels--Tate pairing on the `2`-Selmer group is valuable: it can
separate classes coming from Mordell--Weil points from classes in
`Sha[2]`, and its kernel detects liftability to a higher Selmer group.  It
can therefore improve a rank bound.

Its output is nevertheless an alternating form on a finite `F_2`-space.
Neither the ordinary `2`-Selmer group nor this pairing includes the
Neron--Tate Gram matrix of the free Mordell--Weil lattice.

There is also a strict group-theoretic warning about `Sha`.  The groups

\[
  G_m=(\mathbf Z/2^m\mathbf Z)^2
  \tag{5.1}
\]

carry the standard perfect alternating pairing, have the same
two-torsion dimension two, but have orders `2^(2m)`.  Thus fixed
two-torsion dimension and an alternating pairing do not bound the order of
a finite two-primary group.  This is a logical obstruction to an inference
from first `2`-descent data; it is not a claim that all the groups (5.1)
occur as Tate--Shafarevich groups in the Frey family.

Iterating all `2^k`-descents could in principle determine the full
two-primary part of `Sha`.  It would still leave the odd-primary part, the
regulator, and the leading `L`-value in (1.3).

Primary references for the cohomological descent and the pairing are
[Schaefer's explicit `2`-descent](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1995.1044)
and [Fisher--Schaefer--Stoll's identification of Cassels' pairing with the
Cassels--Tate pairing](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/casselspairing.pdf).

## 6. Canonical height: the visible points are exactly the wrong points

For a canonical height,

\[
  \widehat h([m]P)=m^2\widehat h(P),
\]

and `hat h(P)=0` precisely for torsion points.  Hence

\[
 \widehat h(T_0)=\widehat h(T_a)=\widehat h(T_b)=0.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

On (4.1), however,

\[
  h(x(T_a))=(2n+2)\log3\longrightarrow\infty.
  \tag{6.2}
\]

So no inequality which replaces source height by the maximum canonical
height of the visible `2`-torsion can hold.  The Lean module formalizes the
unbounded integral coordinate in (6.2); it does not introduce a fake
canonical-height definition.

The regulator is the determinant of the canonical-height pairing on
`E(Q)/E(Q)_tors`.  Torsion contributes no row or column.  In rank zero the
regulator convention is `Reg(E)=1`; in positive rank, knowing only the rank
does not bound the determinant.  Abstract rank-one lattices with Gram
matrix `(R)` have fixed rank and arbitrary positive regulator `R`.

One possible conditional repair would use a Lang--Silverman lower bound for
every non-torsion point and then geometry of numbers to lower-bound the
regulator.  This has two decisive limitations here.

1. It says nothing when the Frey curve has rank zero.
2. The required uniform number-field Lang-height statement is itself deep;
   Szpiro/abc implies such height lower bounds.  It cannot be silently used
   as an unconditional Selmer consequence.

See [Hindry--Silverman](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~marc.hindry/MarcJoe.pdf)
for the canonical-height conjecture and its dependence on the Szpiro ratio,
and [Silverman's comparison paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3895) for the
relation between Lang height and Szpiro.

## 7. Local canonical heights see depth, but conserve it

There is a subtler possibility: although a global torsion height is zero,
its individual local heights need not be zero.

At an odd split multiplicative place, write the Tate parameter as `q` and
put

\[
  n=v_p(q)=v_p(\Delta_{\min})=2e.
\]

For the Tate representative `u`, let

\[
  r=\frac{v_p(u)}{v_p(q)}\in[0,1).
\]

The nonarchimedean Tate local-height formula contains

\[
  \frac12 B_2(r)\log|q|_p^{-1},
  \qquad B_2(X)=X^2-X+\frac16.
  \tag{7.1}
\]

Full rational two-torsion consists of the classes represented by
`-1`, `q^(1/2)`, and `-q^(1/2)`.  At odd `p`, all product terms in the Tate
formula have absolute value one, including `|1-(-1)|_p=1`.  Since

\[
  B_2(0)=\frac16,
  \qquad
  B_2\!\left(\frac12\right)=-\frac1{12},
\]

the multiset of local heights of the three nonzero two-torsion points is

\[
 \left\{
   \frac e6\log p,
   -\frac e{12}\log p,
   -\frac e{12}\log p
 \right\}.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

The same multiset is obtained after the unramified quadratic twist in the
nonsplit multiplicative case.  In particular,

\[
  \sum_{T\in E[2]\setminus\{O\}}\lambda_p(T)=0.
  \tag{7.3}
\]

Thus local heights genuinely recover the full weighted depth `e log p`,
but their signs conserve it exactly.  Summing (7.3) gives no positive
global carrier.  Taking positive parts would retain `(e/6)log p`, but
positive part is not additive under the product formula and is not a
canonical height.

For the Frey labeling, the positive point varies with the branch collision:

* at `p | a`, it is the point at `x=-b`;
* at `p | b`, it is the point at `x=a`;
* at `p | c`, it is the point at `x=0`.

Consequently no single globally fixed two-torsion point collects all
positive local contributions.  Its negative finite contributions and its
archimedean local height cancel to the global value zero.

This is a conservation obstruction, not a claim that local heights are
irrelevant.  A genuinely new route would need a non-torsion global point or
a positive adelic functional that preserves the exponent mass without
introducing new bad support.

The local formula is the Tate-curve specialization of the Neron local
height formula; a primary computational reference for the global/local
decomposition is [Silverman, *Computing heights on elliptic curves*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1988-0942161-4).

## 8. The archimedean period bridge

The real roots of the Frey cubic are `-b,0,a`.  For the displayed invariant
differential, one real period is

\[
 \Omega_{\rm disp}
   =\int_a^\infty
      \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-a)(x+b)}}.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Put

\[
  k^2=\frac bc,
  \qquad k'^2=1-k^2=\frac ac.
\]

The standard substitution reducing a cubic-root integral to the complete
elliptic integral gives the exact identity

\[
  \Omega_{\rm disp}
   =\frac{2}{\sqrt c}K(k),
  \qquad
  K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}
     \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\theta}}.
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Two elementary estimates suffice.

First, the denominator in `K(k)` is at most one, so

\[
  K(k)\ge\frac\pi2.
  \tag{8.3}
\]

Second, writing `t=pi/2-theta`, splitting the integral at `pi/3`, and using
`sin t >= 2t/pi` and `cos t >= 1/2` on the first interval gives

\[
  K(k)\le C_0\bigl(1+\log(1/k')\bigr)
  \le C_0\bigl(1+\log c\bigr)
  \tag{8.4}
\]

for an absolute constant `C_0`.  Combining (8.2)--(8.4), and absorbing the
bounded real-component/minimal-differential factor discussed in Section 2,
gives

\[
 \frac12\log c-C_1-\log(1+\log c)
 \le \log(1/\Omega_E)
 \le \frac12\log c+C_2.
 \tag{8.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \log c
 \le 2\log(1/\Omega_E)
      +2\log(1+\log c)+C_3.
 \tag{8.6}
\]

For every fixed `rho>0`, the logarithmic error in (8.6) is at most
`rho log c + C_rho`, so it is absorbable in an abc epsilon-budget.

This period calculation is unconditional.  It is the positive bridge found
by this audit.

## 9. What strong BSD would and would not add

Write `r=rank E(Q)` and

\[
 \Lambda_E=\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}.
\]

The strong BSD leading-term conjecture is

\[
 \Lambda_E
 =\frac{\Omega_E\,\operatorname{Reg}(E)\,|\Sha(E)|
            \prod_p c_p(E)}{|E(\mathbf Q)_{\rm tors}|^2}.
 \tag{9.1}
\]

Set

\[
 Q_E=
 \log\!\left(
   \frac{\operatorname{Reg}(E)\,|\Sha(E)|\prod_p c_p(E)}
        {\Lambda_E\,|E(\mathbf Q)_{\rm tors}|^2}
 \right).
 \tag{9.2}
\]

Taking logarithms in (9.1) gives the exact cancellation

\[
 Q_E=-\log\Omega_E.
 \tag{9.3}
\]

Therefore (8.6) becomes

\[
  \log c\le2Q_E+O(\log(1+\log c)).
  \tag{9.4}
\]

The odd part of the Frey conductor is `rad(abc)` and the two-part is
bounded, so

\[
  \log N_E=\log\operatorname{rad}(abc)+O(1).
  \tag{9.5}
\]

Combining (9.4)--(9.5), the half-slope estimate

\[
  Q_E\le\frac{1+\eta}{2}\log N_E+C_\eta
  \tag{9.6}
\]

implies abc after choosing `eta` slightly smaller than the requested final
epsilon and absorbing the `log log c` term.  The coefficient `1/2` is exact;
the companion Lean theorem checks this scalar transfer.

Conversely, the upper half of (8.5) and abc imply (9.6), again after an
epsilon adjustment.  So (9.6) is essentially an abc-equivalent target on
this Frey family.  It is useful because it identifies exactly which BSD
factors must balance, but it is not a completed proof.

Strong BSD alone asserts the equality (9.1) and finiteness of `Sha`; it does
not provide the upper bound (9.6).  In particular:

* `2`-descent bounds only `Sha[2]`, not `|Sha|`;
* Cassels--Tate at level two does not bound higher primary exponents;
* rank does not bound the regulator;
* convexity-type upper bounds for `Lambda_E`, when (9.1) is solved for the
  regulator, introduce `Omega_E^{-1}` and hence the source height itself;
* GRH locates zeros but does not by itself give the uniform lower bound on
  a nonzero central leading coefficient needed in (9.6).

The original BSD leading-term framework is discussed in
[Cassels, *Arithmetic on curves of genus 1. VIII*](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN243919689_0217/PPN243919689_0217.pdf).

## 10. A non-tautological bridge that remains open

The local calculation in Section 7 suggests one precise use for Selmer
duality which is not merely a rewriting of (9.6).

For every odd bad prime, let `T_p^+` be the unique nonzero rational
two-torsion point in the identity component.  Its local height is the
positive entry in (7.2).  The resulting weighted local selector has mass

\[
  M^+(E)=\frac16\sum_{p\mid abc,\ p\ne2}e_p\log p.
  \tag{10.1}
\]

A genuinely new theorem would globalize a controlled portion of this
selector: construct a non-torsion Mordell--Weil class whose component data
agrees with `T_p^+` on a set carrying a fixed positive proportion of
`M^+(E)`, while bounding the adverse finite and archimedean local heights by
`O_epsilon(log N_E)`.  The local component prescriptions naturally define
an element of a product of local Kummer spaces.  Poitou--Tate duality and
the Cassels--Tate pairing are the correct mechanisms for deciding whether
that element lies in the global Selmer image and then in the
Mordell--Weil image.

Two losses must be confronted explicitly.

1. A Selmer class is only an everywhere locally soluble two-covering; its
   Cassels--Tate obstruction may prevent a rational point.
2. Even a rational class can be torsion.  The visible classes in (2.1)
   demonstrate exact local mass but have global canonical height zero.

Thus the needed statement must include a **non-torsion lift with a
quantitative height comparison**.  Rank or Selmer dimension alone cannot
supply it, and rank-zero Frey curves are an immediate obstruction unless an
auxiliary curve or twist with controlled conductor is introduced.  This
weighted local-to-global alignment problem is the one genuinely Selmer-
theoretic opening left by the audit; it is not proved here and is strictly
stronger than qualitative local solubility.

## 11. Exact status by hypothesis class

### Unconditional in this audit

* the Kummer/Selmer exact sequence and the support-squareclass ambient bound;
* the local valuation-parity collapse;
* the primitive family (4.1) and its unbounded exponent at three;
* zero canonical height of rational torsion;
* the Tate local-height multiset and its conservation at odd multiplicative
  places;
* the elliptic-integral period identity and the corridor (8.5);
* all scalar implications formalized in Lean.

### Conditional on strong BSD

* the identification `Q_E=-log Omega_E` in (9.3), including finiteness of
  `Sha` and equality of analytic and algebraic rank.

### Not supplied by BSD or GRH

* the half-slope upper bound (9.6);
* a uniform regulator upper bound free of the reciprocal period;
* a uniform lower bound for `Lambda_E` strong enough after all arithmetic
  factors are inserted;
* a positive-rank or marked non-torsion point for every Frey curve.

### Essentially abc-equivalent on the Frey locus

* the uniform family of estimates (9.6), after the harmless `log log`
  absorption and the bounded conductor discrepancy at two.

## 12. Lean boundary

`IUTThreeClosures/FreySelmerRegulatorAudit.lean` formalizes only:

1. the support/parity profile and its invariance under adding even depth;
2. the actual primitive even-depth family at three;
3. the fully quantified failure of every profile-only local exponent bound;
4. unbounded integral coordinate scale of the visible point `(a,0)`;
5. the signed scalar local-height ledger and its exact conservation;
6. the scalar full-two-torsion Selmer rank bound;
7. the exact logarithmic BSD cancellation, with BSD as an explicit equality
   hypothesis;
8. the exact half-slope coefficient transfer;
9. a quantifier countermodel showing that nonnegative/finiteness data alone
   does not bound the BSD quotient.

It does not formalize or postulate Selmer groups, local Kummer images,
Cassels--Tate pairings, elliptic local heights, complete elliptic integrals,
minimal models, regulators, `Sha`, `L`-functions, BSD, GRH, Szpiro, or abc.
No theorem declaration has a conjectural estimate hidden as a structure
field.
