\section{The weighted small-order tail}
\label{sec:mersenne-weighted-order-tail}

This section sharpens the third alternative in
Proposition~\ref{prop:mersenne-near-quadratic-tail}.  It also records a
logical limitation of the almost-all order theorem: density in the
prime-size variable does not control logarithmic mass in a moving
exact-order fibre.  None of the results in this section closes the
Mersenne route unconditionally.

For an odd prime $p$, write
\[
 f(p)=\ord_p(2),\qquad
 \alpha_p=v_p(2^{f(p)}-1).
\]
The valuation formula of Murty--S\'eguin and the intrinsic-prime
classification give, for $d>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-tail-exact-ledger}
 \log E_d
 =\sum_{f(p)=d}(\alpha_p-1)\log p
 =\log T_d+\log D_d.
\end{equation}
Here $T_d$ contains one copy of every $p$ with $f(p)=d$ and
$\alpha_p\ge2$, while $D_d$ contains the remaining depth.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-tail-square-budget}
 T_d^2\mid\Phi_d(2),\qquad
 \log T_d<\frac{\varphi(d)+1}{2}\log2.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Global order-index budget]
\label{prop:global-order-index-budget}
For $Y\ge2$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\ \text{odd prime}\\f(p)\le Y}}
       \alpha_p\log p
 \le \sum_{d\le Y}\log\Phi_d(2)=O(Y^2).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \#\{p\text{ odd prime}:f(p)\le Y\}=O(Y^2/\log Y).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $f(p)=d$, the full power $p^{\alpha_p}$ belongs to the exact-order
part of $\Phi_d(2)$.  The order is unique, so contributions belonging to
different $d$ do not overlap.  Sum the logarithms and use
$\Phi_d(2)<2^{\varphi(d)+1}$ from
\cite{PomeranceCyclotomic2025}.  For the counting assertion, split the
primes at $Y^2$; every prime above the split contributes at least
$2\log Y$.
\end{proof}

This is a quadratic cumulative estimate.  Restricting it to a single
order cannot produce the required $o(\varphi(d))$ bound.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted consequence of the almost-all order theorem]
\label{prop:erdos-murty-weighted-consequence}
Fix $\delta>0$ and set
\[
 \mathcal B_\delta
 =\{p:f(p)\le p^{1/(2+\delta)}\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \#\{p\le x:p\in\mathcal B_\delta\}=o(x/\log x),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\in\mathcal B_\delta}}\log p=o(x).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Erd\H{o}s--Murty prove that, for every positive $\eta(p)\to0$, outside
$o(\pi(x))$ primes one has
$f(p)>p^{1/(2+\eta(p))}$ \cite{ErdosMurty1999}.  Take
$\eta(p)<\delta$ eventually.  If $A_\delta(x)$ denotes the exceptional
count, Abel summation gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\in\mathcal B_\delta}}\log p
 =A_\delta(x)\log x-
   \int_2^x\frac{A_\delta(t)}t\,dt=o(x).
\]
\end{proof}

At the beginning of the extreme arm, $x=d^{2+\delta}$, the last estimate
need not be $o(\varphi(d))$.  The weighted Brun--Titchmarsh theorem
\cite[Theorem~2.4]{MurtySeguin2019} gives a little-oh precisely in the
range
\[
 x=o(\varphi(d)^2),\qquad d<x^\theta,\qquad
 \frac{\log x}{\log(x/d)}=O(1)
\]
for a fixed $\theta<1$.  It gives only the natural $O(\varphi(d))$
scale when $x\asymp\varphi(d)^2$.  Thus neither result covers the
extreme range.

In a dyadic shell $(X,2X]$, the available inputs reduce to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-coarse-shell-bounds}
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal W_d\\X<p\le2X}}\log p
 \le\min\left\{
 o(X),
 O\left(\frac{X\log X}{\varphi(d)\log(X/d)}\right),
 O(\varphi(d))
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The final term is \eqref{eq:weighted-tail-square-budget}; at every
relevant scale it is the only generally effective one, and it has no
strict saving.  Across larger $X$, the available theorems furnish no
uniform $o(\varphi(d))$ saving, so the known shell estimates do not create
the missing little-oh when summed.

\begin{proposition}[Coarse information does not imply a fibrewise bound]
\label{prop:weighted-tail-coarse-nogo}
There exist primes $p_j$ and integers $d_j\to\infty$ such that
\[
 p_j\equiv1\pmod {d_j},\qquad
 d_j<p_j^{1/(2+\delta)},\qquad
 \#\{j:p_j\le x\}=O(\log\log x),
\]
for every fixed $\delta>0$, while positive numbers $\phi_j$ satisfy
\[
 \log p_j\asymp\phi_j,\qquad
 2\log p_j<(\phi_j+1)\log2.
\]
Hence global zero density, the congruence forced by exact order, the
small-order scale, and the numerical square budget do not by themselves
imply $o(\phi_j)$ mass in a moving labelled fibre.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $d_j=2^j$, $\phi_j=\varphi(d_j)=2^{j-1}$, fix
$0<c<\log2/2$, and put $X_j=\exp(c\phi_j)$.  Since
$d_j=O(\log X_j)$, the classical Siegel--Walfisz theorem supplies, for
large $j$, a prime
\[
 p_j\in[X_j,2X_j],\qquad p_j\equiv1\pmod {d_j}.
\]
The logarithms grow geometrically, proving the count.  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{p_j\le x}\log p_j
   =O(\log x\log\log x)=o(x),
\]
so even the global logarithmic mass is sparse.  Exponential growth gives
the small-order inequality, and $\log p_j=c\phi_j+O(1)$ gives the stated
linear fibre mass.  Finally,
$\Phi_{2^j}(2)=2^{\phi_j}+1$, and eventually
\[
 2\log p_j<\phi_j\log2<\log\Phi_{d_j}(2),
\]
which proves the asserted numerical square budget.
\end{proof}

In Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-tail-coarse-nogo}, assigning abstract
labels $\widetilde f(p_j)=d_j$ and
$\widetilde\alpha_{p_j}=2$ makes every fibre a singleton of linear
logarithmic mass.  These labels are not asserted to equal the actual
multiplicative order and valuation.  The proposition therefore refutes
only an inference from the four coarse estimates; it is not an
arithmetic counterexample to the tail and does not close the route.

\begin{proposition}[Summable-profile shell localization]
\label{prop:weighted-tail-profile-localization}
Suppose a finite nonnegative mass satisfies
$H\le M\le\sum_{k\in I}M_k$, where $H>0$.  If
$a_k\ge0$ and $\sum_{k\in I}a_k\le1$, then some $k\in I$ satisfies
\[
                              M_k\ge Ha_k.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Otherwise $\sum_kM_k<H\sum_ka_k\le H$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For
\[
 L_d=(2+\delta)\log d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_{d,k}
 =\{p\in\mathcal W_d:2^kL_d<\log p\le2^{k+1}L_d\},
\]
take $a_k=6/(\pi^2(k+1)^2)$.  An extreme mass at least
$\epsilon\varphi(d)/4$ forces, for some $k$,
\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal S_{d,k}}\log p
 \ge\frac{3\epsilon}{2\pi^2(k+1)^2}\varphi(d)
\]
and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-tail-shell-cardinality}
 |\mathcal S_{d,k}|
 \ge
 \frac{3\epsilon\varphi(d)}
 {2\pi^2(k+1)^2\,2^{k+1}(2+\delta)\log d}.
\end{equation}
A summable-envelope upper bound of the reverse form, with a common
coefficient tending to zero, would close the extreme arm.

There is also a useful concentration split.  At any logarithmic height
$h>0$, a mass at least $H$ either has mass at least $H/2$ above $h$, or
contains at least $H/(2h)$ points below $h$.  The square budget bounds the
number of points above $h$ by $O(\varphi(d)/h)$.  Thus a failed tail has
either a macroscopic component of rank $O(\varphi(d)/h)$ or an increasingly
diffuse submacroscopic component.  The first rank is bounded when $h$ is
chosen comparable to $\varphi(d)$.

The pointwise block estimate is stronger than the endpoint actually
requires.  Put
\[
 B_m=\prod_{d\mid m}E_d,
 \qquad
 A(m)=\log B_m=\sum_{d\mid m}\log E_d.
\]
The exact lifting identity and $1\le L_m\le m$ give
\[
 W_m=L_mB_m,
 \qquad
 \log W_m=A(m)+\log L_m,
 \qquad \log L_m=o(m).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact divisor-average endpoint]
\label{thm:mersenne-divisor-average-equivalence}
One has
\[
 \log W_m=o(m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{d\mid m}\log E_d=o(m).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From left to right, subtract the actual LTE term
$\log L_m=o(m)$ from $\log W_m=\log B_m+\log L_m$.  From right to left,
add that same term.  Both steps use the exact product identity.
\end{proof}

The quantifier in both little-oh statements is
$\forall\epsilon>0\,\exists M\,\forall m\ge M$.  The identity holds for
every positive $m$, and $L_m\mid m$ is the LTE lifting input that proves
$0\le\log L_m\le\log m=o(m)$.  Thus both directions include the complete
lifting remainder.  In particular, the reverse implication is not inferred
from a product inequality: it uses $W_m=L_mB_m$ and the negligible lifting
factor.

Let $s_d$ be the support mass below
$Y_d=\varphi(d)^2/\log\log(3d)$, and write
\[
 \log E_d=s_d+r_d,
\]
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-tail-remainder}
 r_d=
 \sum_{\substack{f(p)=d,\ \alpha_p\ge2\\p>Y_d}}\log p
 +\sum_{f(p)=d}(\alpha_p-2)_+\log p.
\end{equation}
The proved small-arm estimate is $s_d=o(\varphi(d))$.

\begin{corollary}[Divisor-average remainder criterion]
\label{cor:mersenne-divisor-average-remainder}
The Mersenne endpoint follows from
\[
 \forall\epsilon>0\ \exists M\ \forall m\ge M,
 \qquad \sum_{d\mid m}r_d\le\epsilon m.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The identity
\[
 \sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)=m
\]
converts the small-arm little-oh into
\[
 \sum_{d\mid m}s_d=o(m).
\]
Adding the displayed hypothesis and applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:mersenne-divisor-average-equivalence} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

This criterion is strictly weaker than $r_d=o(\varphi(d))$.  To see
strictness, select a rapidly increasing divisibility chain of squarefree
primorials $n_1\mid n_2\mid\cdots$ such that
\[
 \sum_{i<k}n_i=o(n_k),\qquad
 \varphi(n_k)/n_k\longrightarrow0,
\]
and define $r_{n_k}=\varphi(n_k)$ and $r_d=0$ otherwise.  Pointwise
$o(\varphi(d))$ fails along $n_k$.  If $n_k$ is the largest selected
primorial dividing $m$, then
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{d\mid m}r_d
 \le
 \frac{\varphi(n_k)+\sum_{i<k}n_i}{n_k}=o(1).
\]
When the largest such $k$ remains bounded, the numerator is fixed and the
ratio again tends to zero.  The example is abstract and asserts nothing
about the actual Mersenne remainders.  The divisor-average criterion opens
a second analytic attack on the tail, through divisor lattices rather than
uniform control of every fibre.

For a fixed $\delta>0$, a pointwise proof still reduces to the three
nonnegative gates
\[
 \sum_{f(p)=d}(\alpha_p-2)_+\log p=o(\varphi(d)),
\]
\[
 \#\{p\in\mathcal W_d:Y_d<p\le d^{2+\delta}\}
       =o(\varphi(d)/\log d),
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal W_d\\p>d^{2+\delta}}}\log p
       =o(\varphi(d)).
\]
The current literature supplies none of these three estimates in the
required uniform form.  The exact divisor-average criterion and the
summable-profile localization are formalized in
\texttt{MersenneWeightedOrderTail20260901}; all analytic inputs remain
external to the Lean kernel.
