\section{Signed endpoint prime-token transport}\label{sec:endpoint-prime-token-transport}

We next attach a finite transport problem to every positive primitive $abc$
triple; unit arms are allowed in this section.  Set
\[
 K_c=\frac{c}{\rad(c)},\qquad
 E_{ab}=\rad(a)\rad(b),\qquad R=\rad(abc).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-core balance]\label{prop:endpoint-core-balance}
Every positive primitive triple satisfies
\[
 E_{ab}=\rad(ab),\qquad R=E_{ab}\rad(c),
 \qquad E_{ab}c=RK_c.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \delta_c(a,b,c):=\log K_c-\log E_{ab}
 =\log c-\log R.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The three coordinates are pairwise coprime, so the squarefree radical is
multiplicative across their supports.  Since $c=\rad(c)K_c$, multiplication
gives $E_{ab}c=RK_c$.  Every factor is positive; taking logarithms and
rearranging proves the last identity.
\end{proof}

For $p\mid c$, write $e_p=v_p(c)$.  The source tokens and their masses are
\[
 \mathcal S_c=\{(p,j):p\mid c,\ 1\le j<e_p\},
 \qquad w(p,j)=\log p.
\]
Thus one source token is retained for each prime layer above the first copy.
The sink tokens and capacities are
\[
 \mathcal T_{ab}=\{q:q\mid ab\},\qquad v(q)=\log q,
\]
with one sink for every distinct external prime.  Canonical factorisation
immediately gives
\[
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_c}w(s)=\log K_c,\qquad
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal T_{ab}}v(q)=\log E_{ab}.
\tag{EPT-1}
\]

\begin{definition}[Fractional monotone endpoint transport]
A transport is a family $F(s,q)\in\R_{\ge0}$ such that
\[
 \sum_qF(s,q)\le w(s),\qquad
 \sum_sF(s,q)\le v(q),
\]
and $F((p,j),q)>0$ only when $p\le q$.  Write
\[
 U(F)=\sum_s\left(w(s)-\sum_qF(s,q)\right),
 \quad
 V(F)=\sum_q\left(v(q)-\sum_sF(s,q)\right)
\]
for unmatched source mass and unused sink capacity.
\end{definition}

The zero transport always exists.  The content of the construction is the
size of $U(F)$, together with the order restriction on its edges.

\begin{theorem}[Exact accounting and height control]
\label{thm:endpoint-transport-accounting}
Every fractional monotone endpoint transport satisfies
\[
 U(F)=\delta_c(a,b,c)+V(F).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \delta_c(a,b,c)\le U(F),
 \qquad \log c\le\log R+U(F).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $C(F)=\sum_{s,q}F(s,q)$.  Finite interchange of summation gives
\[
 U(F)=\sum_sw(s)-C(F),\qquad
 V(F)=\sum_qv(q)-C(F).
\]
Subtracting and using the two token-mass identities gives
$U(F)-V(F)=\delta_c(a,b,c)$.  The column-capacity inequalities make
$V(F)\ge0$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-core-balance} converts the
first resulting inequality into the height bound.
\end{proof}

The monotone edge condition carries information that the scalar identity does
not see.  For $t\in\R$, put
\[
 S_{>t}=\sum_{(p,j)\in\mathcal S_c,\ p>t}\log p,
 \qquad T_{>t}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal T_{ab},\ q>t}\log q.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Weighted Hall tail obstruction]
\label{thm:endpoint-hall-tail}
Every fractional monotone endpoint transport satisfies
\[
 U(F)\ge S_{>t}-T_{>t}
\qquad(t\in\R).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mass emitted by a source over $p>t$ can enter only a sink $q\ge p>t$.
Consequently the total carried mass from that source tail is at most the total
capacity $T_{>t}$ of the sink tail.  Its unmatched mass is therefore at least
$S_{>t}-T_{>t}$, and it is at most the total unmatched mass because every row
deficit is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

We do not use a converse max-flow formula.  The proved tail inequalities are
necessary tests for any proposed transport construction.

\begin{definition}[Uniform endpoint-flow gate]
For every $\eps>0$, require a constant $C_\eps$ such that every positive
primitive triple admits a monotone fractional transport satisfying
\[
 U(F)\le\eps\log R+C_\eps.
\tag{EPF}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[EPF implies standard $abc$]
If the uniform endpoint-flow gate holds, then the standard $abc$ conjecture
holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the flow supplied by EPF,
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-transport-accounting} gives
\[
 \log c\le\log R+U(F)
 \le(1+\eps)\log R+C_\eps.
\]
Exponentiating yields the usual constant-times
$\rad(abc)^{1+\eps}$ inequality, with the same quantifier order.
\end{proof}

The gate EPF is refuted by the infinite prime-square family in
Section~\ref{sec:ordered-prime-transport-obstruction}.  Two smaller
complete-premise examples separately show why an integral replacement is too
rigid.  A \emph{full integral
matching} assigns every source token injectively to a sink token whose prime
is at least the source prime.

\begin{proposition}[Integral matching boundaries]
For $3+13=16$, the source multiset is $\{2,2,2\}$ and the sink set is
$\{3,13\}$, so no full integral matching exists.  For $9+16=25$, the source
has one token over $5$ and the sinks are $\{2,3\}$, so no monotone edge
exists, although
\[
 K_c=5\le6=E_{ab}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is the pigeonhole principle applied to three distinct layer
tokens and two sinks.  In the second example every available sink prime is
strictly below $5$.  The displayed aggregate inequality follows from
$\rad(25)=5$, $\rad(9)=3$, and $\rad(16)=2$.
\end{proof}

These witnesses refute universal full integral matching and the inference
from $K_c\le E_{ab}$ to such a matching.  On their own they do not settle the
fractional epsilon gate; the later infinite family does.

There is also an infinite obstruction to removing the epsilon allowance from
the scalar endpoint-core estimate.

\begin{proposition}[No fixed zero-epsilon multiplier]
Let $X_k=4^{3^k}$.  Then
\[
 3^{k+1}\mid X_k-1,\qquad
 3^k\rad(X_k-1)\le X_k-1,
\]
and the primitive triple $(1,X_k-1,X_k)$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{K_{X_k}}{\rad(1)\rad(X_k-1)}>\frac{3^k}{2}.
\]
Consequently, for every $K\in\N$ there is a positive primitive triple with
$K_c>K\rad(a)\rad(b)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The divisibility is by induction.  It is true at $k=0$ because $X_0-1=3$.
If $3^{k+1}\mid X_k-1$, then $X_k\equiv1\pmod3$ and hence
$3\mid X_k^2+X_k+1$.  The factorisation
\[
 X_{k+1}-1=X_k^3-1=(X_k-1)(X_k^2+X_k+1)
\]
supplies the next power of $3$.  Write $X_k-1=3^{k+1}u$.  Radical
submultiplicativity and $\rad(u)\le u$ give
\[
 \rad(X_k-1)\le3\rad(u)\le3u,
\]
which is the second inequality.  Since $X_k$ is a power of $2$,
$K_{X_k}=X_k/2$; therefore
\[
 \frac{K_{X_k}}{\rad(X_k-1)}
 \ge \frac{(X_k/2)3^k}{X_k-1}>\frac{3^k}{2}.
\]
Choosing, for example, $k=2K+1$ makes $3^k>2K$ and proves the final claim.
\end{proof}

This is an infinite, full-quantifier counterexample to a fixed natural
multiplier at epsilon zero.  This particular family does not refute $abc$ or
the epsilon gate: the displayed certified logarithmic lower bound grows only
linearly in $k$, while $\log X_k=3^k\log4$.  The logically separate
prime-square family in the next section does refute the ordered epsilon gate.
