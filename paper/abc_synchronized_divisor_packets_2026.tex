\section{Synchronized divisor packets and orientation rigidity}
\label{sec:synchronized-divisor-packets}

\noindent\textbf{Author: ChatGPT.}
This section extracts a finite incidence structure directly from the equation
$a+b=c$.  Its points are triples of divisors whose squares collide modulo the
opposite arms.  The construction records exponent caps, the allocation of
primes among the three arms, and local square-root signs.  It is therefore
different from a scalar reweighting of the usual radical.  We prove the
finite algebraic statements first and state the Lean boundary only after the
traditional proofs.  No form of the $abc$ conjecture is assumed or proved.

\subsection{The packet spectrum}

Let
\begin{equation}
 a,b,c\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\qquad a+b=c,\qquad (a,b)=1,
 \qquad a>1,\quad b>1.
 \label{eq:sdp-primitive-data}
\end{equation}
For positive integers $r,s$, write
\[
 \Delta(r,s)=|r^2-s^2|.
\]

\begin{definition}[Synchronized divisor packet]
\label{def:sdp-packet}
A synchronized divisor packet for $(a,b,c)$ is a triple $Q=(x,y,z)$
satisfying
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
 x,y,z>1,\qquad x\mid a,\quad y\mid b,\quad z\mid c,\\
 a\mid\Delta(y,z),\qquad
 b\mid\Delta(x,z),\qquad
 c\mid\Delta(x,y).
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:sdp-packet-conditions}
\end{equation}
The finite set of all packets is denoted by $\mathcal S(a,b,c)$.  A packet
is proper if it differs from $(a,b,c)$.  We attach to a packet the quantities
\begin{align}
 D(Q)&=\Delta(y,z)\Delta(x,z)\Delta(x,y),
 \label{eq:sdp-gap-product}\\
 B(Q)&=\max(y,z)^2\max(x,z)^2\max(x,y)^2,
 \label{eq:sdp-pair-bound}\\
 T(Q)&=\max(x,y,z).
 \label{eq:sdp-height}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The restriction $a,b>1$ removes unit-arm degeneracies from this section.
It is a stated scope restriction and does not remove unit-arm triples from
the $abc$ conjecture.

\begin{proposition}[Nonempty finite spectrum]
\label{prop:sdp-nonempty}
The coordinates of every packet are pairwise coprime and pairwise distinct.
Moreover, $(a,b,c)\in\mathcal S(a,b,c)$, so the spectrum is finite and
nonempty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equation and $(a,b)=1$ give
\[
 (a,c)=(a,a+b)=1,
 \qquad
 (b,c)=(b,a+b)=1.
\]
Common divisors of packet coordinates divide the corresponding pairs of
arms, so $(x,y)=(x,z)=(y,z)=1$.  The coordinates exceed one and are therefore
distinct.

For the full packet, the congruences
\[
 c\equiv b\pmod a,
 \qquad c\equiv a\pmod b,
 \qquad a\equiv-b\pmod c
\]
give the three square congruences in
\eqref{eq:sdp-packet-conditions}.  Finally, each arm has finitely many
divisors.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Product divisibility and height envelope]
\label{thm:sdp-height-envelope}
Every $Q\in\mathcal S(a,b,c)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 abc\mid D(Q),
 \qquad
 abc\le D(Q)\le B(Q)\le T(Q)^6.
 \label{eq:sdp-height-envelope}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The packet congruences give positive integers $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ with
\[
 \Delta(y,z)=a\alpha,
 \qquad
 \Delta(x,z)=b\beta,
 \qquad
 \Delta(x,y)=c\gamma.
\]
Their product proves $abc\mid D(Q)$.  The coordinate gaps are nonzero by
Proposition~\ref{prop:sdp-nonempty}, so a positive multiple of $abc$ is at
least $abc$.  For every pair $r,s$,
\[
 \Delta(r,s)=\max(r,s)^2-\min(r,s)^2\le\max(r,s)^2.
\]
Multiplication yields $D(Q)\le B(Q)$, and each pair maximum is at most
$T(Q)$.
\end{proof}

The pair-max term in \eqref{eq:sdp-height-envelope} is the exact output of
the argument.  Passing immediately to $T^6$ loses anisotropic information.

\subsection{Index one and an infinite exact-gap stratum}

The divisibility in Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp-height-envelope} defines the
positive integer
\begin{equation}
 \kappa(Q)=\frac{D(Q)}{abc}.
 \label{eq:sdp-index}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Index-one gap rigidity]
\label{prop:sdp-index-one}
One has $\kappa(Q)=1$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(y,z)=a,
 \qquad
 \Delta(x,z)=b,
 \qquad
 \Delta(x,y)=c.
 \label{eq:sdp-exact-gaps}
\end{equation}
In that case $z$ lies strictly between $x$ and $y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using the positive quotients $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ from the preceding proof,
we have $\kappa(Q)=\alpha\beta\gamma$.  A product of positive integers is one
exactly when every factor is one.  Under
\eqref{eq:sdp-exact-gaps}, the equation $c=a+b$ becomes
\[
 |x^2-y^2|=|y^2-z^2|+|x^2-z^2|.
\]
Equality in the triangle inequality on the real line places $z^2$ between
$x^2$ and $y^2$.  Positivity then places $z$ between $x$ and $y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Parametric exact-gap packets]
\label{prop:sdp-family}
For every integer $t\ge2$, put
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 a_t&=2t+1,&
 b_t&=t(3t+2),&
 c_t&=(t+1)(3t+1),\\
 x_t&=2t+1,& y_t&=t,& z_t&=t+1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sdp-family}
\end{equation}
Then $(a_t,b_t,c_t)$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:sdp-primitive-data}, and $(x_t,y_t,z_t)$ is a proper packet of
index one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expansion gives
\[
 a_t+b_t=3t^2+4t+1=(t+1)(3t+1)=c_t.
\]
We have $(2t+1,t)=1$, while
\[
 2(3t+2)-3(2t+1)=1;
\]
hence $(a_t,b_t)=1$.  The three coordinate divisibilities in
\eqref{eq:sdp-packet-conditions} are visible in
\eqref{eq:sdp-family}.  Finally,
\begin{align*}
 (t+1)^2-t^2&=2t+1=a_t,\\
 (2t+1)^2-(t+1)^2&=t(3t+2)=b_t,\\
 (2t+1)^2-t^2&=(t+1)(3t+1)=c_t.
\end{align*}
Thus the gaps are exact and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sdp-index-one} applies.  The packet is proper because
$b_t>y_t$ and $c_t>z_t$.
\end{proof}

In this family $a_tb_tc_t\asymp18t^5$ and $T(Q_t)=2t+1\asymp2t$.
Consequently no exponent strictly smaller than five can replace six in a
universal estimate with constant one.  The proposition is a square-difference
identity and overlaps familiar quadratic constructions.  Its role here is to
exhibit an exact stratum of the finite packet spectrum; it is not presented as
a renamed independent discovery.

\subsection{Prime-power orientations and a rigid global sign}

For an odd prime power $p^e\Vert a$, the first square congruence factors as
\[
 p^e\mid(y-z)(y+z).
\]
The prime $p$ cannot divide both factors: otherwise it divides $2y$ and
$2z$, and oddness together with $(y,z)=1$ gives a contradiction.  The whole
prime power therefore occupies exactly one of the channels
\begin{equation}
 p^e\mid y-z
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 p^e\mid y+z.
 \label{eq:sdp-orientation-channel}
\end{equation}
The same statement holds cyclically on the other arms.  Different prime
powers on one composite arm may choose different signs.  At the unique
possible even arm the factors need not be coprime, so the $2$-primary branch
must be retained separately.

\begin{definition}[Canonical orientation]
\label{def:sdp-canonical-orientation}
A packet is canonically oriented when
\begin{equation}
 y\equiv z\pmod a,
 \qquad
 x\equiv z\pmod b,
 \qquad
 x\equiv-y\pmod c.
 \label{eq:sdp-canonical-orientation}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

These are the three whole-arm signs used by the full packet.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical-orientation rigidity]
\label{thm:sdp-canonical-rigidity}
A canonically oriented packet is the full packet $(a,b,c)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The divisor conditions give $x\le a$, $y\le b$, and $z\le c$.  The final
congruence in \eqref{eq:sdp-canonical-orientation} says $c\mid x+y$.
Because $x+y>0$,
\[
 c\le x+y\le a+b=c.
\]
It follows that $x+y=a+b$; the two coordinate inequalities then force
$x=a$ and $y=b$.

We claim that $c<ab$.  Since $a,b>1$, the product
$(a-1)(b-1)$ is positive.  If it were one, then $a=b=2$, contradicting
$(a,b)=1$.  Hence $(a-1)(b-1)\ge2$, which is equivalent to $a+b<ab$.

After substituting $x=a$ and $y=b$, the first two congruences say that $z$
and $c=a+b$ have the same residue modulo both $a$ and $b$.  The coprime
Chinese remainder theorem yields $z\equiv c\pmod{ab}$.  But
$0<z\le c<ab$, so $z=c$.
\end{proof}

Every proper packet therefore has an actual orientation defect.  On odd
prime powers it changes or fragments at least one of the canonical signs;
the possible even arm carries the separate $2$-adic ambiguity.

\subsection{Connection with radical, height, and quality}

Let
\[
 \mathcal R=\operatorname{rad}(abc),
 \qquad
 q_s(a,b,c)=\frac{\log c}{\log \mathcal R}.
\]
The spectrum is finite and nonempty, so one may define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{sync}}(a,b,c)
 =\min_{Q\in\mathcal S(a,b,c)}
 \frac{\log B(Q)}{\log \mathcal R}.
 \label{eq:sdp-energy}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Packet-energy majorant]
\label{cor:sdp-energy-majorant}
For every triple in the present scope,
\[
 q_s(a,b,c)\le\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sync}}(a,b,c).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp-height-envelope} gives $c\le abc\le B(Q)$ for every
packet.  Take logarithms, divide by the positive number $\log \mathcal R$,
and then
take the minimum.
\end{proof}

The original candidate global statement is now visible.  If, for each
$\varepsilon>0$ and all but finitely many primitive nonunit triples, one
could produce a packet with
\begin{equation}
 B(Q)\le \mathcal R^{1+\varepsilon},
 \label{eq:sdp-open-gate}
\end{equation}
then Corollary~\ref{cor:sdp-energy-majorant} would imply the usual
$abc$ inequality.  The infinite primitive family established in the
companion radical-excess note refutes
\eqref{eq:sdp-open-gate} at $\varepsilon=1/3$.  Only this uncompensated gate
is retired.  The corrected live gate inserts the factor $ab$:
\begin{equation}
 B(Q)\le ab\,\mathcal R^{1+\varepsilon}.
 \label{eq:sdp-compensated-open-gate}
\end{equation}
Together with $abc\le B(Q)$, this would imply
$c\le\mathcal R^{1+\varepsilon}$.  No eventual estimate of the compensated
form is established or refuted here.

For comparison, Sankaran's alternative-quality construction
\cite{Sankaran2026AlternativeQuality} assigns to the distinct primes
$p_1,\ldots,p_\omega$ dividing $abc$ the arithmetic and geometric means
\[
 A=\frac1\omega\sum_i\log p_i,
 \qquad
 G=\left(\prod_i\log p_i\right)^{1/\omega},
 \qquad
 \eta=G/A,
\]
and proves the exact factorization
\[
 q_s=\eta q_{\mathrm{DGM}},
 \qquad
 q_{\mathrm{DGM}}=\frac{\log c}{\omega G}.
\]
The packet spectrum is not this scalar factorization under another name.
It depends on exponent caps, arm assignment, simultaneous modular incidence,
and orientation channels.  For example, $(2,3,5)$ and $(3,5,8)$ have the
same radical $30$ and hence the same $\eta$, but the first has one packet
and the second has the additional proper packet $(3,5,2)$.

\subsection{Counterexample and finite-search audit}

The following complete-premise examples eliminate tempting stronger claims:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
$(a,b,c)$ & packet $(x,y,z)$ & eliminated claim\\ \hline
$(3,5,8)$ & $(3,5,2)$ & uniqueness of the full packet\\
$(5,7,12)$ & $(5,7,2)$ & $abc\le T^3$\\
$(5,16,21)$ & $(5,2,3)$ & $abc\le T^4$ and $abc\le(xyz)^2$\\
$(385,527,912)$ & $(7,31,24)$ & $abc\le T^5$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the last two rows the three gaps equal $(a,b,c)$ exactly.  In particular,
\[
 385\cdot527\cdot912=185040240>31^5=28629151.
\]
These counterexamples do not touch the pair-max/sixth-power theorem.

A deterministic integer search scanned all $3{,}795{,}230$ normalized
primitive triples with $c\le5000$.  It enumerated every packet for
$c\le1000$, for the top 200 standard-quality triples through $c=5000$, and
for every triple in that range with $q_s\ge1$.  Across 151,244 enumerated
triples it found 151,711 packets, including 467 proper packets and 105
index-one packets.  Exact assertions checked the three packet congruences,
the product divisibility, and both bounds in
\eqref{eq:sdp-height-envelope}.  The family in
Proposition~\ref{prop:sdp-family} was separately checked for
$2\le t\le100000$.  The selection $q_s\ge1$ used the equivalent exact
integer condition $c\ge\operatorname{rad}(abc)$; floating-point logarithms
were used for ranking and reported ratios.

The twenty highest-quality triples in the finite range had only their full
packet.  This is adverse finite evidence, but a finite range neither proves
eventual packet rigidity nor decides the compensated all-but-finitely-many
assertion.

\subsection{Lean boundary and audit}

The independent Lean companion formalizes the exact finite core: the
primitive data, synchronized divisor packets, inherited coprimality, product
divisibility, pair-max and sixth-power bounds, canonical-orientation rigidity,
and generic channel lemmas.  It also defines the actual squarefree radical,
standard quality, packet energy, and the minimum over the finite nonempty
packet spectrum, and proves the quality majorant in
Corollary~\ref{cor:sdp-energy-majorant}.  Each counterexample in the preceding table is
an explicit Lean datum and packet satisfying all packet premises.  The
deterministic computation and both global radical estimates are absent from
this original formalization; in particular no upper bound on the minimum
packet energy is assumed.  The companion radical-excess module formalizes the
counterfamily and the rational-exponent compensated implication.  A separate
axiom audit lists all dependencies.

Thus the surviving output of this route is a finite structural invariant and
the compensated radical-control problem.  The original uncompensated problem
has a full-premise counterfamily.  Neither result proves or disproves the
$abc$ conjecture.
