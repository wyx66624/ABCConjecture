\section{Shared CRT incidence after exclusive packets}
\label{sec:shared-crt-incidence-successor}

The Linnik counterfamily to exclusive prime packets identifies an arithmetic
loss rather than a shortage of total radical mass.  One prime
$\ell\equiv-1\pmod {M^2}$ certifies all congruences modulo the prime-power
factors of $M^2$ simultaneously.  Its capacity $\log\ell$ must therefore be
shareable, but it may be charged only once.  This section gives a first
capacity-correct shared-incidence successor, proves its height bridge, and
separates it from the scalar relaxation.  Its uniform estimate remains open.

Let $P=(a,b,c)$ be a positive primitive point:
\[
 a,b,c\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad a+b=c,qquad
 \gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,c)=\gcd(c,a)=1.
\]
Put
\[
 \operatorname{core}(n)=\frac{n}{\rad(n)},\qquad
 h(P)=\log c,\qquad r(P)=\log\rad(abc),
\]
and define the endpoint sources and external sinks by
\[
 I=I(P)=\{p:p\mid c,\ v_p(c)\ge2\},\qquad
 J=J(P)=\{q:q\mid ab\}.
\]
For $e_p=v_p(c)$, set
\[
 x_p=(e_p-1)\log p,\qquad y_q=\log q,
\]
and write
\[
 X=\sum_{p\in I}x_p=\log\operatorname{core}(c),\qquad
 Y=\sum_{q\in J}y_q=\log\rad(ab).
\tag{SCRT-1}
\]
For $S\subseteq I$ and $T\subseteq J$, let
\[
 \begin{split}
 m(S)&=\prod_{p\in S}p^{e_p},\\
 a_T&=\prod_{\substack{q\in T\\q\mid a}}q^{v_q(a)},\qquad
 b_T=\prod_{\substack{q\in T\\q\mid b}}q^{v_q(b)},\\
 x(S)&=\sum_{p\in S}x_p,\qquad y(T)=\sum_{q\in T}y_q.
 \end{split}
\tag{SCRT-2}
\]
The empty products are one.  Thus $a_T,b_T$ are unitary divisors carrying
the complete valuation of every selected external prime.

\begin{definition}[Shared CRT block]
A pair $(S,T)$ with $\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I$ and
$\varnothing\ne T\subseteq J$ is \emph{CRT compatible} when
\[
                         m(S)\mid a_T+b_T.                    \tag{SCRT-3}
\]
It is \emph{saturated} when also
\[
                         x(S)\le y(T).                        \tag{SCRT-4}
\]
A saturated compatible pair is a \emph{shared CRT block}.
\end{definition}

Compatibility is arithmetic, rather than merely an incidence convention:
the truncated relation $a_T+b_T$ simultaneously certifies every prime-power
modulus indexed by $S$.  When $I$ and $J$ are nonempty, the full pair is
always compatible because $a_J=a$, $b_J=b$, and $m(I)\mid c$.

\begin{definition}[SCRT-0 configuration]
An SCRT-0 configuration consists of a finite family $\mathcal H$ of
saturated blocks $(S_\nu,T_\nu)$ whose source sets are pairwise disjoint and
whose sink sets are pairwise disjoint.  Put
\[
 I_0=I\setminus\bigcup_\nu S_\nu,
 \qquad
 J_0=J\setminus\bigcup_\nu T_\nu.
\]
The remaining data are an exclusive assignment
\[
                     o:J_0\longrightarrow I_0\sqcup\{\varnothing\}.
\]
Thus a residual sink is unused or belongs to one residual source, while
several sinks may belong to the same source.  For $p\in I_0$, put
\[
 M_p(o)=\sum_{\substack{q\in J_0\\o(q)=p}}y_q,
 \qquad
 R_p(o)=\max\{x_p-M_p(o),0\},
\]
and define
\[
 B(\mathcal H,o)=\sum_{p\in I_0}R_p(o),
 \qquad
 B_{\rm SCRT}(P)=\min_{(\mathcal H,o)}B(\mathcal H,o).
\tag{SCRT-5}
\]
\end{definition}

The minimum exists because the underlying sets are finite; the empty block
family with every sink unused is an admissible configuration.  Sources in a
saturated block have zero boundary.  Choosing a block consumes all of
$y(T_\nu)$, including any surplus over $x(S_\nu)$.

The sink union $T_\nu$ occurs in exactly one block, and its total credit is
at most $y(T_\nu)$.  Every residual sink occurs in at most one exclusive
packet.  Thus no logarithmic capacity is counted twice.

\begin{proposition}[Once-charged height bridge]
\label{prop:scrt-height-bridge}
Every SCRT-0 configuration satisfies
\[
                         X\le Y+B(\mathcal H,o)               \tag{SCRT-6}\label{eq:scrt-mass}
\]
and consequently
\[
                         h(P)\le r(P)+B(\mathcal H,o).        \tag{SCRT-7}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Saturation gives $x(S_\nu)\le y(T_\nu)$ block by block.  At every
remaining source, $x_p\le M_p(o)+R_p(o)$.  Disjoint blocks and exclusive
ownership give
\[
 X\le \sum_\nu y(T_\nu)+\sum_{p\in I_0}M_p(o)
        +B(\mathcal H,o)\le Y+B(\mathcal H,o),
\]
which is (SCRT-6).  Prime factorisation gives
\[
 h(P)-r(P)
 =\log\operatorname{core}(c)-\log\rad(ab)=X-Y,
\]
which proves (SCRT-7).
\end{proof}

Let $B_{\rm PBT}(P)$ denote the optimum of the earlier exclusive packet
model and put $\Delta(P)=\max\{X-Y,0\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Sandwich and easy strata]
\label{prop:scrt-sandwich}
For every positive primitive point,
\[
 \Delta(P)\le B_{\rm SCRT}(P)\le B_{\rm PBT}(P).            \tag{SCRT-8}
\]
Moreover, $X\le Y$ implies $B_{\rm SCRT}(P)=0=\Delta(P)$, and
$|I|\le1$ implies $B_{\rm SCRT}(P)=\Delta(P)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity and (SCRT-6) give the lower bound.  Taking no shared blocks
recovers exactly the PBT assignment problem, giving the upper bound.  If
$I=\varnothing$, then $X=0$ and the empty configuration has zero boundary.
If $I\ne\varnothing$ and $X\le Y$, then $J\ne\varnothing$ and the full pair
$(I,J)$ is a valid saturated block, again giving zero boundary.  Finally, if
$I$ has one member, assign every sink to that source; the resulting residual
is $\max\{X-Y,0\}$ and attains the lower bound.
\end{proof}

Thus an additional shared-incidence gap can occur only when $X>Y$ and at
least two endpoint primes carry positive excess weight.

\begin{definition}[Uniform shared-CRT gate]
SCRT-0 is the statement
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_\varepsilon\ \forall P\
 \exists(\mathcal H,o):\quad
 B(\mathcal H,o)\le\varepsilon r(P)+C_\varepsilon.          \tag{SCRT-0}
\]
Here $P$ ranges over all positive primitive $abc$ points.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional reduction to $abc$]
\label{thm:scrt-implies-abc}
If SCRT-0 holds, then the standard logarithmic $abc$ conjecture holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the configuration supplied by SCRT-0, Proposition~\ref{prop:scrt-height-bridge}
gives
\[
 h(P)\le r(P)+B(\mathcal H,o)
       \le(1+\varepsilon)r(P)+C_\varepsilon.
\]
This has the standard quantifier order.
\end{proof}

No instance of SCRT-0 is assumed or proved here.

Both clauses in the definition are necessary.  If the capacity of one
shared sink union were repeated once per incident source, the point
$(1,224,225)$ would be assigned zero boundary: its two source weights are
$\log3,\log5$ and its external mass is $\log14$, available through the full
relation.  Yet
\[
                         h-r=\log(15/14)>0,
\]
so the height bridge would fail.  At the other extreme, if the full block
may distribute arbitrary partial credits $z_p$ with
$\sum z_p\le Y$, its optimal boundary is
\[
 X-\min(X,Y)=(X-Y)_+.                 \tag{SCRT-9}\label{eq:scrt-scalar-collapse}
\]
The unrestricted model is therefore exactly the scalar defect.  In the
reverse logical implication from $abc$, the additive constant may first be
replaced by its maximum with zero before taking positive parts.

SCRT-0 is strictly different pointwise.  At
\[
                         (343,625,968)=(7^3,5^4,2^3 11^2),
\]
the source weights are $\log4,\log11$ and the sink weights are
$\log5,\log7$.  The joint block is unsaturated because $\log44>\log35$.
The saturated blocks are
\[
 (\{2\},\{7\}),\quad(\{2\},\{5,7\}),\quad
 (\{11\},\{5,7\}),
\]
with resulting best residuals $\log(11/5),\log11,\log4$, respectively.
The empty block family and split assignment $5\mapsto2$, $7\mapsto11$
attain $\log(11/7)$, and the finite list of other assignments is worse.
Thus
\[
 B_{\rm SCRT}(343,625,968)=\log(11/7)
 >\log(44/35)=\Delta.                                       \tag{SCRT-10}\label{eq:scrt-noncollapse}
\]

The recorded infinite counterfamilies do not reproduce their earlier
artificial losses.  Both $(1,p^2-1,p^2)$ and the prime-hypotenuse
Pythagorean-square family have one endpoint source, so
$B_{\rm SCRT}=\Delta$.  The same holds for the fixed-prime family
$(1,p^N-1,p^N)$.  In the Linnik family $(1,\ell,\ell+1)$, the single sink
$\ell$ gives a full shared block: since $\ell$ is odd,
\[
 \operatorname{core}(\ell+1)\le(\ell+1)/2\le\ell,
\]
so that block is saturated and has zero boundary.  For the shifted family
$(2,\ell,\ell+2)$ with $\ell\equiv-2\pmod {M^2}$ and $M$ odd, the full sink
union $\{2,\ell\}$ likewise closes every forced source once; indeed
$\operatorname{core}(\ell+2)\le(\ell+2)/3\le\ell$.  For
$(4^r,3^r,4^r+3^r)$, the full block works whenever
$\operatorname{core}(4^r+3^r)\le6$, and a single repeated endpoint prime again gives
$B_{\rm SCRT}=\Delta$.  The earlier balanced-family obstruction does not
prove an infinite subsequence with several powerful endpoint primes and a
bad SCRT partition, so it does not refute this gate.

A harder child model, SCRT-SAT, deletes the exclusive residual stage and
charges every source outside saturated blocks in full.  It is false.  For
\[
 n=284+310t,
 \qquad P_n=(2,15^n-2,15^n),
\]
direct modular exponentiation gives
$15^{284}\equiv2\pmod {31^2}$ and
$15^{310}\equiv1\pmod {31^2}$.  Hence $31^2\mid15^n-2$ and
\[
 Y=\log\bigl(2\rad(15^n-2)\bigr)
 <\log(2\cdot15^n/31)<(n-1)\log15=X.                        \tag{SCRT-11}\label{eq:scrt-sat-gap}
\]
Disjoint saturated blocks cannot close both endpoint sources, so SCRT-SAT
leaves at least $(n-1)\log3$.  Since its conductor is less than
$n\log15$, every fixed
$0<\varepsilon<\log3/\log15$ refutes its uniform gate.  SCRT-0 retains the
external prime-log partition and therefore is not refuted by this argument;
\eqref{eq:scrt-sat-gap} alone supplies only the constant total-capacity gap
$X-Y>\log(31/30)$.

The saturated rule currently consumes the whole capacity $y(T)$, including
surplus beyond $x(S)$.  This is a design choice.  A surplus-reuse successor
would need a proved fractional-cover or signed-chain identity which keeps
each sink charged once without admitting the unrestricted pooling in
\eqref{eq:scrt-scalar-collapse}.

The Lean module
\texttt{ABCSharedCRTIncidence\allowbreak Successor20260903} formalises the abstract
once-charged certificate and the mass inequality \eqref{eq:scrt-mass}.  It
proves the endpoint height bridge and the conditional implication to
\texttt{ABCConjecture}.  It also checks the duplicated-capacity failure and
the scalar optimum \eqref{eq:scrt-scalar-collapse}.  The module has $30$
public declarations and a one-for-one $30$-query axiom audit whose union is
only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.  The
arithmetic CRT-block configuration, the optimum \eqref{eq:scrt-noncollapse}, and the
SCRT-SAT counterfamily are ordinary proofs at this checkpoint and are not
represented by axioms or opaque gate inhabitants.  Proving or refuting the
uniform SCRT-0 estimate is the next unresolved step.
