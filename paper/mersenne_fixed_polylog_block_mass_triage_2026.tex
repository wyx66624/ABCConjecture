\section{A threshold-free fixed-polylogarithmic Mersenne gate}
\label{sec:mersenne-fixed-polylog-block-mass}

Let $E_d$ be the canonical exact-order excess block and put
$a_d=\log E_d$.  Previous sections prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mersenne-block-cap-recalled}
 0\le a_d\le(\log3)\varphi(d),\qquad
 \log W_m=o(m)\Longleftrightarrow
 A(m):=\sum_{d\mid m}a_d=o(m).
\end{equation}
For $L_m=\log\log(3m)$ and a fixed positive integer $k$, define
\[
 P_k(m)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\\log(m/d)<kL_m}}a_d,
 \qquad
 F_k(m)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\kL_m\le\log(m/d)}}a_d.
\]
The strict and closed inequalities give the exact partition
$A(m)=P_k(m)+F_k(m)$.

\begin{theorem}[Actual fixed-window equivalence]
\label{thm:mersenne-fixed-window-equivalence}
One has
\[
 \log W_m=o(m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{for every fixed }k\ge1,\quad P_k(m)=o(m).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward implication follows from $0\le P_k\le A$.  Conversely, the
exact logarithmic-deficit moment and weighted Markov inequality provide an
absolute $C_0$ such that, eventually and uniformly in $k\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\kL_m\le\log(m/d)}}\varphi(d)
 \le \frac{C_0}{k}m.
\]
By \eqref{eq:mersenne-block-cap-recalled},
$F_k(m)\le(\log3)C_0m/k$.  Given $\eta>0$, first fix $k$ so large that this
upper bound is below $\eta m/2$, and only then use the fixed-$k$ hypothesis
to make $P_k(m)<\eta m/2$.  Hence $A(m)=o(m)$, and
\eqref{eq:mersenne-block-cap-recalled} finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

Inside this window, write the exact-order Wieferich decomposition
\[
 a_d=S_d+T_{d,\delta}+X_{d,\delta}+G_d,
\]
where $S_d$ contains one copy of primes $p\le
Y_d=\varphi(d)^2/\log\log(3d)$, $T_{d,\delta}$ contains one copy for
$Y_d<p\le d^{2+\delta}$, $X_{d,\delta}$ contains one copy for
$p>d^{2+\delta}$, and
\[
 G_d=\sum_{\substack{\ord_p(2)=d\\v_p(2^d-1)\ge3}}
       (v_p(2^d-1)-2)\log p.
\]
The proved bound $S_d=o(\varphi(d))$ and the fact that all window indices tend
to infinity combine with
\[
 \sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)=m
\]
to show that the localized $S$-mass is $o(m)$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fixed-window three-arm obstruction]
\label{thm:mersenne-fixed-window-three-arm}
Fix $\delta>0$.  If $\log W_m\ne o(m)$, then there are fixed $k\ge1$ and
$\epsilon>0$, and an unbounded sequence $m_j$, for which
$P_k(m_j)\ge\epsilon m_j$.  For every fixed $0<\gamma<1/3$, after passage
to a subsequence one same alternative holds eventually:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the localized sum of $G_d$ is at least $\gamma\epsilon m_j$;
\item at least
\[
 \frac{\gamma\epsilon m_j}{(2+\delta)\log m_j}
\]
distinct base-two Wieferich primes lie in the localized transition arm;
\item the localized sum of $X_{d,\delta}$ is at least
$\gamma\epsilon m_j$; each such prime satisfies
$\ord_p(2)<p^{1/(2+\delta)}$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, one may take $\gamma=1/4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The contrapositive of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mersenne-fixed-window-equivalence} fixes $k$ and a linear
lower bound along a subsequence.  After subtracting the localized
$o(m_j)$ small arm, the three remaining nonnegative masses total
$(\epsilon-o(1))m_j$.  Because $3\gamma<1$, one carries at least
$\gamma\epsilon m_j$ for all large $j$; infinite pigeonhole fixes the arm.
In the transition range, $p\le d^{2+\delta}\le m_j^{2+\delta}$, so each
summand is at most $(2+\delta)\log m_j$.  Exact-order prime sets are
disjoint, yielding the cardinality bound.  The extreme-order inequality is
the rearrangement of $p>d^{2+\delta}$.
\end{proof}

The constants are sharp at the stated algebraic level.  The full
quarter-budget mass ledger
\[
 (R,S,G,T,X)=(4,1,1,1,1)
\]
refutes every uniform coefficient greater than $1/4$.  The ledger
$(3,0,1,1,1)$ refutes every coefficient greater than $1/3$ when the small arm
is zero.  These ledgers are not asserted to arise from Mersenne blocks.

Actual finite pressure comes from the sealed exhaustive scan through
$10^7$, independently rechecked by a segmented sieve.  Its only base-two
Wieferich hits are
\[
 (p,\ord_p(2),v_p(2^{\ord_p(2)}-1))=(1093,364,2),
 \quad(3511,1755,2).
\]
They refute the finite strengthenings that every localized block vanishes,
or that every localized repeated prime must already be deep, transition, or
extreme.  Two isolated indices do not refute any little-oh assertion, and
the absence of depth at most $10^7$ has no asymptotic consequence.

For any fixed $\delta>0$, closure now reduces to proving, for every fixed
$k$, that the localized deep mass and extreme mass are $o(m)$ and that the
transition support is $o(m/\log m)$.  No full-premise counterexample to these
three arithmetic gates is known, so all three remain active.
