\section{Multiplier-index compression of the localized Mersenne loss}
\label{sec:mersenne-multiplier-index-two-arm}

We retain the notation of
Section~\ref{sec:mersenne-fixed-polylog-block-mass}.  For an odd prime $p$ put
\[
 d_p=\ord_p(2),\qquad w_p=v_p(2^{d_p}-1),
 \qquad r_p=\frac{p-1}{d_p}.
\]
Lagrange's theorem gives $d_p\mid p-1$, so $r_p$ is a positive integer and
$p=1+d_pr_p$.  In particular, for fixed $d$, the multiplier $r_p$ determines
$p$ injectively inside the exact-order fibre $d_p=d$.  This elementary
observation removes one entire localized mass arm.

Set
\[
 L_m=\log\log(3m),\qquad
 \Lambda_m=\log(3m)L_m^2.
\]
For $d\mid m$, split the one-copy repeated-prime support as
\begin{align*}
 U_d(m)&=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge2\\
                         p\le d^2/\Lambda_m}}\log p,\\
 B_d(m)&=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge2\\
                         p>d^2/\Lambda_m}}\log p,
\end{align*}
and retain the deep excess
\[
 G_d=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d\\w_p\ge3}}(w_p-2)\log p.
\]
Prime by prime, the canonical loss is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier-three-part-exact}
                         a_d=U_d(m)+B_d(m)+G_d.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform low-multiplier estimate]
\label{prop:mersenne-low-multiplier-estimate}
For every fixed positive integer $k$, and all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\\log(m/d)<kL_m}}U_d(m)
 \le \frac{2m\log m}{\Lambda_m}(1+kL_m)=o(m).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $p$ contributes to $U_d(m)$, then
\[
       dr_p=p-1<p\le d^2/\Lambda_m,
       \qquad r_p<d/\Lambda_m.
\]
The positive integral multipliers are distinct, so this fibre has at most
$d/\Lambda_m$ elements.  Since $p\le d^2\le m^2$ eventually,
$U_d(m)\le2d\log m/\Lambda_m$.  Writing $q=m/d$ and enlarging the divisor sum
to all positive integers $q<(\log(3m))^k$ gives
\[
 \sum U_d(m)
 \le \frac{2m\log m}{\Lambda_m}
      \sum_{q<(\log(3m))^k}\frac1q
 \le \frac{2m\log m}{\Lambda_m}(1+kL_m).
\]
After division by $m$, the final expression is at most
$2(1+kL_m)/L_m^2$, which tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-arm near-square-root obstruction]
\label{thm:mersenne-multiplier-two-arm}
Suppose that the Mersenne endpoint $\log W_m=o(m)$ fails.  Then there are a
fixed $k\ge1$, an $\epsilon>0$, and an unbounded sequence $m_j$ such that,
for every fixed $0<\gamma<1/2$, after passage to a subsequence one same
alternative holds for all large $j$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the localized deep mass satisfies
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m_j\\\log(m_j/d)<kL_{m_j}}}G_d
 \ge\gamma\epsilon m_j;
\]
\item the localized $B$-mass satisfies
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m_j\\\log(m_j/d)<kL_{m_j}}}B_d(m_j)
 \ge\gamma\epsilon m_j,
\]
and every prime in this sum obeys
\[
              \ord_p(2)<\sqrt{p\Lambda_{m_j}}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-window equivalence supplies $k,\epsilon$ and $m_j$ for which the
localized sum of $a_d$ is at least $\epsilon m_j$.  Sum
\eqref{eq:multiplier-three-part-exact} and apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:mersenne-low-multiplier-estimate}.  The two unresolved
arms then total at least $(\epsilon-o(1))m_j$.  If both were smaller than
$\gamma\epsilon m_j$, their sum would be smaller than
$2\gamma\epsilon m_j$, contradicting $2\gamma<1$ for large $j$.  Infinite
pigeonhole fixes an arm on a subsequence.  Finally,
$p>d^2/\Lambda_{m_j}$ implies $d<\sqrt{p\Lambda_{m_j}}$.
\end{proof}

Thus this route closes if, for every fixed $k$, both localized sums of $G_d$
and $B_d(m)$ are $o(m)$.  The coefficient $1/2$ is sharp at the exact
nonnegative-ledger level: $(R,U,G,B)=(2,0,1,1)$ satisfies every displayed
mass premise and defeats each coefficient greater than $1/2$.  This tuple is
an abstract full-premise counterexample to the stronger ledger conclusion;
it is not a Mersenne block.

The exact-order lower bounds of Erd\H{o}s--Murty~\cite{ErdosMurty1999} are global
and unweighted, and leave a genuine $p^{1/2-o(1)}$ to $p^{1/2+o(1)}$ band.
The valuation dictionary of Murty--S\'eguin~\cite{MurtySeguin2019} and the
cyclotomic description in Pomerance~\cite{PomeranceCyclotomic2025} justify
the block language, but do not give the localized Wieferich-weighted estimate
needed here.  The conditional alternatives in
Fellini--Murty~\cite{FelliniMurty2026} likewise do not supply either remaining
unconditional little-oh statement.  Hence both arms remain active.

The formal companion proves the integral multiplier representation and
injectivity, the finite cardinality and logarithmic-mass bounds, the exact
two-arm subtraction, its fixed-window finite-sum form, and the sharp ledger
counterexample.  The harmonic asymptotic and the realization of the three
mass functions remain explicit paper mathematics; no distribution or
squarefreeness hypothesis is installed as a Lean axiom.
