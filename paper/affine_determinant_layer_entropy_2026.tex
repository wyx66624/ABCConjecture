\section{Determinant layers and canonical-kernel entropy}
\label{sec:affine-determinant-layer-entropy}

We refine the fixed-template estimate of
Section~\ref{sec:affine-template-entropy} by using all three affine
congruences simultaneously.  Let $S\subseteq[1,M]^2$, put $N=M-1$, and
suppose every difference vector $(x,y)$ in one fixed template satisfies
\[
 d_U\mid x,\qquad d_V\mid x+Cy,\qquad d_W\mid x+By,
\]
where $d_U,d_V,d_W$ are pairwise coprime.  Set $D=d_Ud_Vd_W$.

\begin{proposition}[Three-form determinant divisor]
\label{prop:three-form-determinant-divisor}
For any two template difference vectors $v=(x,y)$ and $w=(x',y')$,
\[
                         D\mid\det(v,w).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The modulus $d_U$ divides $xy'-yx'$.  The identities
\[
 \det(v,w)=(x+Cy)y'-y(x'+Cy')
           =(x+By)y'-y(x'+By')
\]
give divisibility by $d_V$ and $d_W$.  Pairwise coprimality multiplies the
three divisibilities.
\end{proof}

Choose a shortest nonzero difference vector $v$ and put
$\delta=\lVert v\rVert_\infty$.  The determinant map
$p\mapsto\det(v,p-p_0)$ has values in $D\mathbb Z$, range length at most
$N(|v_1|+|v_2|)$, and level sets parallel to $v$.  Projection to a dominant
coordinate gives the following capacity.

\begin{theorem}[Determinant-layer capacity]
\label{thm:determinant-layer-capacity}
If $|S|\ge2$, then
\[
 |S|\le
 \left(\left\lfloor\frac N\delta\right\rfloor+1\right)
 \left(\left\lfloor
   \frac{N(|v_1|+|v_2|)}D\right\rfloor+1\right)
 \le\frac{4N^2}{D}+\frac N\delta+1.
\]
If $D>2N^2$, all points of $S$ are collinear.  If the complete three-form
separation theorem also yields $\delta>L$, then
\[
 |S|\le\left\lfloor\frac{M-1}{L+1}\right\rfloor+1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An interval of the displayed determinant range contains at most the second
number of multiples of $D$; a level contains at most the first number of
points.  Expanding and using $\delta\le N$ gives the coarse inequality.
When $D>2N^2$, every determinant has absolute value below $D$ and hence is
zero.  The final estimate is one-dimensional interval packing.
\end{proof}

Return to a positive primitive seed $a+b=c$, oriented by $b\ge c/2$, with
$R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)<c$ and
\[
 M=\left\lfloor\frac{c^6}{4R}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad T_0=\frac{Rc^{14}}{8192}.
\]
The cases $c=6,7,8$ are incompatible with $R<c$, so $c\ge9$; also $R\ge6$.
Consequently $T_0>2(M-1)^2$.  Every complete certificate with repeated-loss
product above $T_0$ is therefore in the zero determinant layer.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical one-dimensional capacities]
\label{thm:canonical-one-dimensional-capacities}
At the fixed scale $L_{12}=\lfloor c^4/12\rfloor$ every canonical template
satisfies
\[
                         |S|<\frac{31c^2}{10R}.
\]
More generally, if $s\ge1$ and $s^3\le R$, then at
$L_s=\lfloor sc^4/22\rfloor$ one has
\[
                         |S|<\frac{57c^2}{10Rs}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cubic separation inequality at scale $12$ follows from
\[
 8192\cdot100<81\cdot12^3\cdot6,
\]
and the weakest long-arm cap from $8192<12\cdot6\cdot9^3$.
At scale $s/22$, use $s^3\le R$ and
$8192\cdot100<81\cdot22^3$, together with
$8192<22\cdot9^3$.  One-dimensional packing gives respectively
$|S|<3c^2/R+1$ and $|S|<11c^2/(2Rs)+1$; the inequalities
$10R<c^2$ and $5Rs<c^2$ absorb the final terms.
\end{proof}

For $n>0$ define its repeated kernel by
\[
 K(n)=\prod_{p^e\parallel n,\ e\ge2}p^e.
\]
Primewise comparison proves
\[
 K(n)\mid n,\qquad
 \frac{K(n)}{\operatorname{rad}K(n)}=
 \frac n{\operatorname{rad}n},
\]
and $K(n)$ divides every divisor of $n$ preserving this exact loss.  Thus
partitioning exceptional affine points by
$(K(U),K(V),K(W))$ is the unique coarsest exact-lossless template partition.

\begin{corollary}[Forced canonical-kernel entropy]
\label{cor:forced-canonical-kernel-entropy}
Let $\kappa,\eta>0$.  If the exceptional packet has size at least
$\kappa R^{-2/3}c^{4+\eta}$ and $N_{\rm ker}$ canonical-kernel triples are
realized, then, for any integer $s\ge1$ with $s^3\le R$,
\[
 N_{\rm ker}>
 \max\left\{\frac{10\kappa}{31}R^{1/3}c^{2+\eta},
             \frac{10\kappa}{57}sR^{1/3}c^{2+\eta}\right\}.
\]
Taking $s=\lfloor R^{1/3}\rfloor$ yields
\[
 N_{\rm ker}>\frac{5\kappa}{57}R^{2/3}c^{2+\eta}.
\]
Every realized triple additionally satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rad}\bigl(K(U)K(V)K(W)\bigr)
 <\frac{8192c^6}{R}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The canonical-kernel triples partition the exceptional packet into disjoint
fixed-template classes.  Each class retains the complete loss threshold
$\Delta>T_0$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-one-dimensional-capacities}
applies to it at both scales.  Summing the two strict class bounds gives
\[
 |\mathcal E|<N_{\rm ker}\frac{31c^2}{10R},
 \qquad
 |\mathcal E|<N_{\rm ker}\frac{57c^2}{10Rs}.
\]
Combining these inequalities with the assumed lower bound and dividing by
the positive capacities gives the displayed maximum.

For $s=\lfloor R^{1/3}\rfloor$, the standing bound $R\ge6$ implies
$s\ge1$, $s^3\le R$, and $s\ge R^{1/3}/2$.  Substitution in the second
term of the maximum yields
$N_{\rm ker}>(5\kappa/57)R^{2/3}c^{2+\eta}$.

Finally put $P=K(U)K(V)K(W)$.  The repeated-kernel identities, together
with the pairwise coprimality of the primitive arms, give
$P/\operatorname{rad}P=\Delta>T_0$.  Also $K(n)\mid n$ and the canonical
arm bounds give $P\le UVW<c^{20}$.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rad}P=\frac{P}{P/\operatorname{rad}P}
 <\frac{c^{20}}{T_0}=\frac{8192c^6}{R},
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

The positivity of $\kappa$ is essential to this exact formulation.  If it is
omitted, $\kappa=0$, an empty exceptional packet, and $N_{\rm ker}=0$ satisfy
the remaining premises but contradict the strict conclusion.  This
full-premise counterexample retires only the zero-density strengthening.

For a fractional cover in which each template meets $\mathcal E$ in at most
$K_s$ points and each point receives total nonnegative weight at least one,
the finite inequality $|\mathcal E|\le K_s\sum_\tau w_\tau$ handles
correlated overlaps exactly.
It does not produce the required adaptive weight bound.  Two complete
integer witnesses mark precise boundaries: one certified template has
$4M^2<D$, disproving an area-only estimate, while two adjacent points carry
different complete certificates, disproving the assertion that a union of
pointwise selected templates inherits fixed-template separation.  The latter
witness lies outside the canonical box.  Neither counterexample removes the
canonical adaptive-kernel route; the unresolved task is to produce or exclude
the required $R^{2/3}c^{2+\eta}$ family under the actual exception inequality.
