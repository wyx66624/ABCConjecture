\section{Totient-weighted concentration at the Mersenne endpoint}
\label{sec:mersenne-totient-concentration}

The exact order-block identity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mersenne-divisor-average-equivalence} changes the remaining
Mersenne problem from a pointwise estimate into a divisor-average problem.
This section identifies the natural probability measure on that divisor
lattice and localizes the unresolved arithmetic mass to divisors extremely
close to the ambient index.

For an integer $m\ge1$ and a nonnegative sequence $a_d$ put
\[
 A(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}a_d,
 \qquad
 \mu_m(d)=\frac{\varphi(d)}m\quad(d\mid m).
\]
The identity $\sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)=m$ makes $\mu_m$ a probability
measure.  Assume throughout the boundedness condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totient-uniform-cap}
             0\le a_d\le C\varphi(d)
\end{equation}
with one constant $C\ge0$, and define
\[
 B_\epsilon(m)=
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\a_d>\epsilon\varphi(d)}}\varphi(d).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact totient exceptional-mass criterion]
\label{thm:totient-exceptional-mass-criterion}
Under \eqref{eq:totient-uniform-cap},
\[
 A(m)=o(m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_\epsilon(m)=o(m)\quad\hbox{for every fixed }\epsilon>0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every positive $\epsilon$ one has the finite inequalities
\[
 \epsilon B_\epsilon(m)\le A(m)
 \le \epsilon m+C B_\epsilon(m).
\]
The first implication follows from the lower inequality.  For the reverse
implication, given $\eta>0$, apply the hypothesis with
$\epsilon=\eta/2$ and require
$B_\epsilon(m)\le\eta m/(2(C+1))$.
\end{proof}

The divisor probability has an exact logarithmic moment.  If
$m=\prod_{p^a\parallel m}p^a$ and $D$ is distributed according to $\mu_m$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totient-log-deficit-moment}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_m}\log\frac mD
 =\sum_{p^a\parallel m}
   \frac{1-p^{-a}}{p-1}\log p.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the exponent $J_p=v_p(D)$ has probability
$\varphi(p^j)/p^a$, and the tail-sum identity gives
$\mathbb E(a-J_p)=\sum_{r=1}^ap^{-r}$.

\begin{theorem}[Moving near-diagonal concentration]
\label{thm:totient-moving-concentration}
There is an absolute $C_0$ such that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mu_m}\log\frac mD
 \le C_0\log\log(3m).
\]
Consequently, if $H(m)>0$ eventually and
$H(m)/\log\log(3m)\to\infty$, then
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\
        d\le m\exp(-H(m))}}\varphi(d)\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bound \eqref{eq:totient-log-deficit-moment} by
$\sum_{p\mid m}\log p/(p-1)$.  Enlarge the absolute constant to absorb the
finitely many small $m$, and assume $z=\log m>1$.  Split at $z$.
Chebyshev's estimate $\vartheta(x)=O(x)$ bounds every dyadic shell below
$z$ by an absolute constant, giving $O(\log z)$ in total.  Above $z$ the
sum is at most $\log\operatorname{rad}(m)/(z-1)=O(1)$.  Markov's inequality
at height $H(m)$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The growth condition on $H$ cannot uniformly be replaced by
$H=C\log\log m$ with fixed $C$.  For the primorial
$m_y=\prod_{p\le y}p$, the complementary divisor $m_y/D$ contains each
$p\le y$ independently with probability $1/p$.  Choose an integer $r>C+1$
and disjoint intervals
$0<u_1<v_1<\cdots<u_r<v_r<1$ with $\sum_i u_i>C+1$.
For each $i$, let $A_i(y)$ be the event that $m_y/D$ contains a prime in
$(y^{u_i},y^{v_i}]$.  Mertens's prime-product theorem \cite{Mertens1874}
gives
\[
 \Pr(A_i(y)^c)=
 \prod_{y^{u_i}<p\le y^{v_i}}(1-1/p)\longrightarrow u_i/v_i.
\]
The events are independent because their intervals are disjoint, hence
$\liminf_y\Pr(\bigcap_iA_i(y))=\prod_i(1-u_i/v_i)>0$.
On this intersection $m_y/D>y^{C+1}$; Chebyshev's two-sided bounds for
$\vartheta(y)=\log m_y$ imply
$y^{C+1}>(\log m_y)^C$ eventually.  Therefore, for every fixed $C>0$,
\[
 \liminf_{y\to\infty}\mu_{m_y}
 \left\{D\le\frac{m_y}{(\log m_y)^C}\right\}>0.
\]
This is a full-premise counterexample to the proposed uniform strengthening
for arbitrary bounded masses (take $a_d=\varphi(d)$).  It is not a
counterexample to the actual Mersenne blocks.

For those blocks let $a_d=\log E_d$.  If a prime $p$ occurs in $E_d$, then
$\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=d$, so $d\mid p-1$ and $p\nmid d$.  In the identity
$\prod_{e\mid d}\Phi_e(2)=2^d-1$, a divisibility
$p\mid\Phi_e(2)$ with $e\mid d$ would, since $p\nmid e$, make the reduction
of $2$ a primitive $e$th root; hence $e=d$.  Thus the full $p$-adic
multiplicity of $2^d-1$ is carried by $\Phi_d(2)$.  The exponent used in
$E_d$ is one smaller, and the distinct prime-power factors are pairwise
coprime, proving $E_d\mid\Phi_d(2)$.  The root product then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mersenne-cyclotomic-uniform-cap}
       0\le\log E_d\le\varphi(d)\log3.
\end{equation}
The relevant valuation identities and current distribution results are
consistent with \cite{MurtySeguin2019,PomeranceCyclotomic2025,
FelliniMurty2026}; none supplies the following exceptional-weight estimate.
The primitive-divisor refinement \cite{GranvillePrimitive2012} and the
recent generalized-Wieferich congruence criteria
\cite{FalkHarringtonJones2026} likewise control local phenomena rather than
the required moving totient mass.

\begin{corollary}[Localized exact Mersenne gate]
\label{cor:localized-exact-mersenne-gate}
Fix $\delta>0$.  Then
\[
 \log W_m=o(m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d>m^{1-\delta}\\
       \log E_d>\epsilon\varphi(d)}}\varphi(d)=o(m)
 \quad\text{for every }\epsilon>0.
\]
The same statement remains valid when the near-top range is narrowed to
\[
 d>m\exp\bigl(-\{\log\log(3m)\}^2\bigr)=m^{1-o(1)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:totient-exceptional-mass-criterion} using
\eqref{eq:mersenne-cyclotomic-uniform-cap}.  Theorem
\ref{thm:totient-moving-concentration} makes the complementary divisor
range negligible.  Finally use the exact equivalence
$\log W_m=o(m)\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{d\mid m}\log E_d=o(m)$.
\end{proof}

The endpoint still forces $a_m=o(m)$, so pointwise control remains necessary
on primes and fixed prime powers.  What is gained is permission for large
normalized blocks on orders with small $\varphi(d)/d$, provided their
near-diagonal totient mass is negligible.  No checked source, including the
recent powerful-number count \cite{BrowningVerzobio2026}, proves this final
gate, and no full-premise counterexample to it is known.

The Lean companion checks the exact moment, its dyadic
$O(\log\log(3m))$ estimate, the general moving-threshold Markov theorem and
its squared-loglog specialization.  It also proves the exact-order
prime-power divisibility in $\Phi_d(2)$ and hence
\eqref{eq:mersenne-cyclotomic-uniform-cap}.  Combining these results gives a
kernel-checked equivalence between the Mersenne endpoint and the remaining
near-diagonal exceptional-totient estimate; that estimate itself is neither
assumed nor proved.
