\section{Signed trace projectors for a prime-index Pell squarefull gate}
\label{sec:pell-signed-trace-projector}

\noindent\textbf{Author: ChatGPT.}

\paragraph{Summary.}
Let $(1+\sqrt 2)^n=A_n+B_n\sqrt 2$.  We give an exact signed-trace
encoding of the still open assertion that $A_\ell B_\ell$ is not
squarefull at an odd prime index.  At every odd index,
\[
 A_{2n}-1=2A_n^2,\qquad A_{2n}+1=4B_n^2.
\]
Thus squared support in either coordinate is equivalent to fourth-power
support in the corresponding adjacent trace factor.  We also identify a
canonical Chinese-remainder projector
$E_n=(A_{2n}+1)/2=2B_n^2$ whose exact idempotent defect is
$2(A_nB_n)^2$, and show that one explicit Newton correction raises its
precision from $(A_nB_n)^2$ to $(A_nB_n)^4$.

An exhaustive computation over odd prime $\ell\leq 800000$ and support
prime $q\leq 2000000$, independently replayed by matrix powering, finds
exactly two depth-two support collisions, one in each channel.  Both give
signed-trace depth exactly four and therefore disprove several tempting
strengthenings.  The index-seven row is nonsquarefull because its opposite
coordinate is the exponent-one prime $239$; the global squarefull status of
the large row is unresolved.  Neither row is a counterexample to the $abc$
conjecture.  All algebraic claims and the small counterexample are kernel
checked in Lean.

\subsection{The Pell coordinates and the unresolved gate}

Define nonnegative integers $A_n,B_n$ by
\[
 (1+\sqrt2)^n=A_n+B_n\sqrt2.
\]
Equivalently, $(A_0,B_0)=(1,0)$ and
\[
 (A_{n+1},B_{n+1})=(A_n+2B_n,A_n+B_n).
\]
The norm identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell-signed-norm}
 A_n^2-2B_n^2=(-1)^n.
\end{equation}
For an integer $N>0$, \emph{squarefull} means that
$p\mid N$ implies $p^2\mid N$ for every prime $p$.

The present work begins with the following fixed-parameter result from the
companion transversality analysis.

\begin{proposition}[fixed-parameter gate]\label{prop:pell-signed-input}
Let $\ell$ be an odd prime.  Every support prime of $A_\ell$ and
$B_\ell$ is transverse at the Pell polynomial parameter $T=2$.
Furthermore, all first Hensel displacements vanish if and only if
$A_\ell B_\ell$ is squarefull.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:pell-signed-input} is recalled as an input, rather than silently
being folded into a computation.  Classical rank-of-apparition theory for
Lucas sequences supplies the necessary candidate classes
$q\equiv\pm1\pmod{2\ell}$ used below; primitive-divisor results such as
\cite{BHV2001} provide the broader historical setting.  The exclusion of a
squarefull product at every odd prime index remains open.

\subsection{Depth doubling in adjacent signed trace factors}

\begin{lemma}[addition law]\label{lem:pell-signed-addition}
For all $m,n\geq0$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_{m+n}&=A_mA_n+2B_mB_n,\\
 B_{m+n}&=A_mB_n+B_mA_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiply $(A_m+B_m\sqrt2)(A_n+B_n\sqrt2)$ and compare the
rational and $\sqrt2$ coefficients.  This is also an induction using the
defining recurrence.
\end{proof}

Put $Z_n=A_{2n}$.  Taking $m=n$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:pell-signed-addition} gives
\[
 Z_n=A_n^2+2B_n^2,\qquad B_{2n}=2A_nB_n.
\]

\begin{theorem}[signed trace-square identities]\label{thm:pell-signed-trace}
If $n$ is odd, then
\[
 Z_n-1=2A_n^2,\qquad Z_n+1=4B_n^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For odd $n$, equation~\eqref{eq:pell-signed-norm} gives
$2B_n^2=A_n^2+1$.  Substitute this identity into
$Z_n=A_n^2+2B_n^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[exact depth dictionary]\label{thm:pell-signed-depth}
Let $n$ be odd.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $q$ is an odd prime dividing $A_n$, then
\[
 q^4\mid Z_n-1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q^2\mid A_n.
\]
\item If $q$ is an odd prime dividing $B_n$, then
\[
 q^4\mid Z_n+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q^2\mid B_n.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, coordinate depth exactly two gives signed-trace depth exactly four.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Theorem~\ref{thm:pell-signed-trace}.  The factors $2$ and $4$ are coprime
to an odd prime.  Unique factorization gives both fourth-power equivalences;
the same comparison at powers two, three, four, and five gives the exact
depth-two-to-four assertion.
\end{proof}

For odd integers $A,B$ and a trace value $Z$ satisfying the two identities
of Theorem~\ref{thm:pell-signed-trace}, define
\[
\operatorname{ST}_{4}(A,B,Z)
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\begin{cases}
 q^4\mid Z-1&\text{for every prime }q\mid A,\\
 q^4\mid Z+1&\text{for every prime }q\mid B.
\end{cases}
\]

\begin{corollary}[exact signed-packet gate]\label{cor:pell-signed-gate}
For every odd prime $\ell$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\text{all fixed-$T=2$ first Hensel displacements vanish}\\
 &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\
 A_\ell B_\ell\text{ is squarefull}\\
 &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\
 \operatorname{ST}_{4}(A_\ell,B_\ell,A_{2\ell}).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first equivalence is Proposition~\ref{prop:pell-signed-input}.  The coordinates are
coprime, so their product is squarefull exactly when both coordinates are
squarefull.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:pell-signed-depth} to every support prime.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[radical compression]\label{cor:pell-signed-radical}
If both coordinates at an odd index are squarefull, then
\[
 \rad(A_n)^4\mid Z_n-1,\qquad
 \rad(B_n)^4\mid Z_n+1,
\]
and
\[
 \rad(A_nB_n)^4
 \mid (Z_n-1)(Z_n+1)=8(A_nB_n)^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Squarefullness gives $\rad(A_n)^2\mid A_n$ and
$\rad(B_n)^2\mid B_n$.  Square these divisibilities and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:pell-signed-trace}.  Coprimality combines the radicals, while direct
multiplication gives the displayed product identity.
\end{proof}

The last divisibility is compatible with squarefullness and is not a
contradiction.  Its content is the placement of the two complete support
packets in adjacent factors of one trace coordinate.

\subsection{The exact projector and its Newton continuation}

Fix an odd index and abbreviate $A=A_n$, $B=B_n$, $U=AB$, and $Z=Z_n$.
Define
\[
 E=\frac{Z+1}{2}=2B^2=A^2+1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[channel projector]\label{thm:pell-signed-projector}
The integer $E$ satisfies
\[
 E\equiv1\pmod{A^2},\qquad E\equiv0\pmod{B^2},
\]
and has exact defect
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell-signed-defect}
 E^2-E=2U^2.
\end{equation}
Thus $E$ is idempotent modulo $U^2$.  If $U\geq3$, it is not idempotent
modulo $U^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The congruences follow from the two expressions for $E$.  Their product
gives
\[
 E(E-1)=2B^2A^2=2U^2.
\]
If $U^3$ divided the last expression, then $U$ would divide $2$, which is
impossible for $U\geq3$.
\end{proof}

The sharpness of the raw projector does not terminate the construction.
For integers $e,d$, set
\[
 \mathcal N(e,d)=e-(2e-1)d.
\]

\begin{theorem}[one Newton step]\label{thm:pell-signed-newton}
If $e^2-e=d$, then
\[
 \mathcal N(e,d)^2-\mathcal N(e,d)=d^2(4d-3).
\]
For $d=2U^2$, the corrected projector is congruent to $e$ modulo $U^2$
and is idempotent modulo $U^4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $z=2e-1$.  Since $z^2=4d+1$, expansion yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (e-zd)^2-(e-zd)
 &=d-z^2d+z^2d^2\\
 &=d^2(4d-3).
\end{aligned}
\]
The correction $zd$ is divisible by $U^2$, and the new defect contains
$d^2=4U^4$.
\end{proof}

$q\equiv\pm1\pmod{2\ell}$.  It computes $(A_\ell,B_\ell)$ modulo $q^2$,

powering in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$.  A verifier independently powers
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&1\end{pmatrix}^{\ell}.

A separate certificate checks primality by complete trial division and

\begin{corollary}[refuted strengthenings]\label{cor:pell-signed-refuted}
Each of the following universal statements is false:
\begin{enumerate}
\item no support prime repeats at an odd prime index;
\item repetition is confined to the $A$ channel;
\item repetition is confined to the $B$ channel;
\item the minimal representative $q=2\ell\pm1$ forces simple support;
\item coordinate depth two always promotes to signed-trace depth five.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two rows of Table~\ref{tab:pell-signed-hits} meet every premise of the relevant
claims.  The first supplies the $B$-channel counterexample, the second the
$A$-channel counterexample, and both use the minimal residue
representative.  Exact trace depth four refutes the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[logical boundary]
The two rows are local collisions.  Neither row says that both coordinates
at its index are squarefull.  Consequently neither refutes the
signed-packet exclusion in Corollary~\ref{cor:pell-signed-gate}, and neither is an
$abc$ counterexample.  Bounded absence of a simultaneous row is recorded
only as a finite observation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Remaining problem}

The prime-index squarefull gate is now the existence problem for two
fourth-power support packets in the adjacent factors
$A_{2\ell}-1$ and $A_{2\ell}+1$, together with their exact product and one
explicit Newton correction step.  The depth-two collisions prove
that neither a single channel nor one additional trace digit can close the
problem.  A successful continuation must couple all support primes across
both adjacent factors, or construct a genuine simultaneous packet.  Since
no complete-premise counterexample to that global exclusion is presently
known, the route remains open.
