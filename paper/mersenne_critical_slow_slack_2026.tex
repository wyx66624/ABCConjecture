\section{Critical slow-slack compression in the Mersenne route}
\label{sec:mersenne-critical-slow-slack}

The balanced multiplier argument of
Section~\ref{sec:mersenne-balanced-multiplier-depth} can be pushed below every
fixed positive power of $\log\log m$.  For an odd prime $p$, retain
\[
 d_p=\ord_p(2),\qquad w_p=v_p(2^{d_p}-1),\qquad
 r_p=\frac{p-1}{d_p},\qquad L_m=\log\log(3m).
\]
Call a positive function $\sigma$ \emph{slow-admissible} when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slow-admissible}
 \sigma(m)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \frac{L_m\sigma(m)}{\log(3m)}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-slow-scales}
 F_{m,\sigma}=\log(3m)L_m\sigma(m),
 \qquad
 H_{m,\sigma}=\left\lfloor
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(3m)}{L_m\sigma(m)}}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
The latter tends to infinity.  For $d\mid m$, split the repeated one-copy
mass and the deep excess as
\begin{align*}
 U_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 &=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge2\\p\le d^2/F_{m,\sigma}}}\log p,&
 B_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 &=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge2\\p>d^2/F_{m,\sigma}}}\log p,\\
 V_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 &=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge3\\r_p<H_{m,\sigma}}}
       (w_p-2)\log p,&
 G_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 &=\sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge3\\r_p\ge H_{m,\sigma}}}
       (w_p-2)\log p.
\end{align*}
These four nonnegative terms still sum exactly to the block mass $a_d$.

\begin{theorem}[Critical slow-slack removal]
\label{thm:mersenne-critical-slow-removal}
Let $\sigma$ satisfy \eqref{eq:slow-admissible}.  For every fixed positive
integer $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slow-two-negligible-arms}
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\\log(m/d)<kL_m}}U_d^{(\sigma)}(m)=o(m),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\\log(m/d)<kL_m}}V_d^{(\sigma)}(m)=o(m).
\end{equation}
If also $\sigma(m)=o(L_m^\eta)$ for every fixed $\eta>0$, both surviving
cutoff regions are eventually contained in the corresponding fixed-$\eta$
regions of Section~\ref{sec:mersenne-balanced-multiplier-depth}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $q=m/d$ and $Q_m=(\log(3m))^k$.  In the displayed divisor window,
$q<Q_m$.  If $p$ occurs in $U_d^{(\sigma)}$, then
\[
 0<r_p=\frac{p-1}{d}<\frac p d\le\frac d{F_{m,\sigma}}.
\]
The integral multipliers are distinct on a fixed exact-order fibre.  Hence
there are at most $d/F_{m,\sigma}$ such primes, each at most $d^2\le m^2$,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slow-one-copy-bound}
 \sum U_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 \le \frac{2m\log m}{F_{m,\sigma}}
       \sum_{q<Q_m}\frac1q
 \le \frac{2m\log m}{F_{m,\sigma}}(1+kL_m)=o(m).
\end{equation}
The last step uses only $\sigma(m)\to\infty$.

For the deep term, Yamada's pointwise theorem \cite{Yamada2010} and
exact-order LTE give, for $d>1$ and $H\ge2$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d_p=d,\ w_p\ge3\\r_p<H}}(w_p-2)\log p
 \le \frac{C_Yd}{\log d}\frac{H(H-1)}2
       +2(H-1)\log(1+dH),
 \qquad C_Y=283\log3\log6.
\]
Summing with $H=H_{m,\sigma}$ and $d>m/Q_m$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum V_d^{(\sigma)}(m)
 \le{}&\frac{C_YmH(H-1)}{2\log(m/Q_m)}(1+kL_m)\\
      &+2HQ_m\log(1+mH).
\end{align*}
Since $H^2\le\log(3m)/(L_m\sigma(m))$, the first line divided by $m$ is
$O_k(\sigma(m)^{-1})$; the second is polylogarithmic and hence $o(m)$.

Finally,
\[
 \frac{F_{m,\sigma}}{F_{m,\eta}}
   =\frac{\sigma(m)}{L_m^\eta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{\log(3m)/(L_m\sigma(m))}}
      {\sqrt{\log(3m)/L_m^{1+\eta}}}
   =\sqrt{\frac{L_m^\eta}{\sigma(m)}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the near-square survivor threshold rises and the high-multiplier
threshold rises in the required directions.  Taking floors preserves the
second strict inequality eventually because both raw scales diverge.
\end{proof}

The explicit choice
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-explicit-sigma}
                         \sigma_*(m)=\log(3+L_m)
\end{equation}
satisfies every hypothesis.  It replaces every fixed $L_m^\eta$ loss by one
further iterated logarithm.  The cutoff regions are strictly nested as real
intervals; for a particular finite $m$ the actual prime sets may coincide if
the removed band happens to contain no prime.

\begin{theorem}[Critical two-arm alternative]
\label{thm:mersenne-critical-two-arm}
If the Mersenne endpoint
\[
        \log\frac{2^m-1}{\rad(2^m-1)}=o(m)
\]
fails, then for any slow-admissible $\sigma$ there are fixed $k\ge1$,
$\epsilon>0$, and an unbounded sequence $m_j$ such that, after passage to a
subsequence, one of the following carries at least
$\gamma\epsilon m_j$ for every fixed $0<\gamma<1/2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the deep excess with $r_p\ge H_{m_j,\sigma}$;
\item the repeated one-copy mass with
\[
 d_p<\sqrt{p\log(3m_j)L_{m_j}\sigma(m_j)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The same alternative occurs for all sufficiently large indices of the chosen
subsequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-window equivalence supplies $k,\epsilon$ and a localized total
block mass at least $\epsilon m_j$.  Subtract the two $o(m_j)$ terms in
\eqref{eq:slow-two-negligible-arms}.  The $G$ and $B$ masses then total
$(\epsilon-o(1))m_j$.  If both were below $\gamma\epsilon m_j$, their sum
would be below $2\gamma\epsilon m_j$, a contradiction for large $j$.
Infinite pigeonhole fixes one arm on a subsequence.  The order bound is the
rearrangement $p>d_p^2/F_{m_j,\sigma}$.
\end{proof}

Exact order also gives a useful filter that is independent of the asymptotic
argument.

\begin{proposition}[Euler character of the exact-order multiplier]
\label{prop:mersenne-euler-multiplier-character}
For an odd prime $p$, if $d=\ord_p(2)$ and $p-1=dr$, then
\[
                         \left(\frac2p\right)=(-1)^r.
\]
The allowed residue classes modulo eight are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
d\bmod8&r\bmod8\\ \hline
0&0,2,4,6\\
1&0,6\\
2&0,1,4,5\\
3&0,2\\
4&0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\\
5&0,6\\
6&0,3,4,7\\
7&0,2
\end{array}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $d$ is odd, the even number $p-1=dr$ forces $r$ even, and Euler's
half-power is one.  If $d$ is even, exactness implies
$2^{d/2}\not\equiv1\pmod p$, while its square is one; hence it is $-1$ and
$2^{(p-1)/2}\equiv(-1)^r$.  Euler's criterion gives the identity.  Combining
it with the supplementary law for $(2/p)$ and $p\equiv1+dr\pmod8$ yields the
table by the $64$ residue checks.
\end{proof}

The exact arithmetic row
\[
 p=1093,\qquad d_p=364,\qquad w_p=2,\qquad r_p=3
\]
has $1093^2\mid2^{364}-1$ and $1093^3\nmid2^{364}-1$.  It is therefore a
full-premise counterexample to the proposed assertion that every repeated
base-two exact-order prime has even multiplier.  That assertion is retired.
It lies on the unrestricted $d\equiv4\pmod8$ row and does not refute the
character theorem or either slow-slack estimate.  A deterministic scan of all
primes through $10^5$ recovers only the known depth-two rows $1093$ and $3511$;
this finite no-hit statement has no asymptotic consequence.

There is a separate abstract endpoint boundary.  Labels
$r=1,\ldots,H-1$ with weights $x_r=r$ satisfy positivity, injectivity, the
cutoff $r<H$, and the pointwise affine envelope, but have total
$H(H-1)/2$.  With co-divisors $q\le Q$, weights $x_{q,r}=r/q$ attain one half
of $H(H-1)\sum_{q\le Q}1/q$.  This refutes the inference of a subquadratic
energy bound from those abstract premises alone.  It is not an arithmetic
prime packet, so the critical arithmetic target $\sigma\equiv1$ remains
active.  Current order and Wieferich literature, including the exact
valuation dictionary of Murty--S\'eguin \cite{MurtySeguin2019}, supplies no
localized weighted estimate that closes it.

The Lean companion checks support monotonicity, nonnegative survivor-mass
monotonicity, the exact cutoff balance, the finite two-arm ledger, the Euler
half-power identity on actual exact-order fibres, and the full $1093$
certificate.  The real asymptotic estimates and Yamada's theorem remain
paper mathematics with explicit citations; no density statement or $abc$
consequence is introduced as a Lean axiom.
