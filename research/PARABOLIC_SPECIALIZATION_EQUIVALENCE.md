# Parabolic specialization target and geometric abc

For a primitive positive specialization

\[
 \lambda=a/c,\qquad a+b=c,
\]

the ordinary height and the truncated boundary counting function on
`P^1\setminus{0,1,infinity}` are

\[
 h(\lambda)=\log c,
 \qquad
 N_D^{(1)}(\lambda)=\log\operatorname{rad}(abc).
\]

For the Legendre parabolic Hodge line `L`, maximal Kodaira--Spencer gives
`deg L=1/2`; hence for every fixed `ell`, under any uniformly equivalent adelic
metric,

\[
 h_{L^{\ell-1}}(\lambda)
 =\frac{\ell-1}{2}h(\lambda)+O_\ell(1).
\]

Therefore a pointwise estimate

\[
 h_{L^{\ell-1}}(\lambda)
 \le
 \left(\frac{\ell-1}{2}+\eta_\ell\right)
 N_D^{(1)}(\lambda)+O(1)
\]

is equivalent, after division by `(ell-1)/2`, to

\[
 h(\lambda)
 \le
 \left(1+\frac{2\eta_\ell}{\ell-1}\right)
 N_D^{(1)}(\lambda)+O(1).
\]

Thus the exact factor-six identity is a correct coefficient explanation, but
the remaining sharp parabolic maximal-slope theorem is itself a geometric abc
inequality unless an independent determinant/metric mechanism proves it.
Any future proof must be audited so that the truncated boundary-height estimate
is not reintroduced as a hypothesis.
