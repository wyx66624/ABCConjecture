## 1. Two canonical integer layers

Let

\[
n=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{e_p},\qquad n>0.
\]

Define the exponent-one layer

\[
A_1(n)=\prod_{e_p=1}p
\]

and the above-two layer

\[
U_2(n)=\prod_{e_p\ge3}p^{e_p-2}.
\]

For each prime exponent separately,

\[
e_p+\mathbf 1_{e_p=1}
=
2+\max(e_p-2,0).
\]

Multiplying primewise yields the exact integer identity

\[
\boxed{
nA_1(n)=\operatorname{rad}(n)^2U_2(n).
}
\]

The identity has no denominator and is valid for every nonzero natural number.

## 2. Logarithmic signed defect

All four factors are positive, so taking logarithms gives

\[
\boxed{
\log n-2\log\operatorname{rad}(n)
=
\log U_2(n)-\log A_1(n).
}
\]

This is the concrete integer form of the abstract v17 weighted identity.
It shows exactly why an unsigned cubeful quotient is too strong: the
exponent-one layer belongs on the opposite side of the estimate.

Exponent two disappears identically. A prime cube contributes one copy of
`log p`; a prime fourth power contributes two copies; and an exponent-one
prime cancels one copy.

## 3. Consequence for an abc violation

For a primitive positive abc point, write

\[
m=\min(a,b),\qquad M=\max(a,b),
\qquad R=\log\operatorname{rad}(abc).
\]

The v17 localization proves that if

\[
\log c>(1+\varepsilon)R+C,
\]

then at least one `x` in `{M,c}` satisfies

\[
\log x-2\log\operatorname{rad}(x)
>
\log\operatorname{rad}(m)
+\varepsilon R+C-\frac{\log2}{2}.
\]

Using the integer layer identity, the conclusion becomes

\[
\boxed{
\log U_2(x)-\log A_1(x)
>
\log\operatorname{rad}(m)
+\varepsilon R+C-\frac{\log2}{2}.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
U_2(x)
>
A_1(x)\operatorname{rad}(m)
\exp\!\left(
\varepsilon R+C-\frac{\log2}{2}
\right).
\]

Thus a counterexample cannot be generated by merely inserting a fixed cube or
by making one endpoint highly powerful while the neighbouring endpoint has a
large exponent-one radical. It must create a conductor-scale imbalance on one
specific large endpoint after all exponent-one cancellation is charged.

## 4. Remaining arithmetic problem

The positive route is now concrete:

> for coprime short-gap endpoints arising from `a+b=c`, prove that neither
> endpoint can have `U_2/A_1` larger than the small-endpoint radical times an
> arbitrary fixed positive power of the full radical, apart from a uniform
> constant.

The counterexample route is equally concrete: construct an unbounded family
where that ratio has a fixed positive conductor slope and where the other
endpoint does not supply compensating exponent-one radical mass.

Known results on powerful and squarefull numbers address special cases in
which `A_1=1` or has restricted form. They do not currently provide the
uniform signed estimate above; the distinction is essential.
