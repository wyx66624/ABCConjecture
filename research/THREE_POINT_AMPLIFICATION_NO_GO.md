# Riemann--Hurwitz obstruction to exact three-point amplification

Let `f:P^1->P^1` be a rational map of degree `d` over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero, and put

\[
 T_f=f^{-1}(\{0,1,\infty\}).
\]

For each of the three fibers, the ramification contribution is `d-r_y`, where
`r_y` is the number of distinct points in the fiber.  Riemann--Hurwitz gives

\[
 3d-|T_f|\le2d-2,
\]

hence

\[
 |T_f|\ge d+2.
\]

Therefore a map satisfying

\[
 f^{-1}(\{0,1,\infty\})\subseteq\{0,1,\infty\}
\]

has degree one and is one of the six anharmonic permutations.  Every degree
`d>1` map introduces at least `d-1` new boundary points outside the original
three, and the `n`-th iterate has at least `d^n+2` boundary points.

Thus no nontrivial fixed rational self-map can amplify abc triples while
preserving exactly the old prime-support divisors.  The broader amplification
route survives only if it controls the smoothness or norms of the necessarily
new boundary factors.
