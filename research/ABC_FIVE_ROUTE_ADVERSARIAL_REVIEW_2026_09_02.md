## 4. Mersenne Farey denominator entropy

### 4.1 Finite inequality and strictness

For each prefix denominator `q<=T`, a row is determined by its multiplier
`1<=r<H`, so its total contribution is at most

\[
 q^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{H-1}r=\frac{H(H-1)}{2q}.
\]

For `q>T`, every row has the strict pointwise bound `r/q<H/T`.  Replacing
that strict inequality by the non-strict summed upper bound in (4.2) is
valid.  There is no division by zero because the theorem assumes `T>=1`.

Depth transport also has the correct direction.  From
`p-1=dr` and `p^3 | 2^d-1`, one gets

\[
 2^d-1\mid2^{dr}-1=2^{p-1}-1.
\]

The fixed-index row-to-prime map is injective because the prime determines
its exact order `d`, after which `q=m/d` and `r=(p-1)/d` are determined.

### 4.2 Swarm theorem and critical exponent

Set `eta=min(epsilon/4,k/2)` and `T=floor(A^eta)`.  For sufficiently large
`m`, `1<=T<Q`, and

\[
 \frac{H(H-1)}2\mathcal H_T
 \le \frac{A}{2L}(\eta L+O(1))
 \le \frac{\varepsilon A}{4}.
\]

Consequently a lower bound `E>=epsilon A` forces

\[
 (H/T)N_{>T}\ge 3\varepsilon A/4.
\]

Since `T` is comparable to `A^eta` and `A/H>=sqrt(AL)`, the exponent
`1/2+eta` and the factor `L^(1/2)` in the row lower bound are correct.  The
short-window inequalities use both strict hypotheses `q<Q` and `q>T`, so
the rows lie in the displayed `Delta_3` interval.  The limsup contradiction
then follows because `log log(1+mH)=log A+o(1)`.

No known theorem is silently inserted here: the global bound with critical
exponent `1/2` is explicitly an open hypothesis.  Even if supplied, it only
closes the low-multiplier depth-three arm and not the stable packet needed
for abc.

## 6. Refined tensor Haar volume and same pilot

### 6.1 Local field and Haar normalization

For every finite extension `L/Q_p`, the ring of integers is a DVR.  For a
uniformizer `pi`,

\[
 p=u\pi^e,
 \qquad |k_L|=p^f,
 \qquad [L:\mathbb Q_p]=ef,
\]

with `u` a unit.  These are the correct ramification, residue-degree, and
defectless-degree identities.

Under additive Haar measure normalized by `mu(O_L)=1`, multiplication by
`p` has modulus `p^(-ef)`.  Therefore the inverse image under multiplication
by `p` multiplies measure by `p^(ef)`.  On a finite product of primitive
factors the raw logarithmic shift is

\[
 \left(\sum_d e_df_d\right)\log p=N_c\log p.
\]

Dividing by total degree `N_c`, rather than factor count, gives exactly one
`log p`.  Relative factor weights `n_d/N_c` then reproduce the same
normalization.  All denominators are positive because every local degree is
positive and every tuple has at least one factor.

The two normalization counterexamples meet their mathematical premises.  A
quadratic field as a one-factor etale algebra has factor count one and degree
two, so factor-count normalization leaves `2 log p`.  For an unramified
quadratic Galois extension,

\[
 K\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}K\simeq K\times K,
\]

and copying parent weight one to both factors produces total weight two.
These refute only the two proposed weighting rules.

### 6.2 Openness, finiteness, and pointedness

An arbitrary union of homeomorphic images of an open seed is open and hence
Borel measurable.  The identity branch supplies nonemptiness and the
pointed inclusion of the seed.  If the union lies in an envelope with compact
closure, its closure is compact and its Haar measure is finite; nonempty
openness makes the measure positive.  For an infinite orbit this proves

## 7. Steinberg valuation contact surface

### 7.1 Exterior algebra and standard Bloch boundary

For finite prime divisors,

\[
 \Omega(A,B,C)=(A-C)\wedge(B-C)
               =A\wedge B+B\wedge C+C\wedge A.
\]

Translation invariance, quadratic scaling, and the sign action of `S_3`
follow directly.  Pairwise disjoint supports of a primitive abc triple make
the three rectangular coefficient blocks disjoint, so the weighted
coefficient norm is

\[
 \Phi=h_ah_b+h_bh_c+h_ch_a.
\]

The five-term formula has the standard signs and arguments.  With

\[
 z_4=\frac{y(1-x)}{x(1-y)},\qquad
 z_5=\frac{1-x}{1-y},
\]

direct divisor expansion cancels every wedge coefficient.

The terminology paragraph correctly warns that this is not a nonzero
Milnor-K2 Steinberg symbol: `{z,1-z}=0` after imposing the Steinberg
relation.  The most precise description is

\[
 \Omega(z)=(\wedge^2d)\bigl(\delta([z])\bigr),
 \qquad \delta([z])=z\wedge(1-z),
\]

the divisor image of the standard Bloch boundary before passage to Milnor
K2.  Thus the exterior element and its five-term cancellation are standard
functorial algebra.  The proposed weighted contact cost and calibrated
filling problem are the potentially new part.  This wording avoids claiming
a new K2 class, and the revised checkpoint now uses it.  Some conventions
insert a factor of two in the boundary map; this normalization choice does
not affect five-term cancellation.  See, for example, Neumann's definition
of the pre-Bloch and Bloch groups in
[arXiv:math/0307092](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0307092).

### 7.2 Full-premise infinite refutation of MC

For every integer `t>=1`, let

\[
 x=2t+1,\qquad y=2t(t+1),\qquad z=2t^2+2t+1.
\]

Then `x^2+y^2=z^2`.  Moreover `x` is odd and is coprime to both `t` and
`t+1`, so `gcd(x,y)=1`.  The Pythagorean identity then implies pairwise
coprimality of `x,y,z`, and hence of their squares.  Thus

\[
 (a,b,c)=(x^2,y^2,z^2)
\]

is a positive primitive abc point for every `t>=1`.

For a square leg,

\[
 r(u^2)=\log\operatorname{rad}(u)\le\log u
       =\tfrac12 h(u^2).
\]

Applying this bound to the two polarized terms for each unordered leg pair
gives

\[
 \mathcal M\le\Phi.
\]

Also `Phi>=h_b H`, and

\[
 h_b=2\log(2t(t+1))\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

If Gate MC held, choose the permitted fixed value `epsilon=1/2`.  For its
single constant `C_(1/2)`, every member of the family would satisfy

\[
 \Phi\le\frac34\mathcal M+C_{1/2}H
      \le\frac34\Phi+C_{1/2}H,
\]

or `Phi/H<=4C_(1/2)`, contradicting the preceding unbounded lower bound.
The same one epsilon and one constant are used across the whole family, so
the universal and existential quantifiers are genuinely contradicted.

The Lean theorem `not_uniformSingleCellMixedAreaGate` formalizes this full
argument, including positivity, the Pythagorean identity, pairwise
coprimality, radical half-bounds, and unboundedness.

### 7.4 The surviving five-term route

The exponent identity

\[
 h-r=(g-1)u_h+(u_h-r)
\]

is correct when `g` is the gcd of the positive prime exponents and
`n=u^g`.  Both terms are nonnegative.  On a square, the coherent term is at
least half the total height, which is enough to account for the failed
single-cell comparison.  The phrase “the obstruction lies in V” should not
be read as saying that the residual term vanishes.  For example, the
`t=2` family member has the leg `144=12^2`; its primitive-base residual is
`log 12-log 6=log 2>0`.  A precise sentence is that the coherent Veronese
part alone already supplies the obstruction, so the examples do not by
themselves refute a separate residual estimate.

Gate VF received the needed definitional repair during this audit.  In the
broader rational convention, write each `z=m/n` in lowest terms with `n>0`
and `m(n-m) != 0`, and use the primitive signed triple

\[
 (A_z,B_z,C_z)=(m,n-m,n).
\]

The three absolute legs are pairwise coprime because `gcd(m,n)=1`; signs do
not affect finite prime divisors.  Heights and radicals use their absolute
values.  The conventions `g(1)=1` and `h=r=u_h=0` on a unit leg make every
defect and cost total.

More conservatively, Gate VF is restricted to chains generated by elementary
moves with `0<y<x<1`.  This restriction is closed under the five displayed
arguments: `y/x` lies in `(0,1)`,

\[
 0<\frac{y(1-x)}{x(1-y)}<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y<x,
\]

and `0<(1-x)/(1-y)<1`.  Hence every cell has a unique reduced positive
primitive triple `(m,n-m,n)`, and the starting cell `a/c` belongs to this
domain.  A finite chain obtained by adding the permitted five-term relations
defines the paper-level equivalence relation.  These changes resolve S-B1;
they do not assert the existence of a chain satisfying the two analytic
bounds.

With this repair, the former third VF inequality is automatic.  Five-term
equivalence gives

\[
 \Omega(a/c)=\sum_j n_j\Omega(z_j).
\]

The weighted `l^1` triangle inequality and the exact cell split give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi(a,b,c)
 &=\|\Omega(a/c)\|_{1,\log}\\
 &\le\sum_j|n_j|\,\|\Omega(z_j)\|_{1,\log}\\
 &=\sum_j|n_j|\Phi_j
  =\tfrac12\bigl(\mathcal Q(\Gamma)+\mathcal R(\Gamma)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Indeed `Q_j=M_j+V_j` and `2Phi_j-M_j=V_j+R_j`, so
`Q_j+R_j=2Phi_j`.  Proposition 8.1 now records this theorem, and Lean's
`finiteFilling_boundary_le_calibratedCost` proves the finite-coordinate
