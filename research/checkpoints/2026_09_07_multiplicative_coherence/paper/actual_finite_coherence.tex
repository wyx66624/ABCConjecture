\section{Actual pair phases and a finite root-count bridge in Lean}
\label{afc-section}

The integer rigidity step in multiplicative coherence admits a finite
formalization that retains the actual pairs. For integers $a,b$ put
\[
 r(a,b)=ab-1,\qquad s(a,b)=a+b+1,
 \qquad K(u,v)=u^2+uv+v^2.
\]
Then $(a+\zeta)(b+\zeta)=r(a,b)+s(a,b)\zeta$. For two such pairs,
the conjugate cross difference has coordinates $(D,-2D)$, where
\[
 D=r(a,b)s(c,d)-r(c,d)s(a,b).
\]
These are identities in the actual integer-coordinate multiplication
of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$.

\begin{lemma}[An actual divisor fiber]\label{afc-divisor}
Let $u,v$ be positive integers and let $T$ be a finite set of ordered
positive integer pairs $(a,b)$ satisfying $v(ab-1)=u(a+b+1)$.
Then
\[
 |T|\le\tau(K(u,v)).
\]
There is no coprimality requirement on $u,v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expansion gives $(va-u)(vb-u)=K(u,v)$. Also
$b(va-u)=u(a+1)+v>0$, and similarly $a(vb-u)>0$.
Thus both factors are positive. The map $(a,b)\mapsto va-u$
lands in the actual positive divisors of $K(u,v)$. Its value determines
$a$ since $v>0$, then determines $vb-u=K(u,v)/(va-u)$ and hence $b$.
It is injective, including on diagonal and exchanged pairs.
\end{proof}

For a natural number $N$, define the finite envelope
\[
 \mathcal D(N)=\max_{0\le k\le N}
       |\operatorname{divisors}(k)|.
\]
The formal definition is a finite supremum over
\texttt{Finset.range}$(N+1)$, with the library's empty divisor set at zero.
Only positive $K$ occurs below. No asymptotic divisor estimate is part
of this definition.

\begin{proposition}[Finite counting with an explicit image interface]
\label{afc-count}
Let $n$ be a natural number, and let $B,m$ be integers with $B>0$
and $m>864B^3$. Let $S$ be a finite
set of integers in $[3B,6B)$, and let $H$ be a finite set with a binary
operation. Suppose $\phi:S\to H$, $|H|\le3n$, and for every
$a,b,c,d\in S$,
\[
 \phi(a)\phi(b)=\phi(c)\phi(d)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\mid D.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{afc-square}
 |S|^2\le3n\,\mathcal D(2500B^4).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On this block the positive coordinates satisfy $r\le36B^2$ and
$s\le12B$. Thus $|D|\le864B^3<m$, and the assumed divisibility forces
$D=0$. Partition $S^2$ by the product image in $H$. In every nonempty
fiber choose an actual representative $(a,b)$ and take
$u=r(a,b)$, $v=s(a,b)$, without reducing their fraction.
All pairs in the fiber have this phase. Lemma~\ref{afc-divisor} applies,
and
\[
 0<K(u,v)\le2500B^4.
\]
The last bound follows from the coordinate bounds and $B\ge1$:
$u^2+uv+v^2\le1296B^4+432B^3+144B^2\le2500B^4$.
Every fiber therefore has size at most the displayed envelope, including
empty fibers. Summing over all $H$ proves~\eqref{afc-square}.
\end{proof}

For natural numbers $n,q$ with $n>0$ and $q>6n^3$ one has
\[
 q^4>1296n^{12}>864(n^4)^3.
\]
Consequently, when $B=n^4$ and the explicit divisibility modulus is $q^4$,
the same finite theorem gives
\[
 |S|^2\le3n\,\mathcal D(2500n^{16}),\qquad
 |S|\le\sqrt{3n}\sqrt{\mathcal D(2500n^{16})}.
\]
The square-root conclusion is a statement in the real numbers, obtained
by casting the natural-number inequality and using monotonicity of
the real square root. The finite theorem itself does not require
primality or any group law beyond the specified binary operation.
The ordinary number-theoretic application supplies a group and the
two explicit interface hypotheses; it is not built into the formal
statement by an extra axiom.
