\begin{abstract}
We regroup actual Eisenstein prime factors according to common exponent blocks.
This changes the number of logarithms from the prime support size to the number
of chosen blocks, and gives uniform archimedean and local multiplicity bounds
without fixing their prime sizes or count. A single growing exponent content
already controls the angular loss and the entire small-prime part; a bounded
number of blocks also handles global content one. Explicit compensated high-prime conditions yield restricted abc bounds, not standard abc. Independently, a
Linnik prime-neighbour construction gives arbitrarily deep large-prime excess
with negative abc defect, disproving an unrestricted raw-excess smallness
claim. We distinguish ordinary proofs, cited analytic inputs, finite checks,
and the narrower Lean companion throughout.
\end{abstract}
