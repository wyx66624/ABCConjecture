# Sixteenth continuation: ordinary and formal boundaries

The ordinary packet JP1--JP3 and geometric QS1--QS4 arguments received root
and two peer reviews. QH1--QH3 subsequently received both peer reviews of
the actual ground-field Picard, rank, good-reduction and finite-map hypotheses.
The new actual phase bridge was ordinarily proved first and reviewed by
both peers before its Lean implementation. These are internal independent
reviews, not external journal peer review.

The formal modules have the following intended scope; successful verification
is determined by verification/mathlib_validation.json for the exact source
hashes, not by this prose description.

* JointPhasePackets: ten theorems. Actual integer determinant addition and
  norm identities; the cubic discriminant identity and square bound;
  divisibility/height forcing a zero phase; individual and product-modulus
  divisibility; a joint-map injection and finite target power bound; private
  signed-product independence through a group homomorphism with cubic kernel.
  The finite target theorem assumes actual collision divisibility, height and
  separation for its pairs. It does not construct the JP norm-one residue
  quotients, lift their torsion, count the entire integer l1 ball or prove
  that arbitrary ABC data satisfy these premises.
* QuotientCurveArithmetic: seventeen theorems. The homogeneous cubic symmetry,
  projective Mobius cube on integer coordinates and cubic difference; eight
  complete square-fibre enumerations in the displayed finite base and quadratic
  pair models; two integer nonsquare theorems and their two trace obstructions;
  the actual Frobenius-coefficient arithmetic; the torsion gcd/divisor arithmetic.
  The eight counts add the two infinity points as an explicit arithmetic term.
  Their interpretation as smooth projective point counts is the ordinary
  smoothness/chart proof. Pair multiplication is explicit, but no equivalence
  with an abstract finite-field quotient is silently asserted in Lean.

QS's field action, Jacobians, rational simplicity, ranks and torsion groups
remain ordinary mathematics using the cited primary sources. QH's Picard
groups, finite quadratic Chabauty set, inverse image and conditional direct
entry likewise remain ordinary mathematics. In particular a finite-container
existence theorem has not computed its coordinates or classified rational
points, and is not a Lean theorem in these modules.

All unrefuted parent routes remain open. The new seventeenth-round CC, DP and
QL notes are separate research, not silently included in this formal scope.
