\section{Exact twists of the two non-CM boundary orbits}
\label{bt-section}

The two surviving pure-branch orbits are related by quadratic twists.
This section proves the relation using a complete exact coefficient
comparison and a proved common-level determination bound. It then
derives the boundary module premise for every actual pure prime power.
The modular-form coefficients are exact software inputs, independently
repeated; they are not asserted to have Lean proofs.

Retain $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, the actual quartic $F$, the Frey
curves $E_{a,b}$, the boundary curve $E_0$, and the order-four character
$\chi$ from the preceding sections. Write $\epsilon=\psi_3$ for the
quadratic character of conductor $12$. Let $f$ be the normalized
representative of the non-CM orbit at level $576$ identified in
Theorem~\ref{bm-orbit}, and let $g$ be the representative of the
unique non-CM orbit at level $288$ in the exact enumeration.
For both, fix the recorded field model $L=\mathbb Q[z]/(z^4+1)$.

\begin{lemma}[Common level and coefficient determination]\label{bt-common-level}
Both a coefficient embedding of $g$ and either quadratic coefficient
twist $f\otimes\psi_{-2}$ or $f\otimes\psi_2$ belong to
\[
 S_2(\Gamma_0(M),\epsilon),\qquad M=36864.
\]
Equality of their Fourier coefficients in degrees $0$ through $12288$
implies equality of the modular forms.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A quadratic twist of conductor $m=8$ has a valid level bound $Nm^2$
and the same character as a form of level $N$ and character $\epsilon$.
Here is a direct check. For $T_u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u/m\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the nonzero Gauss sum gives the twist as
\[
 \tau(\psi)^{-1}\sum_{u\bmod m}\psi(u)f|_2T_u.
\]
Its coefficients are $\psi(n)a_n(f)$. Given
$\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\in\Gamma_0(Nm^2)$, choose $v\equiv ud^2\pmod m$. Then
\[
 T_u\gamma T_{-v}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a+uc/m&b+(ud-av)/m-ucv/m^2\\
 c&d-cv/m
 \end{pmatrix}
 \in\Gamma_0(N).
\]
Its lower-right entry equals $d$ modulo $N$. The permutation
$u\mapsto ud^2$ preserves the quadratic character, giving exactly
the transformation character $\epsilon(d)$. Rational translates
preserve holomorphy and vanishing at cusps. This proves the level
bound, independently of a software metadata level.

Take $N=576$. Then $M=576\cdot8^2=36864$, and $288\mid M$.
The index and safe cutoff are
\begin{equation}\label{bt-sturm-bound}
 I=[\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(M)]
   =M(1+1/2)(1+1/3)=73728,
 \qquad B=\left\lfloor\frac{2I}{12}\right\rfloor=12288.
\end{equation}
To prove coefficient determination, suppose a nonzero difference $H$
vanishes through degree $B$. Form the product of its weight-two
translates over the $I$ right cosets of $\Gamma_0(M)$ in
$\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Its square is a nonzero
holomorphic level-one form of weight $4I$: squaring removes the
possible transformation signs of the quadratic character. Its order
at infinity is at least $2(B+1)$, as every other cusp contribution
is nonnegative. This exceeds $4I/12$, contradicting the level-one
valence formula; see \cite[Proposition~4.12 and Example~4.13]{MilneModularFunctions}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The two exact boundary twists]\label{bt-twists}
In the recorded common field model, let $\sigma_j(z)=z^j$ for odd
$j$ modulo $8$. The normalized forms satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{bt-twist-identities}
 g^{\sigma_7}=f\otimes\psi_{-2},\qquad
 g^{\sigma_5}=f\otimes\psi_2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The polynomial $z^4+1$ is the eighth cyclotomic polynomial, so these
are field automorphisms. In the basis $(1,z,z^2,z^3)$ they act by
\begin{align*}
 \sigma_7(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)&=(c_0,-c_3,-c_2,-c_1),\\
 \sigma_5(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)&=(c_0,-c_1,c_2,-c_3).
\end{align*}
The retained PARI/GP~2.15.4 computation initializes both complete
newspaces, selects the unique non-CM form in each by its CM test,
and checks their field and character models. Their dimensions are
$4,8$, and the automatically selected orbit indices are $1,3$.
It outputs every coefficient from degree $0$ to degree $12288$ in
four rational coordinates. The independent Python comparison applies
the two displayed coordinate transformations and the quadratic
Kronecker values of discriminants $-8,8$. All coefficients agree,
including every degree divisible by $2$ or $3$.

The canonical evidence retains both complete arrays, each with $12289$
rows. A second independent implementation selects the forms afresh,
uses PARI's actual twist operator, and compares the four coordinates;
all $98312$ coordinate equalities agree. It does not reuse the first
program's coefficient cache or polynomial substitution. These are
complete exact modular-form computations, rather than floating point
approximations. Lemma~\ref{bt-common-level} upgrades these full-cutoff
equalities to \eqref{bt-twist-identities}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The common boundary module for every actual pure power]
\label{bt-pure-module}
Let $a,b$ be positive coprime integers, let $p>7$ be prime, and
suppose $F(a,b)=Q^p$ for an integer $Q>1$. Then for some character
\[
 \nu\in\{1,\psi_3|_{G_K},\psi_{-2}|_{G_K},\psi_{-6}|_{G_K}\}
\]
one has the full residual module isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{bt-module-isomorphism}
 E_{a,b}[p]\otimes\overline{\mathbb F}_p
 \simeq (E_0[p]\otimes\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\otimes\nu.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual pure-branch modular theorem, the CM exclusion and the
complete enumeration give exactly the two possible orbits in
Corollary~\ref{cb-two-orbits}. By \eqref{bt-twist-identities}, every
embedding of the $g$ orbit is the quadratic twist by $\psi_{-2}$ of
some embedding of the $f$ orbit; rational character values commute
with embeddings.

For the $f$ orbit, the compatible boundary identification restricts
to the system of $E_0$ times $\chi^\sigma$, whereas the actual system
restricts to $E_{a,b}$ times $\chi$. The ratio is either $1$ or
$\chi^{-2}=\psi_3|_{G_K}$. The $g$ orbit adds
$\psi_{-2}|_{G_K}$, producing exactly the four displayed characters.
An equality of twisted modular forms gives the corresponding twist
of compatible representations: their Frobenius polynomials agree at
every prime outside the finite ramification set, so Chebotarev and
semisimplicity identify the representations. The checked large
projective-image input gives absolute irreducibility of the actual
residual representation. Hence the semisimplified congruence becomes
the full module isomorphism \eqref{bt-module-isomorphism}.
All four characters are quadratic and unramified outside $6$.
\end{proof}

Thus the boundary premise of Theorem~\ref{bc-pure} is now derived
for every actual pure $p$-th power. In particular every prime $q\mid Q$
satisfies $q\ge(\sqrt p-1)^2$, neither $7$ nor $13$ divides $Q$,
and for $q\ne p$ one has $p\mid(q+1)^2-t_q(E_0)^2$.
For every sufficiently large fixed $p$, its permitted split rational
prime support has density
\[
 \frac{2p^2-p-5}{2(p-1)^2(p+1)}\sim\frac1p.
\]
This remains a fixed-$p$ statement with no uniform Chebotarev error
term. The next section uses two-torsion to remove the exception $q=p$
and improve the cutoff.

Already the displayed cutoff and $F\le(a+b)^4$ give
\[
 \log(a+b)\ge\frac p2\log(\sqrt p-1).
\]
Alternatively $F\le13\max(a,b)^4$ gives the same lower bound for
$\log\max(a,b)$ minus $(\log13)/4$. These are point-height lower
bounds, compatible with an $O(p)$ coefficient-height bound for a
defining Kummer cover. Neither is an upper bound for its points.
