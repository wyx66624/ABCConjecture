\section{Elementary gaps between actual norm squares}
\label{sgap-section}

The following bounds are elementary restrictions on particular residual
square classes. They use actual polynomial identities and integer
square gaps, with no probabilistic transfer.

\begin{theorem}[A residue-sensitive axis gap]\label{sgap-axis}
Let $a,b$ be positive coprime integers and $F(a,b)=q^2$, $q>0$.
Put $S=8a^2+12ab+11b^2$ and let $r\in\{1,\ldots,8\}$ be its
least positive residue modulo eight. Then
\[
 2ra<9b^3,\qquad
 \max\{a,b\}<\tfrac32\min\{a,b\}^3.
\]
In particular no such seed has $\min\{a,b\}=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expansion gives
\[
 D:=S^2-64F=72ab^3+57b^4>0.
\]
Thus $8q<S$, and congruence modulo eight gives $8q\le S-r$.
Both sides are nonnegative, so $D\ge r(2S-r)$. If $2ra\ge9b^3$,
then $72ab^3\le16ra^2$ and $24rab\ge108b^4$, giving
\[
 r(2S-r)-D\ge51b^4+22rb^2-r^2>0
\]
because $b\ge1$ and $r\le8$. This is a contradiction. For odd $b$,
$S\equiv4a+3\pmod8$, so $r=3$ or $7$. For even $b$, primitivity
forces $a$ odd, giving $r=4$ or $8$. Hence $r\ge3$ and $2a<3b^3$;
interchange $a,b$. If the minimum is one, the bound forces both to
be one, whereas $F(1,1)=13$ is not a square.
\end{proof}

This applies to every actual even exponent with a square residual,
and in particular to pure even powers. It gives an explicit finite
axis search bound, while remaining compatible with the earlier
infinite family of positive square second norms.

\begin{proposition}[A complementary diagonal identity]\label{sgap-diagonal}
Let $a,b$ be positive coprime integers and $F(a,b)=13q^2$ with $q>0$.
If $c=a-b\ne0$, then
\[
 52ab+14c^2+1\le3c^4.
\]
For each fixed nonzero $c$, all positive solutions in this square
class admit a finite exact divisor parametrization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $A=7a^2+12ab+7b^2=26ab+7c^2$. The exact identity
\[
 52F=A^2+3c^4
\]
gives $(26q-A)(26q+A)=3c^4$. The smaller factor is positive, so
the difference of squares is at least $2A+1$, proving the inequality.

For completeness, choose positive divisor pairs $d_1<d_2$ of $3c^4$.
Retain only integral positive
\[
 q=(d_1+d_2)/52,\qquad A=(d_2-d_1)/2,
 \qquad u=(A-7c^2)/26.
\]
Require $c^2+4u=h^2$ for a positive integer $h>|c|$ with $h-c$ even.
Then $a=(h+c)/2$, $b=(h-c)/2$ give exactly all positive solutions;
impose their gcd condition if desired. Indeed these tests are precisely
$a-b=c$, $ab=u$ and the displayed difference of squares. Conversely
each solution supplies $d_1=26q-A$, $d_2=26q+A$.
\end{proof}

The diagonal identity and its divisor classification remain useful
explicit arithmetic even if a separate global argument further
restricts that square class. These gap arguments alone assert neither
such a global exclusion nor control of odd exponents, arbitrary
residual classes, or a full signed top-rank packet.
