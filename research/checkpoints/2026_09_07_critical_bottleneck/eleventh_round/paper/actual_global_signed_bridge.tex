\section{A formal passage from the signed tail to the global radical}
\label{ags-section}

Retain the actual prime-factorization observables of
Section~\ref{apl-section}: $S_Y,L_Y,R_Y$ and the signed logarithmic
cost $\mathcal S_Y=S_Y-3R_Y$, with a natural cutoff $Y$.
For positive $N$, $R_0(N)=\log\operatorname{rad}(N)$.
The following finite step is independent of how a tail estimate
or a small-prime estimate is obtained.

\begin{lemma}[Global signed comparison]\label{ags-global}
For every positive integer $N$ and natural $Y$,
\begin{equation}\label{ags-comparison}
 \mathcal S_0(N)\le\mathcal S_Y(N)+L_Y(N).
\end{equation}
If real numbers $t,\delta,b,l$ satisfy
\[
 3t-\delta\le\log N,\qquad
 \mathcal S_Y(N)\le b,\qquad L_Y(N)\le l,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{ags-height}
 t-(\delta+b+l)/3\le R_0(N).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A prime's logarithmic weight can only decrease when the cutoff
increases. Summing over the actual prime support gives $R_Y(N)\le R_0(N)$.
There is no prime at most zero, so $L_0(N)=0$ and
$S_0(N)=\log N=S_Y(N)+L_Y(N)$. Substitute these identities into
the signed costs to obtain \eqref{ags-comparison}. Since
$\mathcal S_0(N)=\log N-3R_0(N)$, the three assumed inequalities
then give \eqref{ags-height}.
\end{proof}

In particular, for a positive integer $n$ and $t>0$, the finite
hypotheses
\[
 3t-4t/n\le\log N,\qquad
 \mathcal S_Y(N)\le39t/n+2\eta t+e,\qquad
 L_Y(N)\le\eta t
\]
imply exactly
\begin{equation}\label{ags-numerical}
 1-43/(3n)-\eta-e/(3t)\le R_0(N)/t.
\end{equation}
This is the numerical step used by the large-cutoff bridge; the
analytic availability of its hypotheses is not assumed universally.

Under the positive integer arm hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{apl-finite-compensation}, with $T=(aA)(bB)(dC)$,
$U=abd$ and $\Delta=3\log H-\log T$, the same comparison proves
\begin{align}
 \mathcal S_0(T)\le{}&\Delta+\log H_1+\log U+\tfrac32 L_Y(T)
 \label{ags-arm}\\
 &+\tfrac32(E_{2,Y}(A)+E_{2,Y}(B))-3R_Y(C).\notag
\end{align}
The coefficient of the small-prime mass is three halves because
\eqref{ags-comparison} adds its full mass to the half-mass term
already present in the actual finite tail theorem. This argument
preserves arbitrary overlap with the old factor $U$.

\paragraph{Formal scope and verification.}
The module \texttt{ActualGlobalSignedBridge} contains nine proved
declarations. They establish antitonicity of actual prime weights
and radical sums, the zero-cutoff identities, the global comparison,
the height transfer, the numerical constants in
\eqref{ags-numerical}, and the actual finite global arm theorem
\eqref{ags-arm}. Its definitions are the actual
\texttt{Nat.factorization}, \texttt{Nat.primeFactors} and
\texttt{Real.log} observables imported from the preceding two modules.
The formal radical is the actual finite sum of prime logarithms.

A fresh scoped run compiled these nine declarations and all 24 local
prime-logarithm dependency declarations with Lean 4.32.0 and warnings
as errors. The pinned Mathlib cache was reused at revision
\texttt{81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997}; no complete Mathlib
rebuild is claimed. All 33 declarations were queried for axioms, with
only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}
in the union. Independent source review checked all nine statements
and proofs and matched the final source hashes to the manifest.

The numerical theorem explicitly assumes its height, tail and
small-mass bounds, as well as $t>0$ and a nonzero natural $n$.
It does not prove the common-set analytic estimate, actual cap
membership, or exceptional-root control. Thus the finite global
prime-factorization step is formalized, while the large-cutoff
analytic and pointwise number-theoretic obligations remain distinct.
