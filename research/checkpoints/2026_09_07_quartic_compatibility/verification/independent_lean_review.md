The pair multiplication uses zeta squared equal to zeta minus one; hence r=2*zeta-1 has square -3. The definitions of alphaPlus and alphaMinus are exactly 3*y^2 plus or minus x*r, not independent formal placeholders. The first four statements are the universal actual quartic/Fermat identity, the inverse-square identity, and the two odd-chart substitution identities. Their lack of positivity or parity hypotheses is appropriate for polynomial identities; no inverse parametrization is inferred.

Their actual values are c4=288*(5*y^2-x*r), c6=6912*y*(3*x*r-7*y^2), and discriminant=165888*F*alphaPlus. These signs and constants agree with the ordinary proof.

The residue statements are actual finite `Fin 2` and `Fin 3` computations transferred to all integer pairs under Int.gcd(a,b)=1. They prove F odd and x nonzero modulo three. The discriminant nonvanishing proof correctly uses the resulting F nonzero and the alpha norm identity, and remains valid for negative or boundary primitive pairs. The final divisibility theorem gives all three actual consecutive Frey residues for every integer L,k, with explicit quotient witnesses. It does not assume that a locally compatible seed is a global power point.
