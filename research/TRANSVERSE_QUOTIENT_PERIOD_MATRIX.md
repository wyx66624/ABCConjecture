# Exact period matrices for a universally transverse graph quotient

## 1. Complex set-up

Let

\[
 E_\tau=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z),
 \qquad \tau\in\mathfrak H,
\]

and put

\[
 A_\tau=E_\tau^g
 =\mathbb C^g/(\mathbb Z^g+\tau\mathbb Z^g).
\]

Let `ell` be odd and let `T` be an integral symmetric `g x g` matrix whose
reduction modulo `ell` is invertible.  In the standard level-`ell`
symplectic coordinates, consider the graph

\[
 H_T=
 \left\{
 \frac{Tb+\tau b}{\ell}mod
  (\mathbb Z^g+\tau\mathbb Z^g):
 b\in(\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z)^g
 \right\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The symmetry of `T` is precisely the isotropy condition for the product Weil
pairing.  Since `H_T` has order `ell^g`, it is maximal isotropic.

## 2. Exact quotient period matrix

### Theorem 2.1

The complex torus quotient `A_tau/H_T` has period lattice

\[
 \Lambda_T
 =\mathbb Z^g+\Omega_T\mathbb Z^g,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\Omega_T=\frac{T+\tau I_g}{\ell}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \Im\Omega_T=\frac{\Im\tau}{\ell}I_g,
 \qquad
 \det\Im\Omega_T=\left(\frac{\Im\tau}{\ell}\right)^g.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The descended polarization is principal and its pullback is `ell` times the
product principal polarization on `E_tau^g`.

#### Proof

The quotient lattice is the superlattice

\[
 \Lambda_T
 =\mathbb Z^g+\tau\mathbb Z^g
  +\frac{T+\tau I_g}{\ell}\mathbb Z^g.
\]

Let

\[
 h_b=\frac{Tb+\tau b}{\ell}.
\]

Then

\[
 \tau b=\ell h_b-Tb,
\]

and `Tb` is integral.  Hence the original `tau Z^g` is already contained in

\[
 \mathbb Z^g+rac{T+\tau I_g}{\ell}\mathbb Z^g.
\]

The reverse inclusion is immediate, proving (2.1).  Symmetry and positive
definiteness of the imaginary part put `Omega_T` in Siegel upper half-space.
The polarization assertion is the standard descent theorem for a maximal
isotropic subgroup of `A[ell]`.

## 3. Relation with the repository's graph convention

Some modules write the universal graph as `B=T A` rather than `A=T B`.  If
`T` is invertible, the latter is the graph of `T^{-1}` in the former
convention.  For the symmetric scalar-square operators used in the repository,

\[
 T^2=\nu I,
 \qquad
 T^{-1}=\nu^{-1}T,
\]

so the inverse is again symmetric and has no ground-field eigenvalue.  Thus the
period-matrix convention (1.1) does not change universal transversality or
isotropy.

## 4. Nonarchimedean Tate analogue

Let `K` be a complete nonarchimedean field and let

\[
 E_q^{\rm an}=K^\times/q^\mathbb Z,
 \qquad 0<|q|<1.
\]

The product uniformization is

\[
 (K^\times)^g/q^{\mathbb Z^g}.
\]

Assume the field contains `mu_ell` and an `ell`-th root `s` of `q`.  The graph
subgroup corresponding to (1.1) is generated by

\[
 \bigl(\zeta^{Tb_1}s^{b_1},\ldots,
       \zeta^{Tb_g}s^{b_g}\bigr),
 \qquad b\in(\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z)^g,
\]

with the obvious vector notation for the root-of-unity factor.

### Theorem 4.1 (tropical division)

The quotient tropical period lattice is

\[
 \frac{v(q)}\ell\mathbb Z^g.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently every toric monodromy exponent is divided by `ell`.

#### Proof

Root-of-unity factors have valuation zero.  The added period `s^b` has
valuation vector

\[
 \frac{v(q)}\ell b.
\]

The same lattice calculation as in Section 2 shows that the old lattice
`v(q)Z^g` is contained in the lattice generated by these root periods, and the
latter is exactly `(v(q)/ell)Z^g`.

### Corollary 4.2

If `ell>v(q)`, every normalized quotient toric exponent is strictly less than
one.  Summed over multiplicative primes, the quotient tropical boundary is at
most one radical copy per toric direction.

## 5. Archimedean selection problem

Formula (2.1) makes the remaining complex problem explicit.  A raw quotient
matrix has imaginary part `y/ell`, where `y=Im tau`, and real part `T/ell`.
After reduction by `Sp_(2g)(Z)`, its cusp height depends on Diophantine
approximation properties of the rational symmetric matrix `T/ell`.

For `g=1`, the Hecke points

\[
 \frac{a+\tau}{\ell}
\]

show both behaviours: rational numbers `a/ell` with a short continued-fraction
denominator lead to compact representatives, while exceptional residues may
lead to cusp height comparable to `ell/y` or `y/ell`.  In higher dimension the
same issue is controlled by simultaneous rational approximation to `T/ell`.

The universal-transversality condition is algebraic modulo `ell`; it leaves a
positive proportion of symmetric matrices available.  This motivates the
following retained target.

### Target theorem 5.1 (transverse Hecke compactness on average)

For `g=2`, let `U_ell` be the orbit of symmetric matrices whose characteristic
polynomial is irreducible over `F_ell`.  Prove a uniform bound for a logarithmic
Siegel cusp-height function `G` of the form

\[
 \frac1{|U_\ell|}
 \sum_{T\in U_\ell}
 G\!\left(\frac{T+\tau I_2}{\ell}\right)
 \le
 C\left(1+\frac{\Im\tau}{\ell}ight)
 +C\log\ell.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The logarithmic cusp singularity is integrable, so effective Hecke
 equidistribution or a direct geometry-of-numbers count may prove (5.1).

## 6. Why the Galois average is the relevant average

When the Frey mod-`ell` image contains `SL_2(F_ell)`, Galois acts on graph
parameters by fractional linear transformations

\[
 T\longmapsto
 (\gamma I+\delta T)(\alpha I+\beta T)^{-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The no-eigenvalue condition makes every denominator invertible.  This action
preserves the nonsplit/universally transverse locus and is transitive on the
appropriate nonsplit-Cartan orbit.  Hence the normalized archimedean sum over
embeddings of the chosen quotient is the orbit average in (5.1), rather than
the value at one arbitrarily bad Hecke point.

At finite multiplicative places every conjugate kernel remains transverse, so
all places simultaneously retain the tropical division (4.1).  This is the
mechanism unavailable to a single cyclic line.

## 7. Remaining arithmetic bridge

Even a proof of (5.1) must be inserted into an arithmetic boundary-form
identity.  The finite Heisenberg--Fourier theorem gives the exact complex basis
change, while the root-discriminant theorem controls the descent field.  The
remaining tasks are:

1. identify a genus-two toroidal boundary modular form for the descended
   principal polarization;
2. compute its nonarchimedean order from (4.1);
3. compare its archimedean Petersson/Quillen norm with the cusp-height function
   in (5.1);
4. normalize the product formula to recover the elliptic `Q/6` coefficient.

The period calculation closes the local analytic geometry; it does not by
itself assert the global inequality.

## 8. Formalization plan

The lattice identity in Theorem 2.1 is finite linear algebra and can be
formalized before Siegel reduction.  The nonarchimedean statement can be
packaged at the value-group level.  Hecke equidistribution and the boundary
metric remain mathematical source theorems and are not introduced as axioms.
