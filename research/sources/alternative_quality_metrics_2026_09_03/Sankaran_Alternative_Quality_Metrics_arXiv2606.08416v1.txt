ABSTRACT
Theabc-conjecture (Masser and Oesterl´ e) has remained open for decades. By measuringabc-triples using a
particularquality metric, the conjecture seeks the asymptotic distribution of triples of sufficient quality. We
create new classes of quality metrics to develop variants on theabc-conjecture, with each metric based upon
the doubly geometric mean of the prime factors of triples. We investigate the behavior of the resulting class
of quality metrics; by determining families of triples that yield high quality, we establish several asymptotic
results that are analogous to theabc-conjecture for our metrics. We also establish sharp phase transitions for
the behavior of families of such quality metrics within specified parametrizations for smoothness of primes
inabc-triples, using heuristics from the Szpiro ratio for associated Frey curves. Finally, we implement
algorithms to determine triples with high qualities with sub-linear runtime, an asymptotic speedup over
na¨ ıve approaches. Our analysis offers robust variations of, and connections to, theabc-conjecture that offer
independent questions of analytical interest.
1.Introduction
Theabc-conjecture, introduced by Joseph Oesterl´ e [1] and David Masser [2] in the 1980s, has remained
an active conjecture in number theory. The conjecture asserts an asymptotic result on what we denote the
qualityq(·) of triples (a, b, c) witha+b=c, for coprimea, b, c; such triples are denotedabc-triples. The
significance of theabc-conjecture extends far beyond an isolated statement; a proof would immediately
imply Fermat’s Last Theorem forn≥6 [3, 4], the Fermat–Catalan and Beal conjectures, and Szpiro’s
conjecture on elliptic curves [4], while also shedding light on the distribution of prime gaps. Outside pure
mathematics, deeper understanding of prime structure underlies cryptographic primitives (e.g. RSA), effi-
cient integer factorization (the General Number Field Sieve depends on the abundance of smooth numbers
[5]), and even biological phenomena – such as the prime-numbered life cycles of cicadas.
Several recent works have established theoretical results that provide insight towards the conjecture
itself [6] and developed computer algorithms to find high-quality triples quickly [7, 8, 9]. Nitaj character-
ized several of the relevant methodologies for triple searches [10]; van der Horst introduced an algorithm
using elliptic curves [11]; and Elkies illustrated connections to other unsolved problems in number theory
[12]. Given the intractable nature of determining high-quality triples under the standardquality metricto
evaluate the suitability ofabc-triples as measured by the conjecture, a natural strategy is to pursuealter-
nativequality metrics that preserve the conjecture’s qualitative content while being more tractable from
analytic standpoints. This approach is the focus of the ensuing manuscript; we note that previous variants
of the quality used in theabc-conjecture can be found in ratios involving conductors and discriminants of
associated elliptic curves, as well as minor algebraic variations [4, 11, 13]. Our metrics, which are based on
the Doubly Geometric Mean of the primes dividingabcfor each suchabc-triple (a, b, c), assume a distinct
approach by directly penalizing the numberωof distinct prime divisors ofabc.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
(1) We define several new quality metrics (Definitions 3.2, 4.1) in the same spirit as the standard
quality for theabc-conjecture, which (i) penalize the number of distinct prime divisors ofabc, and
(ii) incorporate roundness of anabc-triple.
(2) We analyze all variant quality metrics by constructing families of high-quality triples and investigat-
ing asymptotic growth rates (Theorems 3.5, 3.6, 3.8.) We develop an analogue of theabc-conjecture

6.Conclusion
We defined novel quality metrics that prioritize triples with smallω, using the Doubly Geometric Mean
(Def. 3.2). We analyzed the resulting quality metric to determine high-quality triples and asymptotics, de-
veloping characterizations of families yielding high-quality triples, including the Mersenne family (Theorem
3.8), the twin prime family (Theorem 3.10), and fixed sequences of primes (Theorem 3.7). We also invoked
Chen’s Theorem (Thm. 3.11) to develop a proof of the asymptotic analogue of theabc-conjecture for our
quality metric (Theorem 3.12). We also developed linear-time algorithms for determination of high-quality
triples (Algs. 3, 4).
We extended our quality metrics via a parametrization through the DGM quality class in Sec. 4 to
modulate asymptotic behavior. Upon establishing phase transition results that determined asymptotics
with respect to to parameterβ(Theorems 4.4, 4.6, 4.7, 4.8), with various degrees of control on families of
abc-triples andωitself, we extended such results to develop asymptotics motivated by Szpiro bounds and
relationships to the standard quality in theabc-conjecture itself (Theorems 4.10, 4.13). A full proof of 4.3
remains open, and finer results regarding bounds on the classq C(·, α, β) may even offer insight towards
proofs of the conjecture itself, given the asymptotic bounds in Theorem 4.13.
Our unconditional resolution of theabc-analogue via Chen’s theorem (Theorem 3.12) illustrates a princi-
ple that the DGM modification brings the analogue of the conjecture within reach of existing sieve methods,
suggesting that the standardabc-conjecture itself may lie on a continuum of quality metrics whose more
tractable members can be unconditionally settled, via the criticalβ 0 =ω/kmeasuring the exact damping

24
required for each family. We view our phase-transition framework as evidence that families of modified
quality metrics may be the natural objects of study, such that finer analysis of phase-space parameters
(α, β), with the relevant factorization of the standard qualityq s, may yield further progress toward the
abc-conjecture itself.
