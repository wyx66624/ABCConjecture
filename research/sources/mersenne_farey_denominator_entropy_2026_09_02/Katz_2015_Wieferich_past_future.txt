1. The early history
Fermat’s Last Theorem (FLT) is the assertion that for n ≥ 3, the equation
Xn +yn =Zn has no solutions in integers X,Y,Z with XYZ ̸= 0. It was proven
by Fermat for n = 4 and by Euler for n = 3, cf. [Weil, page 104]. To prove it in
general, then, it suﬃces to prove it when n is any prime p≥ 5. For ﬁxed p, the
”ﬁrst case” of FLT is the assertion that there are no integer solutions with XYZ
prime to p.
In 1909, Arthur Wieferich, then a 25 year old student at Munster, astounded the
mathematical world with the following theorem.
Theorem 1.1. (Wieferich) Letp≥ 5 be a prime. If the ﬁrst case of FLT is false,
then the following congruence holds:
2p−1≡ 1 mod p2.
A prime p for which this congruence holds is called a Wieferich prime (later, a
Wieferich prime to the base 2). It wasn’t until 1913 that the ﬁrst Wieferich prime,
1093, was found, by Meissner. The second, 3511, was found by Beeger in 1922.
Computer search so far shows that the next one, if there is a next one, exceeds
6.7× 1015, cf. [Do-Kl].
The very next year, 1910, Mirimanoﬀ showed that one could replace 2 by 3.
Theorem 1.2. (Mirimanoﬀ) Letp≥ 5 be a prime. If the ﬁrst case of FLT is false,
then the following congruence holds:
3p−1≡ 1 mod p2.
A primep for which this congruence holds is called a Wieferich prime to the base
3. The ﬁrst one is 11, the next, found by Kloss in 1965, cf. [Son, 3.2], is 1006003,
and the next one, if it exists, exceeds 9.7×1014, cf. [Do-Kl]. At this point, the race
was on.
In 1914, Vandiver showed that one could replace 2 or 3 by 5.
Theorem 1.3. (Vandiver) Letp≥ 7 be a prime. If the ﬁrst case of FLT is false,
then the following congruence holds:
5p−1≡ 1 mod p2.
There are now six known Wieferich primes to the base 5, namely
20771, 40487, 53471161, 1645333507, 6692367337, 188748146801.
The ﬁrst two of these were found in 1961, the last in 2005. If there is a next one,
it exceeds 9.7× 1014, cf. [Do-Kl].
In 1917, Pollaczek claimed one could replace 2 by any prime ≤ 31, cf. [Gr-Mo].
The current record, set is 1994 by Suzuki, is that one can replace 2 by any prime

3. Another point of view: the Wieferich Conjecture, first version
The Crandall-Dilcher-Pomerance prediction is analogous to the Lang-Trotter

5. Interlude: The question of robustness
Suppose we have a situation ( G/Z[1/N],P ∈ G(Z[1/N]), Lie(G)) for which
Wieferich equidistribution holds. Is it true that for any other choice Lie1(G)
of lattice in Lie(G), the modiﬁed situation ( G/Z[1/N],P ∈ G(Z[1/N]), Lie1(G))
also has Wieferich equidistribution.We do not know how to prove this. Moreover,
as an “abstract” statement about equidistribution, it is false.
Here is a cautionary counterexample, due to Deligne. Suppose that we have
a sequence of fractions ap/p, 0≤ ap ≤ p− 1, indexed by odd primes p, which
is equidistributed in Z⊗Z R/Z (for Haar measure). If we change the choice of
lattice from Z to 2Z, then we are asking about the equidistribution in R/Z of the
sequence dp/p, 0≤dp≤p− 1, with dp deﬁned by the congruence 2dp =ap mod p.
Imagine that in our original sequence, each ap is even, say ap = 2bp, with 0≤bp≤
(p− 1)/2. Then for each p we have dp =bp, and visibly this sequence bp/p is not
equidistributed in R/Z, as each term lies in the interval [0 , 1/2].
How do we know there exist sequences {ap/p, 0≤ ap≤ p− 1}odd p which are
equidistributed in R/Z in which everyap is even? In the product space ∏
odd p R/Z
with the product measure, the set of sequences{xp}odd p which are equidistributed
in R/Z for Haar measure has measure one. So there exist such sequences. Take
one, and replace, term by term, xp by the nearest fraction of the form 2 bp/p, 0≤
bp≤ (p− 1)/2; break ties arbitrarily. The distance (say measured in S1 as arc
length) betweenxp and 2bp/p is at most 2π/p, so tends to zero as p grows. Because
continuous functions on a compact metric space are uniformly continuous, if the
sequencexp is equidistributed, then so is any sequenceyp for whichdist(xp,yp)→ 0.
In particular, our sequence {2bp/p} is equidistributed.

gebra Lie(Jm/Z[1/N]) is H 1(C,I (P )⊗ I(Q)). For any proper X/Z extending
C/Z[1/N], and any coherent sheaf F onX extending I(P )⊗I(Q)), we can take
H 1(X,F)/(torsion) as a Z-form of Lie. The arguably simplest choice of such an F
on a givenX is I(P )⊗I(Q). Much remains to be done.
