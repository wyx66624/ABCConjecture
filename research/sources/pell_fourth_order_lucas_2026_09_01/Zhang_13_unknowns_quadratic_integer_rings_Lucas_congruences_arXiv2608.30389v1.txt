GENG-RUI ZHANG
Abstract. For every quadratic number field K, we prove a uniform 3-unknown Dio-
phantine definition of integer tuples in OK, allowing finitely many polynomial nonva-
nishing conditions. This yields an effective +3 transfer principle and a 13-unknown
representation of every recursively enumerable integer relation. Consequently, there
exists an absolute degree bound D0 ≥ 1 such that for every quadratic number field
K, there is no algorithm that, given
P (Y1, . . . , Y13) ∈ Z[Y1, . . . , Y13], deg P ≤ D0,
decides whether P = 0 has a solution in O13
K .
The arithmetic input is a fourth-order Pell–Lucas congruence. It is a specialization
of the norm-one Lucas multiplication formula, which yields exact valuations for the
deviation of a Lucas quotient from its linear term, together with deviation criteria for
Lucas–Wieferich and Wall–Sun–Sun primes. We also establish local surjectivity and
ℓ-adic density for second-order correction terms for norm-one Lucas sequences.

1. Introduction
Hilbert’s tenth problem (HTP) over a commutative ring R asks whether there is an
algorithm deciding the solvability in R of polynomial equations with coeﬀicients in R.
For the rings of integers of quadratic number fields, undecidability was proved by Denef
[Den75]. Koymans–Pagano [ KP26] and Alpöge–Bhargava–Ho–Shnidman [ ABH+26] in-
dependently proved that HTP is undecidable over the ring of integers of every number
field.
We study the quantitative problem for quadratic fields: how many unknowns suﬀice
for undecidability when the input polynomial has coeﬀicients in Z? This question is

finitely many polynomial equalities and nonvanishing conditions, can be transferred to
OK using 3 additional unknowns; this number is independent of the tuple length and
of the number of conditions.

Applying Theorem 1.3 to Theorem 1.4 gives Theorem 4.2: every r.e. relation on Zr
has a representation over OK with 13 unknowns, uniformly in r. Fixing one nonrecursive
r.e. set A ⊆ Z≥0 then yields Theorem 1.1; the degree D0 is uniformly bounded because
in the explicit construction, the degrees of polynomials only depend on deg(QA) and
the signature of K.
1.2. Toward 12 unknowns. The unknown count underlying Theorem 1.1 factors as
13 = 10 + 3 , so there are two possible routes to 12.
The first is to improve the source theorem over Z, Theorem 1.4. Consequently, a
9-unknown undecidability source theorem over Z would yield a 12-unknown bound over
OK via Theorem 1.3. Sun [ Sun21, Theorem 1.1(i)] proved 9-unknown representations
of r.e. sets when one unknown is constrained to be nonnegative. Another possible
approach is to reuse the nonnegativity unknown inside the transfer construction; the
present construction does not achieve this.
The second route is to reduce the 3-unknown cost in Theorem 1.2. In both cases,
its proof uses two Pell-square conditions. In the imaginary case, they force two mixed
expressions to be integral. In the real case, their quotient supplies the rationality
required by Proposition 3.7. The relation-combining polynomial F requires the third
auxiliary unknown; see Lemma 2.2. A uniform 2-unknown definition of
a ∈ Zn; R (a) ̸= 0
inside OK would therefore give a 12-unknown theorem.
1.3. Applications. Section 4 proves the transfer theorems and the 13-unknown r.e.
representation theorem (Theorem 4.2); the latter yields Theorem 1.1. Then we transfer
the explicit 11-unknown universal pair (11; ∆Z ≈ 1:68105 · 1063) over Z of Bayer et
al. [ BDH+25, BD25] to a 14-unknown universal pair (14; 2∆Z) over every quadratic
integer ring. Corollary 4.4 gives the resulting 14-unknown undecidability statement for
all quadratic integer rings, with a uniform explicit degree bound 2∆Z.
Section 5 studies the Lucas expansion behind Proposition 2.4. Proposition 5.1 and
Corollary 5.2 present all-orders and fourth-order expansion formulas. Corollary 5.3
then isolates the finer arithmetic information used here: the exact `-adic valuation of
the deviation of unk=un from its linear term. Corollaries 5.4 and 5.5 express Lucas–
Wieferich and Wall–Sun–Sun conditions through these deviations, while Theorem 5.6
and Corollary 5.7 show local surjectivity and `-adic density for normalized second-order
correction terms.
Structure of the paper. In § 2, we recall the relation-combining polynomial of Sun
and develop the Pell congruence. In § 3, we establish the integrality criteria for imagi-
nary and real quadratic fields. In § 4, we complete the proofs of Theorems 1.2, 1.3, 4.2,
and 1.1; then we derive the universal-pair and the explicit bounded-degree consequence.
In § 5, we present the relevant Lucas multiplication expansion, exact linearization-
deviation valuations, and Wieferich deviation criteria, and prove the local surjectivity
and `-adic density results.
