## 2. The finite local arithmetic passes

Put

\[
 p=109,\quad \ell=7,\quad b=109^{36}-1,\qquad
 C:\ y^2=x(x-1)(x+b).
\]

The triple `(1,b,b+1)` is positive and primitive. Its invariants are

\[
 \Delta=16b^2(b+1)^2,\qquad c_4=16(1+b+b^2).
\]

At 109, `b=-1 mod 109`; consequently `v_p(c4)=0` and
`v_p(Delta)=72`. An integral Weierstrass model with unit `c4` cannot be
made nonminimal by an integral change reducing the discriminant: such a
reduction would also require positive valuation of `c4`. Thus this is a
minimal multiplicative model. The reduced equation is
`y^2=x(x-1)^2`. In coordinates `X=x-1` at its node, its tangent cone is
`y^2=X^2`, with two distinct rational tangent directions. The reduction
is therefore split multiplicative, not merely potentially multiplicative.
The native Tate parameter satisfies

\[
 q\in\mathbb Q_{109},\qquad v_{109}(q)=72.
\]

Let

\[
 L=\mathbb Q(i,C[30]),\quad L'=L(C[7]),\quad E=L'_w\quad(w\mid109).
\]

The Tate uniformization identifies the field of full `N`-torsion with
`Q_p(mu_N,q^(1/N))` for `N` prime to `p`. Indeed, the torsion classes
are represented by `zeta*q^(k/N)` in `Qpbar^*/q^Z`. An automorphism
fixing all these classes fixes these roots: a possible quotient by
`q^Z` has valuation zero and hence is the identity. Coprimality of 30
and 7 gives

\[
 E=\mathbb Q_{109}(i,\mu_{210},q^{1/210}).
\]

The factor `i` is already in `Q_109`. All prime-to-109 roots of unity
and all 210th roots of units become available in an unramified extension,
by the residue-field equation and Hensel's lemma. After this base change,
the ramified generator has valuation

\[
 72/210=12/35.
\]

Since `gcd(12,35)=1`, its ramified field is the one generated by a
35th root of 109. Its ramification index is exactly 35. The extension is
tame, so the different has uniformizer exponent 34. In the normalization
`v_p(p)=1`, this proves

\[
 e(E/\mathbb Q_p)=35,\qquad d_E=34/35.
\]

This argument checks the full division field, including the unit part of
the Tate parameter. It does not infer the ramification index solely from
a lower bound coming from a value-group denominator.

## 3. The native logarithmic tensor passes

For the explicit local implementation, take a root `a in E` with
`a^14=q` and set

\[
 u=\frac{\log(1+109)}{109},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{\log(1+109a)}{109}.
\]

The first term in each logarithm series has strictly smaller valuation
than all subsequent terms. For `z=109` or `109a`, one has `v_p(z)>=1`,
whereas
`v_p(z^n/n)-v_p(z)=(n-1)v_p(z)-v_p(n)>0` for `n>=2`.
It follows that

\[
 v_p(u)=0,\qquad v_p(\tau)=v_p(a)=36/7=180/35.
\]

These are the normalized coefficients of Joshi III (9.7.2.2), p. 110.
The normalization of the background is not an assumption inferred from
the word “logarithm”: Remark 9.8.2.3, p. 117, specifically says that
the logarithmic background contributes unit norm. This agrees with
division by `p`. Using unscaled background logarithms instead would add
2 to the three-slot tensor valuation and would still not repair the
noncontainment below, but it would be a different exact formula.

For `j=2`, choose the three-slot, one-theta/two-background implementation

\[
 T=E\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}E\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}E,
 \qquad t=u\otimes u\otimes\tau.
\]

The field `E/Q_p` is Galois. Writing `d=[E:Q_p]`, its tensor algebra is
`T ≅ E^(d^2)`, and its maximal order is `B ≅ O_E^(d^2)`.
Every component of `t` has valuation `180/35`, because all field
automorphisms preserve the native valuation. In particular `t` is a unit
of the **algebra** `T`, but is not a unit of its integral order `B`.

Any local source implementation containing the specified marked root
classes and the coherent field-marking-induced collation maps contains
this point before taking a hull. Convex closure retains the point; a
`B`-module hull then contains `tB`. This assertion needs no independently
chosen action on repeated tensor slots. It does not, by itself, establish
that the explicit local implementation is a block of a globally admissible
theta region in either source paper.

The slot convention must be stated. Joshi III p. 114 contains inconsistent
procession/product indices. The present test uses `m=j+1`, as fixed by
the explicit slot set `{0,...,j}` in original IUT IV Step (iv), p. 27,
and the displayed one-theta/background recipe. This is not a proof that
all the source's inconsistent indexing has already been resolved.

For completeness, the stated native local family's integral-hull check
also passes. Here logarithm maps `O_E^*` onto the maximal ideal, since
`1/e=1/35>1/(p-1)`. Hence

\[
 I=\frac1p\log(O_E^*)=\pi_E^{-34}O_E,
 \qquad \tau\in p^6I.
\]

Integral topological group isomorphisms of this lattice preserve `p^6I`.
With one theta entry and at most three backgrounds, every component in
the tensor image of its convex closure has valuation at least
`6-m*34/35 >= 74/35 > 0`. Thus, for this explicitly fixed native family,
an integral `B`-module hull exists. This is not a statement that arbitrary
changes to the source family preserve the same bound.

## 4. The original container and all its constants pass

Original IUT I Definition 3.1(c), p. 62, defines `K=F(E_F[ell])`.
Thus the basic field assignment `F=L`, `K=L'` agrees with that original
notation. Example 3.2(iv), p. 71, defines the underlined root parameter as
`q_v^(1/(2ell))`. There is no ambiguity about the denominator `2ell`.

In IUT IV Step (v), p. 27, the distinguished slot is `j`, and the
input parameter at a bad place is the valuation of the `j^2` power of
that underlined root. The factor also appears explicitly in the
coefficient `j^2/(2ell)` on p. 28. Consequently the tested value is

\[
 \lambda_\theta=\frac{j^2}{2\ell}v_p(q)=144/7.
\]

For each of the three copies of `E`, Propositions 1.1–1.2, pp. 9–10,
give

\[
 d_i=34/35,\quad
 a_i=\frac1{35}\left\lceil\frac{35}{107}\right\rceil=1/35,
 \quad
 b_i=\left\lfloor\frac{\log(109\cdot35/108)}{\log109}\right\rfloor-1/35
     =-1/35.
\]

Here `d_i` is the valuation of a **different generator**, not the
unscaled discriminant exponent. The sums are over all three slots:

\[
 d_I=102/35,\qquad a_I=3/35,\qquad b_I=-3/35.
\]

Proposition 1.4(iii), p. 13, has the relevant maximal-order container

\[
 C_\theta=p^{\lfloor\lambda_\theta-d_I-a_I\rfloor-b_I}B
 =p^{598/35}B.
\]

Its hypotheses on `lambda` and ramification hold for the numerical test:
`lambda_theta=720/35` lies in `(1/e)Z`, and all `e_i<=p-2`, so its
exceptional set `I*` is empty. The floor is
`floor(144/7-3)=17`. Rational powers here denote ideals with the specified
valuation; they do not require a chosen real-power scalar.

Since `180/35<598/35`, the finite local conclusion is

\[
 t\notin C_\theta,\qquad
 H_{\rm native}\not\subset C_\theta
 \quad\text{for every }B\text{-module }H_{\rm native}\text{ containing }t.
\]

The corresponding maximal-order-normalized logarithmic volumes are

\[
 V_B(tB)=-\frac{180}{35}\log109,
 \qquad V_B(C_\theta)=-\frac{598}{35}\log109.
\]

The weaker numerical bound in Proposition 1.4(iii) is
`(-lambda_theta+d_I+1)log109=-(583/35)log109`, still smaller than
`V_B(tB)`. Thus applying that bound to this same native hull would also
be false. The source applies it to the powered-pilot family, not to an
arbitrary native principal-unit family.

This failure is a set-theoretic comparison in one algebra before taking
volume. Changing from the tensor-order normalization `A` to maximal-order
normalization `B` does not remove it. The report's index conversion also
checks: in this Galois case
`V_A(U)-V_B(U)=(m-1)*d_E*log(p)/2`, which is `34*log109/35` for `m=3`.
It shifts both compared volumes equally.

## 5. What is, and is not, an admissible source identification

The strongest unconditionally checked assertion is the explicit finite
local noncontainment in section 4. Its interpretation as a dictionary
obstruction has the following precise assumptions: keep the same marked
local field, keep `v_p(p)=1`, retain the unpowered root in the specified
principal unit, and identify its hull directly with a region to which the
original powered-pilot container applies. These assumptions are mutually
incompatible on the displayed example.

This review does not find that either source paper proves that particular
identification. In particular:

* Joshi III (4.2.2.2), p. 33, explicitly scales label absolute values by
  `j^2` at the distinguished bad places. These are not automatically the
  common native valuation used in the test. A conversion from rescaled
  norms to native Haar volumes must keep the reciprocal scale factor.
* A target reset keeping every source class fixed differs from replacing
  the source parameter by a power. The fixed-source covariance argument
  in the reviewed report correctly handles the former. It cannot prove
  equality after an unstated change of input family.
* Joshi IV Remark 6.10.2, p. 66, explicitly warns against directly
  comparing various local quantities after its two arithmeticoids are
  normalized concurrently. The native local test does not override that
  warning or prove the missing comparison.
* Membership in the explicitly defined local native hull is not a proof
  of membership in either full globally admissible theta region. The
  corresponding global initial data and source labels must first exist.

The result should therefore be described as a counterexample to an
**unweighted direct native dictionary**, or simply as a local dictionary
test. It should not be described as a counterexample to an IUT estimate
on the actual family for which that estimate was formulated.

## 6. Global arithmetic passes in part; the proposed prime fails one actual window

The elementary level checks in the reviewed report are correct. Full
2-torsion is rational and

\[
 [L:\mathbb Q]\mid
 2\,|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)|\,|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_5)|
 =46080,
\]

so this Galois extension has degree prime to 7 and has no real embeddings.
At the good prime 17, `b=3 mod17`. Counting the three zero right-hand
sides and six nonzero squares gives `#C(F_17)=16`, trace 2, and
characteristic-polynomial discriminant `2^2-4*17=6 mod7`, a nonsquare.
The mod-7 representation over `Q` is therefore irreducible. At 109 the
Tate inertia action contains a nonidentity transvection because `7` does
not divide 72.

An irreducible subgroup of `GL_2(F_7)` with a transvection contains
`SL_2(F_7)`: an irreducibility argument produces a conjugate with a
different fixed line; in a basis of those lines the two nontrivial
unipotents are upper and lower elementary matrices. Their powers give
all coefficients in `F_7`, and elementary elimination generates `SL_2`.
The image over `L` is normal with index prime to 7, so it contains the
order-7 subgroup and its conjugates. The same conclusion holds over `L`.

However, one global condition is **false**, rather than merely unchecked,
for this chosen `ell=7`. Joshi IV Definition 5.4.1, p. 51, defines
`Q=Tate(C/L)` as a normalized sum of local Tate orders. The 109-part
alone is

\[
 \frac1{[L:\mathbb Q]}
 \sum_{w\mid109}72e_wf_w\log109
 =72\log109.
\]

All other terms are nonnegative. Hence `Q>=72 log109>49`. Theorem 5.7.1,
p. 53, requires its selected prime to satisfy `sqrt(Q)<=ell`. It follows
that 7 is not a prime selected by that theorem for this curve. The
hypothesis chain invoking Theorem 5.7.1 in Theorem 6.10.1, p. 66, and
Proposition 6.10.9 is therefore not instantiated by this example.

This last window is a condition of **Joshi IV 5.7.1 and its subsequent
application**. It must not be inserted into original Mochizuki IUT IV
Theorem 1.10, whose stated hypotheses instead invoke initial theta data
and the situation of IUT III Corollary 3.12. The original orbicurve,
core/cusp, selected-bad-place, and all-prime initial-data conditions have
not all been verified here. No global counterexample follows in either
source.

## 7. Required scope for reuse

The independent computation finds no error in `e=35`, the normalized
unit coefficients, or `180/35<598/35`. The report is safe to reuse with
the following boundaries kept explicit:

1. Name the local native source family and the coherent marking maps;
   do not silently promote it to a globally admissible source theta set.
2. State the three-slot interpretation and both normalizations.
3. Record that this `ell=7` fails the Joshi IV prime window, while not
   claiming that the same window is an assumption of original IUT IV 1.10.
4. Transfer no upper estimate across the unpowered/powered input change
   without a proved map and its volume effect.

The example is useful for rejecting this particular shortcut in a proof
attempt. It does not justify abandoning the IUT route, nor does it supply
an unconditional proof or disproof of ABC.
