# One-sixth valence barrier on the Legendre base

On `X(2) ~= P^1`, the Hodge line `L` has degree `1/2`. If a nonzero section of
`L^k` vanishes to orders `r_0,r_1,r_infinity` at the three cusps, then

\[
 r_0+r_1+r_\infty\le k/2.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \min\{r_0,r_1,r_\infty\}\le k/6.
\]

No scalar modular form can have a uniform cusp-order/Hodge-weight ratio
strictly greater than `1/6`. Equality forces no interior zeros and is realized,
in integral weights, by powers of the discriminant section (`2/12=1/6`).

Thus the abc coefficient is the scalar geometric optimum. Replacing the
Legendre discriminant by another scalar modular form cannot create coefficient
slack; a proof must establish a sharp arithmetic estimate at the boundary or
use a nonlinear/vector-valued construction.
