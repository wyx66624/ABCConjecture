# An arithmetic slope no-go theorem for the naive Steinberg packet argument

## 1. The proposed step

A previous form of the Steinberg sup-packet route considered a global packet
covector and proposed to bound the arithmetic degree of its quotient line only
by the maximal slope of the ambient adelic vector bundle.  This would turn the
local sup norm into an upper bound without separately estimating the height of
the packet morphism.

The following elementary example proves that this step is false in arithmetic
geometry.  The failure is not a technicality: the missing term is precisely
the projective height that the abc argument must control.

## 2. Standard hermitian lattice

Let

\[
 \overline E=(\mathbb Z^2,\|\cdot\|_2)
\]

with the standard Euclidean metric at the real place and the standard integral
lattice at every finite place.  Its arithmetic degree is zero and its maximal
slope is

\[
 \widehat\mu_{\max}(\overline E)=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed, every primitive integral vector `(a,b)` has Euclidean norm at least
one, so the corresponding saturated line has degree at most zero; the lines
generated by `(1,0)` and `(0,1)` have degree zero.

For an integer `N>=1`, define the primitive covector

\[
 f_N:\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 f_N(x,y)=Nx+y.
\tag{2.2}
\]

It is surjective because `f_N(0,1)=1`.  Give the quotient line `Q_N` the
quotient metric induced by the Euclidean metric on `E_R`.

## 3. Exact quotient norm

### Theorem 3.1

\[
 \|1\|_{Q_N}=\frac1{\sqrt{N^2+1}}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

The quotient norm is

\[
 \|1\|_{Q_N}
 =\inf\left\{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}:Nx+y=1,\ x,y\in\mathbb R\right\}.
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
 1=|Nx+y|
 \le\sqrt{N^2+1}\sqrt{x^2+y^2},
\]

so every lift has norm at least `1/sqrt(N^2+1)`.  Equality is attained at

\[
 (x,y)=\frac{(N,1)}{N^2+1}.
\]

This proves (3.1).

### Corollary 3.2

The quotient-line degree is

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat\deg(\overline Q_N)
 =\frac12\log(N^2+1).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

It is unbounded as `N` tends to infinity, whereas the ambient maximal slope is
identically zero.

Therefore no inequality of the form

\[
 \widehat\deg(\overline Q)
 \le\widehat\mu_{\max}(\overline E)+O(1)
\tag{3.3}
\]

can hold for arbitrary primitive quotient covectors with a constant
independent of the covector.

## 4. The dual subline does not repair the problem

The same coefficients define the vector

\[
 v_N=(N,1)\in E.
\]

The saturated subline that it generates has degree

\[
 -\frac12\log(N^2+1).
\]

The valid subline inequality

\[
 \widehat\deg(\mathbb Z v_N)
 \le\widehat\mu_{\max}(\overline E)=0
\]

therefore gives only a lower bound on the projective height
`(1/2) log(N^2+1)`, not the upper bound needed in the abc argument.

Thus switching between a packet vector and its dual covector changes the sign
or changes a subobject into a quotient, but does not eliminate the height of
the morphism.

## 5. Consequence for the Steinberg sup packet

Let `sigma_ell` be the vector or covector of cyclic-line theta/discriminant
coordinates.  Its local dual norm may indeed select the canonical Tate line,
and the finite scalar identities remain correct.  However, an arithmetic
slope inequality for the ambient permutation or Steinberg bundle must contain
one of the following additional terms:

1. the height of the packet morphism itself;
2. an explicit integral determinant/resultant controlling that morphism;
3. a special semistability theorem for the concrete modular-unit section;
4. an arithmetic intersection formula that directly evaluates its projective
   height.

Without such an input, the proposed maximal-slope step is circular: its
missing morphism-height term is exactly the nonlinear projective packet height
whose upper bound would imply abc.

This strictly excludes the **naive ambient-maximal-slope shortcut**.  It does
not exclude:

- the explicit Hecke-discriminant packet route, whose remaining target is
  stated directly as a modular-unit projective-height theorem;
- determinant-of-cohomology plus an independently proved norm bound;
- an IUT/ATS comparison that explicitly transports the packet norm and its
  Jacobian;
- a new arithmetic invariant-theory theorem bounding the packet morphism by
  conductor data.

## 6. Corrected target

The surviving theorem cannot merely state that a quotient line is bounded by
an ambient maximal slope.  It must prove, for the specific Hecke or theta
packet,

\[
 h_{\rm proj}(\sigma_\ell(E))
 \le
 \left(\frac{\ell-1}{2}+o(\ell)\right)
   (\operatorname{Diff}(E)+\operatorname{Cond}(E))
 +O(\ell\log\ell),
\]

by using the algebraic and metric structure of that packet.  The general
counterexample above shows why such structure is indispensable.

## 7. Formalization plan

A Lean formalization can be separated into elementary layers:

1. Cauchy--Schwarz for the covector `(N,1)`;
2. the exact infimum of `sqrt(x^2+y^2)` subject to `Nx+y=1`;
3. the logarithmic quotient-degree formula;
4. unboundedness in `N`;
5. an abstract statement that no uniform quotient-degree bound can follow from
   the ambient maximal slope alone.

No abc statement is assumed.
