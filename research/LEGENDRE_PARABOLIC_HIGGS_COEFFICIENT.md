# Exact one-sixth coefficient from the Legendre parabolic variation

For the Legendre family over

\[
 U=P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\},
\]

let `L` be the Hodge line. The parabolic Kodaira--Spencer map is maximal:

\[
 L^{\otimes2}\simeq\Omega^1_{P^1}(\log\{0,1,\infty\}),
\]

so `deg L=1/2`. The highest Hodge line in
`Sym^(ell-1) R^1 f_*` therefore has degree

\[
 \deg L^{\ell-1}=\frac{\ell-1}{2}.
\]

The canonical Tate `ell`-line has local Bernoulli/Neron q-coefficient

\[
 A_\ell=\frac{\ell-1}{12}.
\]

Hence the exact identity

\[
 \frac{\deg L^{\ell-1}}{A_\ell}=6
\]

recovers the required coefficient `1/6` from classical maximal-Higgs geometry.

The surviving arithmetic target is to construct the integral/parabolic metric
on this one globally labelled Hodge line so that its specialization has:

- finite boundary term `(ell-1)Q/12`;
- zero good-place defect;
- `O(log ell)` level-prime/Jacobian error;
- arithmetic degree at most `((ell-1)/2+o(ell))(Diff+Cond)`.

This would give

\[
 Q/6\le(1+o(1))(Diff+Cond)+O(\log\ell)
\]

and hence abc after quantifier-correct prime selection. The coefficient is now
closed; the arithmetic specialization/slope theorem remains the central
unproved statement.
