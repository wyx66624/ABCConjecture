## 2. Mathematical proof before formalization

Let

\[
 D=\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z[p],\qquad
 E=(\mathbb N\times\mathbb N)\to_0\mathbb Z,
\]

where the ordered-pair model `E` stores both orientations.  For `A,B in D`
write

\[
 (A\wedge B)_{p,q}=A_pB_q-A_qB_p.
\]

Let `S=D x D` be the set of divisor symbols and

\[
 C_0=\mathbb Z^{(S)}
\]

its free integral module.  If `[A,B]` denotes the basis vector of a symbol,
define the linear boundary

\[
 \partial:C_0\longrightarrow E,
 \qquad \partial[A,B]=A\wedge B.                       \tag{2.1}
\]

For `X,U,Y,V,Z in D`, put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 r(X,U,Y,V,Z)={}&[X,U]-[Y,V]+[Y-X,Z-X]\\
 &-[Y+U-X-V,Z-X-V]+[U-V,Z-V].                         \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (each generator is a boundary cycle)

\[
 \partial r(X,U,Y,V,Z)=0.                              \tag{2.3}
\]

**Proof.**  By (2.1), the left side is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X\wedge U-Y\wedge V\\
 &+(Y-X)\wedge(Z-X)\\
 &-(Y+U-X-V)\wedge(Z-X-V)\\
 +(U-V)\wedge(Z-V).
\end{aligned}
\]

Expand every wedge bilinearly.  The coefficients of
`X wedge U`, `X wedge Y`, `X wedge V`, `X wedge Z`,
`U wedge Y`, `U wedge V`, `U wedge Z`, `Y wedge V`,
`Y wedge Z`, and `V wedge Z` cancel pairwise.  Hence the sum is zero.
Equivalently, this is the earlier coefficientwise five-term theorem applied
at every ordered prime pair.  QED.

Let

\[
 R_5=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}
       \{r(X,U,Y,V,Z):X,U,Y,V,Z\in D\}\subseteq C_0.   \tag{2.4}
\]

### Theorem 2.2 (generated relation lies in the kernel)

\[
 R_5\subseteq\ker\partial.                              \tag{2.5}
\]

**Proof.**  The kernel of a linear map is a submodule.  Theorem 2.1 places
every member of the generating set (2.4) in this kernel.  The universal
property of the span gives (2.5).  QED.

Define

\[
 c\sim_5 d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c-d\in R_5.
\]

### Corollary 2.3 (generated equivalence preserves boundary)

If `c ~5 d`, then

\[
 \partial c=\partial d.                                \tag{2.6}
\]

**Proof.**  Theorem 2.2 gives `partial(c-d)=0`.  Linearity gives
`partial c-partial d=0`, which is (2.6).  QED.

This proves the induction over arbitrary finite sums, negatives, and integer
multiples at once.  It is stronger and less error-prone than proving only a
single move.

## 3. Actual positive cells and the smaller generated relation

For a positive reduced cell

\[
 P=(a,b,c),\qquad a+b=c,\quad \gcd(a,b)=1,
\]

put

\[
 \sigma(P)=[d(a)-d(c),\ d(b)-d(c)]\in S.               \tag{3.1}
\]

The boundary of its basis chain is the existing finite contact surface:

\[
 \partial[\sigma(P)]
 =(d(a)-d(c))\wedge(d(b)-d(c))=\Omega(P).              \tag{3.2}
\]

A **positive five-term realization** consists of five positive reduced
cells `P1,...,P5` and divisor data `X,U,Y,V,Z` for which their five symbols
are, individually,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P_1 &[X,U]\\
P_2 &[Y,V]\\
P_3 &[Y-X,Z-X]\\
P_4 &[Y+U-X-V,Z-X-V]\\
P_5 &[U-V,Z-V].
\end{array}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]

These are five local semantic identifications.  No signed boundary equality
is included in the data.

Let `R5+` be the integral span of the signed chains

\[
 [\sigma(P_1)]-[\sigma(P_2)]+[\sigma(P_3)]
 -[\sigma(P_4)]+[\sigma(P_5)]                          \tag{3.4}
\]

over every positive realization.

### Theorem 3.1 (actual positive generated relation)

\[
 R_5^+\subseteq R_5\subseteq\ker\partial.              \tag{3.5}
\]

**Proof.**  The identifications (3.3) turn (3.4) into the algebraic generator
(2.2), so every generator of `R5+` belongs to `R5`.  Taking spans proves the
first inclusion; Theorem 2.2 proves the second.  QED.

Thus a finite signed family of positive cells that is connected to a target
in the relation generated by actual positive moves automatically satisfies

\[
 \Omega(P_0)=\sum_j n_j\Omega(P_j).                    \tag{3.6}
\]

This is the missing input to the previously proved exact-boundary theorem.
Consequently the generated filling satisfies

\[
 \Phi(P_0)\le \frac12\left(
   \sum_j|n_j|\bigl(M(P_j)+V(P_j)\bigr)
   +\sum_j|n_j|R(P_j)\right).                          \tag{3.7}
\]

Equation (3.7) contains no added boundary premise: (3.6) was derived from
positive five-term generation.

## 4. A parameterized family of actual generators

Take positive integers

\[
 0<b<a<c                                                       \tag{4.1}
\]

and suppose the following five pairs are coprime:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(a,c-a),\quad (b,c-b),\quad (b,a-b),\\
 &(b(c-a),c(a-b)),\quad(c-a,a-b).                       \tag{4.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Define

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\text{left}&\text{middle}&\text{total}\\ \hline
P_1&a&c-a&c\\
P_2&b&c-b&c\\
P_3&b&a-b&a\\
P_4&b(c-a)&c(a-b)&a(c-b)\\
P_5&c-a&a-b&c-b.
\end{array}                                             \tag{4.3}
\]

The positivity of every entry follows from (4.1), and (4.2) makes every row
reduced.  The only nonlinear row sum is

\[
 b(c-a)+c(a-b)=bc-ab+ac-bc=a(c-b).                    \tag{4.4}
\]

The other four sums are immediate.

### Theorem 4.1 (common-denominator five-term realization)

The cells in (4.3) are a positive realization of the five-term relation for

\[
 x=\frac ac,\qquad y=\frac bc,                          \tag{4.5}
\]

whose five arguments are

\[
 \frac ac,\quad\frac bc,\quad\frac ba,
 \quad\frac{b(c-a)}{a(c-b)},\quad\frac{c-a}{c-b}.       \tag{4.6}
\]

**Proof.**  Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=d(a)-d(c),& U&=d(c-a)-d(c),\\
Y&=d(b)-d(c),& V&=d(c-b)-d(c),\\
Z&=d(a-b)-d(c).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{4.7}
\]

The first two rows of (3.3) follow from definition.  For `P3`, subtraction
of (4.7) gives

\[
Y-X=d(b)-d(a),\qquad Z-X=d(a-b)-d(a).
\]

For `P4`, use `d(mn)=d(m)+d(n)` to obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(b(c-a))-d(a(c-b))&=Y+U-X-V,\\
d(c(a-b))-d(a(c-b))&=Z-X-V.
\end{aligned}
\]

For `P5`, direct subtraction gives

\[
d(c-a)-d(c-b)=U-V,
\quad d(a-b)-d(c-b)=Z-V.
\]

These are precisely the five symbol identifications (3.3).  Theorem 3.1
then proves the actual surface relation.  QED.

This family is infinite.  For every odd `c>=3`, the choice

\[
 a=2,\qquad b=1                                           \tag{4.8}
\]

satisfies (4.2).  The only nontrivial checks reduce to

\[
 \gcd(2,c-2)=1,
 \qquad \gcd(c-2,c)=\gcd(c-2,2)=1,
\]

because `c-2` is odd.  The Lean constructor
`oddDenominatorFiveTermData` formalizes this parameterized subfamily.

## 5. Counterexample pressure test and the corrected statement

There is a tempting but false shortcut:

> **False child statement.** A five-term generator is already zero in the
> free symbol chain.

Let

\[
 \epsilon:C_0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
 \qquad \epsilon\left(\sum_s n_s[s]\right)=\sum_s n_s
\]

be the augmentation.  For every five-term generator,

\[
 \epsilon(r)=1-1+1-1+1=1.                              \tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{r(X,U,Y,V,Z)\ne0\quad\text{for every input}.}  \tag{5.2}
\]

Together with Theorem 2.1, this also proves that `partial` is not injective.
The exact shortcut is retired by a full-premise proof of its negation.  The
parent route is retained: the correct construction is the generated
submodule or its quotient, followed by the boundary map.

The failure is not an artifact of degenerate divisor inputs.  The positive
reduced choice

\[
 x=\frac12,\qquad y=\frac16                              \tag{5.3}
\]

has five distinct arguments

\[
 \frac12,\quad\frac16,\quad\frac13,\quad\frac15,
 \quad\frac35.                                          \tag{5.4}
\]

Their cell triples are

\[
 (1,1,2),\ (1,5,6),\ (1,2,3),\ (1,4,5),\ (3,2,5).
\]

Lean proves both that their signed surface boundary is zero and that their
formal signed chain is nonzero; its coefficient at the `1/2` symbol is one.
Hence the stronger positive-domain version of the false child statement is
also refuted with every premise present.

The finite search
`research/computation/2026_09_03_steinberg_five_term_boundary_bridge/`
supplies an independent pressure test:

* all `59,049` five-tuples of two-coordinate divisor vectors in
  `{-1,0,1}^2` had zero signed boundary and nonzero formal chain;
* `55,392` had five pairwise-distinct symbols;
* `5,784` had pairwise-distinct symbols and five individually nonzero wedge
  boundaries;
* among `8,001` ordered positive rational pairs of denominator at most `20`,
  `7,858` produced five distinct arguments, and (5.3) was the smallest by
  the frozen denominator score;
* for `c<=100`, the common-denominator scan found `29,691` reduced data and
  `28,170` with five distinct arguments.

These counts are finite computational evidence only.  Equations
(2.3), (2.5), (3.5), (4.6), and (5.2) are proved universally in Lean.

## 7. Refined S-I4C ledger

The former single label S-I4C should now be split.

* **S-I4C-G (generator boundary): CLOSED.**  Both the algebraic five-term
  span and the smaller span generated by actual positive realizations lie
  in the kernel of the finite exterior boundary.
* **S-I4C-C (chain conversion): CLOSED.**  Positive generated equivalence
  produces the exact boundary required by `FiniteRationalCellChain` and
  therefore the calibrated triangle inequality.
* **S-I4C-R (rational realization): PARTIAL.**  A general certified
  realization interface, a common-denominator family, every odd-denominator
  subfamily with `(a,b)=(2,1)`, and a five-distinct concrete move are Lean
  closed.  A canonical Lean normalizer for arbitrary unequal denominators
  has not yet been supplied.
* **S-I4C-E (filling existence): OPEN.**  No theorem constructs, for an
  arbitrary abc target, a finite sequence of positive moves ending in a
  family with favorable costs.
* **Gate VF analytic control: OPEN.**  For
  `Q(Gamma)=sum_j |n_j|(mixedCost_j+coherentCost_j)` and
  `R(Gamma)=sum_j |n_j| residualCost_j`, the exact missing statement is:
  for every `epsilon>0`, find constants `K_epsilon,L_epsilon` and, for every
  primitive abc cell of height `H=log c` and radical logarithm
  `rho=log rad(abc)`, construct a positively generated filling `Gamma` with

  \[
  \mathcal R(\Gamma)\le\epsilon\mathcal Q(\Gamma)+K_\epsilon H,
  \qquad
  \mathcal Q(\Gamma)\le 2H\rho+L_\epsilon H.
  \]

  Neither estimate is proved.  There is also no control yet on coefficient
  growth, support growth, or chain length from which they could be derived.

Accordingly this checkpoint does not prove or disprove the abc conjecture.
It removes a genuine algebraic gap between the five-term identity and the
existing exact-boundary estimate while leaving the hard existence and
analytic gates visible.
