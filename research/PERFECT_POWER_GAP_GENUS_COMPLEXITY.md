# Genus complexity forced by a sub-threshold perfect-power gap

Let a family on a smooth projective curve of genus `g` satisfy

\[
 X^m+A=Y^n.
\]

Let `D` be the dominant height degree and suppose function-field abc gives

\[
 h(A)
 \ge
 \alpha D-G,
 \qquad
 \alpha=1-\frac1m-\frac1n,
\]

where `G` is the geometric error, equal to `2g-2` up to the chosen divisor
normalization.  If the family is required to satisfy

\[
 h(A)\le\theta D
\]

with `theta<alpha`, then

\[
 \boxed{(\alpha-\theta)D\le G.}
\]

Thus a fixed saving `delta=alpha-theta>0` below the generalized
perfect-power threshold forces

\[
 g\ge \frac{\delta}{2}D+O(1).
\]

For square--cube gaps, `alpha=1/6`.  Any algebraic construction with a fixed
saving below `1/6` must therefore live on parameter curves whose genus grows
linearly with the degree/height scale.  Fixed curves, bounded-genus towers and
low-complexity polynomial or rational parametrizations are excluded.

This does not rule out isolated integer points, analytic constructions or
families on rapidly growing-genus curves.  It converts those surviving routes
into a quantitative complexity problem rather than merely observing that no
simple parametrization is known.
