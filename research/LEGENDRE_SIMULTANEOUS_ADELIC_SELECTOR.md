# The exact simultaneous adelic selector for the three Legendre directions

## 1. Statement

Let

\[
 a+b=c,
 \qquad
 \gcd(a,b)=1,
\]

with `a,b>0`.  In the rank-two lattice `Z^2`, consider the three globally
labelled lines

\[
 L_a=\langle(1,0)\rangle,
 \qquad
 L_b=\langle(0,1)\rangle,
 \qquad
 L_c=\langle(1,1)\rangle.
\]

Define

\[
 \boxed{v_{a,b}=(b,-a).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (simultaneous full-depth selection)

The vector `v_(a,b)` satisfies all three projective congruences at their full
arithmetic depths:

\[
 v_{a,b}\equiv b(1,0)\pmod a,
\tag{1.2}
\]

\[
 v_{a,b}\equiv -a(0,1)\pmod b,
\tag{1.3}
\]

\[
 v_{a,b}\equiv -a(1,1)\pmod c.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Equivalently, for every prime power `p^m` exactly dividing the corresponding
boundary integer, the reduction of `v_(a,b)` lies in the associated Legendre
boundary line modulo `p^m`.

#### Proof

The second coordinate of `(b,-a)` is divisible by `a`, proving (1.2).  The
first coordinate is divisible by `b`, proving (1.3).  Finally

\[
 b-(-a)=a+b=c,
\]

so the two coordinates are congruent modulo `c`, proving (1.4).

### Theorem 1.2 (primitive and short)

The vector is primitive and has controlled archimedean size:

\[
 \gcd(b,a)=1,
 \qquad
 \frac c2\le\|v_{a,b}\|_\infty<c.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The lower bound follows because `max(a,b)>=c/2`; the upper bound follows from
`a,b<c`.

## 2. Exact congruence lattice

Let

\[
 \Lambda_{a,b,c}
 =\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:
   a\mid y,\ b\mid x,\ c\mid x-y\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (explicit basis)

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_{a,b,c}
 =\mathbb Z(b,-a)+\mathbb Z(0,ac).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 [\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda_{a,b,c}]=abc,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and the Smith invariants are `1,abc`.

#### Proof

Write `x=bX`, `y=aY`.  The third congruence gives

\[
 bX\equiv aY\pmod c.
\]

Since `b congruent -a mod c` and `gcd(a,c)=1`, this is equivalent to

\[
 X+Y\equiv0\pmod c.
\]

Thus `Y=-X+cS`, and

\[
 (x,y)=X(b,-a)+S(0,ac).
\]

The determinant of the displayed basis is `abc`.  Since the gcd of all four
basis entries is one, the first Smith invariant is one.

## 3. Why this evades the generic projective-dimension barrier

The earlier CRT/Minkowski selector treated the local target lines as arbitrary
and recovered only a `1/n` fraction of their total depths in rank `n`.  The
vector (1.1) recovers **all** three depths simultaneously.  It can do so only
because the local lines and moduli are linked by the global additive equation

\[
 a+b=c.
\]

Thus the generic `1/(ell+1)` no-go theorem does not apply to the globally
labelled three-cusp Legendre configuration.

The vector also reveals a new issue.  It is not one of the three fixed lines
over `Q`; its reduction changes direction from prime to prime.  Therefore a
successful Hodge--Arakelov construction must admit one global rational vector
whose local reductions enter different parabolic lines.  This is exactly the
notion of a parabolic or adelic subline, rather than a fixed geometric torsion
subgroup.

## 4. Candidate arithmetic bundle

Use the congruence conditions (2.1) as local lattice modifications of the
trivial rank-two bundle.  The vector `v_(a,b)` defines a primitive global
subline meeting every local canonical boundary filtration at full depth.  Its
archimedean norm is only `O(c)`, while the determinant of the modified lattice
is `abc`.

The source-facing theorem is now concrete:

### Target theorem 4.1 (selector slope comparison)

Construct the metrized parabolic/Hodge bundle associated with the three
Legendre boundary filtrations so that the line generated by `(b,-a)` has finite
local contribution equal to the required Tate multiplicity term, and prove
that its archimedean and determinant corrections are bounded by

\[
 (1+o(1))\log\operatorname{rad}(abc)+O(\log\ell).
\]

Theorem 1.1 closes the adelic selection problem itself.  What remains is the
metric comparison that turns full congruence depth into the theta/Neron packet
norm without reintroducing the full height `log c`.

## 5. Lean formalization plan

The coordinate congruences, primitivity, sup-norm bounds and explicit lattice
basis are elementary integer statements.  They are formalized first.  The
parabolic vector-bundle interpretation remains a later arithmetic-geometry
layer and is not inserted as an axiom.
