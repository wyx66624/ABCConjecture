# An effective absolute index bound for the specified Pell packet

Author: ChatGPT. Date: 30 August 2026.

This note proves an absolute upper bound for the moving Chebyshev index in
the repository's specified Pell packet. Combined with the previously
checked mathematical specialization of Bérczes–Evertse–Győry, it gives an
absolute height bound, hence finiteness, for this packet. It does not reduce
all possible abc counterexamples to this packet, and does not prove or
disprove abc. The external approximation and integral-point theorems remain
mathematical inputs, not new Lean axioms.

The key change from the preceding note is to retain the normalized exponent
parameter in Matveev's original theorem. Replacing it by the largest raw
exponent would lose the absolute index bound.

## 1. Scope, without unnecessary squarefree or congruence hypotheses

Let positive integers \(b,r,s,X\), and an integer \(p\ge3\), satisfy

\[
 b+2=3r^2,\qquad b+3=s^2,\qquad
 Z:=b^2+3b+1=T_p(X),\qquad X>1.                         \tag{1}
\]

Here \(T_p\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Primality of
\(p\) is not needed for the estimates below. Neither squarefree endpoint
parameters nor residue conditions on them are needed.

Put

\[
 H=\log(b+2),\quad \varepsilon=2+\sqrt3,\quad
 \eta=s+r\sqrt3,\quad \delta=X+\sqrt{X^2-1},\quad
 W=Z+\sqrt{Z^2-1}.                                      \tag{2}
\]

The norm identities imply

\[
 \eta=\varepsilon^m\quad(m\ge1\text{ an integer}),
 \qquad W=\delta^p.                                     \tag{3}
\]

For the first identity, the full unit group of
\(\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\) is generated modulo sign by
\(2+\sqrt3\), and \(s,r>0\) select a positive power. For the second,
the Chebyshev identity gives
\(2Z=\delta^p+\delta^{-p}\), and \(\delta^p>1\) selects the positive
root of \(t^2-2Zt+1\). In particular, \(\delta\) is not assumed to be
a fundamental unit.

In fact \(b\ge22\) follows from (1). If \(1\le b<22\), then
\(s^2=b+3\) forces \(s=2,3,4\). The equation
\(s^2-3r^2=1\) leaves only \(s=2,r=1,b=1\). But then \(Z=5\),
whereas \(X\ge2,p\ge3\) give \(T_p(X)\ge T_2(2)=7\).
This is a contradiction. Thus all estimates below may use \(b\ge22\).

The two real quadratic fields generated by \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\)
have compositum \(K\subset\mathbb R\) of degree \(d\le4\). The fields
need not be distinct. The version of Matveev used below does not require
linear independence of the logarithms.

For comparison with the repository's additional packet data
\(b=Au^2,b+1=Bv^2,D=3AB\), the identity

\[
 Z^2-1=D(uvrs)^2
\]

and \(W=\delta^p\) show that
\(\mathbb Q(\delta)=\mathbb Q(W)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\).
Indeed a positive power of a nontrivial real quadratic norm-one unit
cannot be rational: its conjugate would be both the same rational number
and its reciprocal. This explains the field identification without
assuming that the Chebyshev root is fundamental.

## 2. The original approximation theorem and its constants

We use E. M. Matveev, *An explicit lower bound for a homogeneous rational
linear form in the logarithms of algebraic numbers. II*, Izvestiya:
Mathematics **64**(6) (2000), 1217–1269, DOI
[10.1070/IM2000v064n06ABEH000314](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM2000v064n06ABEH000314).
The original English PDF is available from
[Math-Net](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=im&option_lang=eng&paperid=314&what=fullteng).

His Corollary 2.3, with (1.3) and (2.4), has the following specialization
for a real number field \(K\) of degree \(d\). For real algebraic numbers
\(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in K\) all greater than one, integers \(b_i\), and a nonzero
linear form \(\Lambda=\sum b_i\log\alpha_i\), choose

\[
 A_i\ge\max\{d h(\alpha_i),|\log\alpha_i|,0.16\},\qquad
 B_*=\max\left(1,\max_i\frac{|b_i|A_i}{A_n}\right),
 \quad\Omega=\prod_i A_i.
\]

Then

\[
 \log|\Lambda|>
 -C_1(n,1)d^2\Omega\log(ed)\log(eB_*),
 \qquad C_1(n,1)\le2^{6n+20}.                            \tag{4}
\]

The normalized \(B_*\) is retained in this general corollary. It is not
restricted to strongly independent algebraic numbers. The fixed lower
bound \(0.16\) is included; no height lower bound is silently omitted.
The relevant definitions and statement are on printed pp. 1218–1219
(PDF pages 2–3). For our \(n=3\), \(C_1(3,1)\le2^{38}\).

The archived file is
`research/sources/continuation_2026_08_30/Matveev_2000_explicit_lower_bound.pdf`,
526,302 bytes, SHA-256
`e395e4698837950b683362558441e1e75298ad28cb0cc8cf260a556c93093574`.

## 3. A strict approximation with two fixed generators

**Lemma 1.** For the data (1)–(3),

\[
 1<\frac{\eta^4}{8W}<1+\frac2b,
 \qquad W>\eta^2,\qquad \log W<3H.                       \tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** Put \(f(t)=t+\sqrt{t^2-1}\) for \(t>1\). The elementary
inequality \(t-1<\sqrt{t^2-1}<t\) gives

\[
 2t-1<f(t)<2t.                                          \tag{6}
\]

From the two Pell equations,

\[
 \eta^2=f(2b+5),\qquad
 \eta^4=f(8b^2+40b+49),\qquad W=f(b^2+3b+1).
\]

Consequently

\[
 \eta^4>16b^2+80b+97>16b^2+48b+16>8W,
\]

and

\[
 \frac{\eta^4}{8W}-1
 <\frac{32b+90}{16b^2+48b+8}<\frac2b.
\]

The last inequality follows after multiplication by positive denominators
from \(32b^2+90b<32b^2+96b+16\).

Also \(W>2b^2+6b+1>4b+10>\eta^2\) for \(b\ge22\).
Finally \(W<2Z<2(b+2)^2\), so
\(\log W<\log2+2H<3H\). ∎

Define the nonzero, positive real linear form

\[
 \Lambda=4m\log\varepsilon-3\log2-p\log\delta
 =\log\frac{\eta^4}{8W}.                                \tag{7}
\]

By \(\log(1+t)<t\) for \(t>0\), Lemma 1 implies

\[
 0<\Lambda<2/b,\qquad -\log\Lambda>H/2.                  \tag{8}
\]

For the latter assertion, \(b\ge22\) gives
\((b/2)^2>b+2\), hence \(\log(b/2)>H/2\).

## 4. The normalized exponent is bounded by twice the index

Choose the ordered algebraic numbers and coefficients

\[
 (\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)=(\varepsilon,2,\delta),
 \qquad (b_1,b_2,b_3)=(4m,-3,-p),
\]

and

\[
 A_1=2\log\varepsilon,\quad A_2=4\log2,\quad
 A_3=2\log\delta.                                       \tag{9}
\]

The exact global heights are
\(h(\varepsilon)=\tfrac12\log\varepsilon\),
\(h(2)=\log2\), and
\(h(\delta)=\tfrac12\log\delta\).
The conjugates of both quadratic units are their positive reciprocals;
their minimal polynomials are monic with constant coefficient one.
Thus (9) satisfies every height condition in (4), since \(d\le4\),
\(\delta\ge2+\sqrt3\), and all three \(A_i\) exceed \(0.16\).

The normalized exponent terms are

\[
 \frac{4mA_1}{A_3}
 =\frac{4\log\eta}{\log\delta}
 =\frac{4p\log\eta}{\log W}<2p,
\]

\[
 \frac{3A_2}{A_3}=\frac{6\log2}{\log\delta}<4\le2p,
 \qquad \frac{pA_3}{A_3}=p.
\]

The first strict bound uses \(W>\eta^2\). For the second,
\(\delta^2\ge(2+\sqrt3)^2>8\), so
\(3\log2<2\log\delta\). Therefore

\[
 B_*\le2p.                                               \tag{10}
\]

The product of heights is exactly

\[
 \Omega
 =16\log\varepsilon\log2\,\frac{\log W}{p}
 <48\log\varepsilon\log2\,\frac Hp
 <96\frac Hp,                                           \tag{11}
\]

where \(\log\varepsilon<2\) and \(\log2<1\).
The large raw coefficient \(m\), which is proportional to \(H\), has
disappeared from (10). This is the needed effect of the denominator
\(A_3\); dropping it would only give a bound proportional to \(\log H\).

## 5. An explicit absolute bound and finite height

**Theorem 2.** Every tuple (1) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\quad p<2^{59}.\quad}                             \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Substitute (10)–(11) into (4), use
\(C_1(3,1)\le2^{38}\), \(d^2\le16\),
\(\log(ed)\le\log(4e)<3\), and compare with (8). This gives

\[
 \frac H2<-
 \log\Lambda
 <2^{38}\cdot16\cdot3\cdot96\,
   \frac Hp\log(2ep).
\]

Since \(H,p>0\), cancellation gives

\[
 p<9\cdot2^{48}\log(2ep)<2^{52}\log(2ep).                \tag{13}
\]

Put \(t=p/2^{52}>0\). Then

\[
 t<53\log2+1+\log t.
\]

For every positive \(t\), \(\log t\le t/2\), as proved in the
preceding continuation note. Consequently

\[
 t<2(53\log2+1)<108<128.
\]

Multiplication by \(2^{52}\) proves (12). All constants are explicit and
independent of \(b,r,s,X,p\), their prime support, and their quadratic
fields. ∎

The numerical bound is deliberately coarse:
\(2^{59}=576460752303423488\). It is a finite mathematical upper bound,
not a proposed feasible prime-by-prime computation.

**Corollary 3.** Every tuple (1) satisfies

\[
 H\le\exp(4300p^5)
 <\exp(4300\cdot2^{295}),
\]

and in particular

\[
 \boxed{\quad b+2<\exp\!\bigl(\exp(4300\cdot2^{295})\bigr).\quad}
                                                               \tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** The previously recorded specialization of
Bérczes–Evertse–Győry, Theorem 2.2, applies to
\(4T_p(X)+5=(2b+3)^2\) for every integer \(p\ge3\).
The polynomial has degree \(p\), no repeated root, and coefficient
log-height at most \(4p\). It yields
\(H\le\exp(4300p^5)\); neither primality nor the endpoint parity
kernels enter its proof. Combining with (12) and exponentiating gives
(14). The full earlier derivation, including the source theorem's
parameters, is in Section 3 of
`research/ARITHMETIC_GEOMETRY_SESSION_2026_08_30.md`.
[Original Bérczes–Evertse–Győry theorem, p. 730](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1797/download/10_5486_PMD_2013_5748.pdf). ∎

There are only finitely many tuples (1): (14) bounds the positive integer
\(b\), its square identities determine \(r,s\), (12) bounds \(p\), and
\(T_p(X)=Z\) bounds \(X\), for example by \(X^p\le T_p(X)=Z\).
This provides a uniform result beyond proving finiteness separately for
each fixed index.

## 6. Consequence and remaining boundary in the original research program

Even indices can be excluded directly under the two fixed Pell
conditions, without introducing endpoint squarefree kernels. Suppose
\(Z=T_{2j}(X)\), and put \(Y=T_j(X)\). Then

\[
 Z+1=2Y^2=(b+1)(b+2)=3(b+1)r^2.
\]

But \(b+2\equiv0\pmod3\) implies \(3\nmid b+1\). The 3-adic
valuation of the right side is \(1+2v_3(r)\), whereas that of
\(2Y^2\) is \(2v_3(Y)\), a contradiction. All variables whose
valuations are used are nonzero.

For any positive solution of the fixed Pell conditions, let the
four-consecutive product unit be \(W=\varepsilon_D^k\), where
\(\varepsilon_D\) is the fundamental positive solution in the integral
Pell group \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt D]\) with norm one. Its first coordinate
\(X>1\) is an integer, and \(Z=T_k(X)\). The preceding argument forces
\(k\) odd. If \(k>1\), then \(k\ge3\) and the tuple (1) is obtained
directly with \(p=k\). Thus the product unit is fundamental outside
the finite, explicitly bounded set (14). This is a statement about the
positive integral Pell group; no identification with a generator of a
possibly larger maximal-order unit group is needed.

This does not yet yield the required radical inequality. A fundamental
moving Pell unit can have a regulator proportional to the point's
log-height while its field discriminant is polynomial in that log-height.
Neither (12) nor (14) supplies a lower bound of size \(b^{\theta}\)
for the moving endpoint kernel, or the necessary weighted contribution
from primes in the square bases. Nor is an unconditional reduction of
the full abc conjecture to tuples (1) asserted.
