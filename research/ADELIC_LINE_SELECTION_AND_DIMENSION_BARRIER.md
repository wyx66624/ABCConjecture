# Adelic line-selection theorem and dimension barrier

Let `n>=2`, let `S` be finite, and for each prime `p in S` choose a depth
`m_p` and a target coordinate `c_p`. Put `M=prod p^m_p` and

\[
 \Lambda=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:\ p^{m_p}\mid x_i
          \text{ whenever }i\ne c_p\}.
\]

Then `[Z^n:Lambda]=M^(n-1)`. Minkowski gives a nonzero `x in Lambda` with

\[
  \|x\|_\infty\le2M^{1-1/n}.
\]

After primitive normalization, the retained total projective congruence depth
is at least

\[
  \frac1n\log M-\log2.
\]

For the `n=ell+1` cyclic torsion lines, the canonical and noncanonical Tate
coefficients are

\[
 A_\ell=(\ell-1)/12,
 \qquad
 B_\ell=-(\ell-1)/(12\ell).
\]

The generic `1/(ell+1)` selector gain satisfies the exact identity

\[
 B_\ell+\frac{A_\ell-B_\ell}{\ell+1}=0.
\]

Thus a generic full-projective-orbit CRT/Minkowski selector cannot create a
positive q-coefficient. A surviving route must exploit additional structure:
the three globally labelled Legendre cusps before specialization, a nonlinear
discriminant/exterior-power invariant, or a sharper parabolic/Higgs-bundle
slope theorem.
