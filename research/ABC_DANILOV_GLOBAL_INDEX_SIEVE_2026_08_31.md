orbit.  It is not a search through a finite interval.  The conclusion is

\[
 t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad K_t\text{ squarefull}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 t\equiv
 122136955032565025967809449110840347537827
 \pmod{183205432548847538951714173666260521306741}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Consequently the first

\[

## 2. The orbit and the squarefull transfer

Work in the quadratic ring generated by \(\sqrt5\).  Put

\[
 \alpha _0=682+305\sqrt5,
 \qquad
 \eta=1730726404001+774004377960\sqrt5,
 \qquad
 \alpha_t=\alpha_0\eta^t=z_t+w_t\sqrt5.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
 N(\alpha_0)=-1,\qquad N(\eta)=1.
\]

This is the congruence-preserving Danilov orbit already proved primitive in
the preceding report.  Define

\[
 L_t=2z_t+11,\qquad K_t=\frac{27L_t}{125}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The orbit has \(z_t\equiv57\pmod {125}\), hence \(K_t\) is a positive
integer, and its normalized Hall point satisfies \(X_t^3+K_t=Y_t^2\).

### Lemma 2.1 (exact-valuation transfer)

Let \(p\) be a prime with \(p\nmid3375\).  If

\[
             p\mid L_t,\qquad p^2\nmid L_t,
\]

then \(v_p(K_t)=1\).  In particular \(K_t\) is not squarefull.

**Proof.**  Equation (2.2) is the integer identity

\[
                         125K_t=27L_t.
\]

Because \(p\nmid125\cdot27=3375\), taking the \(p\)-adic valuation gives
\(v_p(K_t)=v_p(L_t)=1\).  A squarefull positive integer has valuation at
least two at each of its prime divisors.  \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2 (nilpotent linearization)

In \((\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)[\sqrt5]\), for all integers \(d\) and all
\(r\ge0\),

\[
                    (1+pd\sqrt5)^r=1+rpd\sqrt5.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

**Proof.**  In the binomial expansion, every term of degree at least two is
divisible by \(p^2\).  \(\square\)

## 3. The first two forced digits

Modulo \(11^2=121\), the exact residues are

\[
 \eta\equiv1+44\sqrt5,\qquad
 \alpha_0\equiv77+63\sqrt5.
\]

Since \((44\sqrt5)^2\equiv0\pmod {121}\), Lemma 2.2 gives

\[
 z_t\equiv77+5\cdot63\cdot44t
     \equiv77+66t\pmod {121}.
\]

Therefore

\[
                     L_t\equiv11(t+4)\pmod {121}.       \tag{3.1}
\]

If \(K_t\) is squarefull, Lemma 2.1 and (3.1) force

\[
                         t=7+11s                       \tag{3.2}
\]

for some \(s\ge0\).

The next lift is modulo \(89^2=7921\).  Repeated squaring gives

\[
 \eta^{11}\equiv1+89\cdot41\sqrt5,\qquad
 \alpha_7\equiv39+3785\sqrt5\pmod {7921}.
\]

Here \(3785\equiv47\pmod {89}\), and hence, using Lemma 2.2,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_{7+11s}
  &\equiv 89\bigl(1+(10\cdot47\cdot41)s\bigr)\\
  &\equiv89(1+46s)\pmod {89^2}.                       \tag{3.3}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The inverse of \(46\) modulo \(89\) is \(60\).  Squarefullness and Lemma
2.1 therefore force \(s\equiv29\pmod {89}\).  Thus

\[
                         t=326+979r                    \tag{3.4}
\]

with \(r\ge0\).

## 5. CRT closure of the finite sieve

The ten moduli are pairwise coprime.  Applying the Chinese remainder theorem
to the ten residues \(\rho\) gives

\[
 r\equiv
 124756848858595532142808426058059599119
 \pmod{187135273287893298214212639087089398679}. \tag{5.1}
\]

Substituting (5.1) into \(t=326+979r\) yields exactly (1.1):

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_0&=122136955032565025967809449110840347537827,\\
Q&=183205432548847538951714173666260521306741,\\
K_t\text{ squarefull}&\Longrightarrow t\equiv T_0\pmod Q.
\end{aligned}
\]

The CRT representative in (5.1) lies strictly between zero and its modulus.
Therefore a nonnegative \(r\) satisfying (5.1) is at least that
representative, and every squarefull index satisfies \(t\ge T_0\).
This proves (1.1).
