balancing-Wieferich primes of rank `ell`, two in each channel, and at least
one prime in each channel has first-occurrence valuation at least three.  No
such packet is known.

This continuation obtains four rigorous advances.

1. It proves the exact order-stagnation formula

   z(q^k)=z(q)q^{\max(0,k-e(q))}\qquad(k\ge1)               \tag{0.1}

2. It proves a new first-order coupling between all prime factors in the two

3. It audits and repairs the Section 8 argument of Fellini--Murty, keeps its

   > Either there are infinitely many rational balancing primes `q` with

## 1. Three units and the exact prime-ideal dictionary

Put

\[
\delta=1+\sqrt2,\qquad
\alpha=\delta^2=3+2\sqrt2,\qquad
\gamma=\alpha^2=17+12\sqrt2.
\]

Write

\[
\delta^n=A_n+B_n\sqrt2.
\]

The inherited identities are

\[
A_n^2-2B_n^2=(-1)^n,\qquad \gcd(A_n,B_n)=1,
\qquad u_n=A_nB_n.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

The use of `gamma`, rather than `alpha`, is important for matching the rank
of apparition exactly.

### Proposition 1.1 (geometric cyclotomic model)

For every `n>=1`,

\[
u_n=\alpha^{1-n}\frac{\gamma^n-1}{\gamma-1}.             \tag{1.2}
\]

If `ell` is prime, the principal ideal generated by `u_ell` is therefore,
up to a unit,

\[
(u_\ell)=(\Phi_\ell(\gamma)).                            \tag{1.3}
\]

**Proof.**  Since `gamma=alpha^2` and
`gamma-1=alpha(alpha-alpha^{-1})`,

\[
\alpha^{1-n}\frac{\gamma^n-1}{\gamma-1}
=\frac{\alpha^n-\alpha^{-n}}{\alpha-\alpha^{-1}}=u_n.
\]

For prime `ell`, the geometric quotient is `Phi_ell(gamma)`.  The factor
`alpha^(1-ell)` is a unit.  This proves both claims. \(\square\)

Let `K=Q(sqrt(2))`, let `O_K=Z[sqrt(2)]`, and let `q` be an odd rational
prime.  For a prime ideal `mathfrak q` above `q`, define

\[
f_\gamma(\mathfrak q)=\operatorname{ord}_{(O_K/\mathfrak q)^\times}(\gamma),
\]

and

\[
\delta_\gamma(\mathfrak q)
=v_{\mathfrak q}\!\left(\gamma^{f_\gamma(\mathfrak q)}-1\right). \tag{1.4}
\]

Recall that `z(q)` is the least positive `n` with `q|u_n`, and
`e(q)=v_q(u_{z(q)})`.

### Proposition 1.2 (source-typed dictionary)

For every odd rational prime `q` and every prime ideal `mathfrak q|q`,

\[
f_\gamma(\mathfrak q)=z(q),\qquad
\delta_\gamma(\mathfrak q)=e(q).                          \tag{1.5}
\]

These quantities are the same for the two prime ideals above a split
rational prime.

**Proof.**  The discriminant of `K` is `8`, so every odd `q` is unramified.
Moreover,

\[
N_{K/\mathbf Q}(\gamma-1)=-32,                            \tag{1.6}
\]

so `gamma-1` is a unit at every `mathfrak q|q`.  Equation (1.2), and the
fact that `alpha` is a global unit, give

\[
\mathfrak q\mid(u_n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gamma^n\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak q}.                       \tag{1.7}
\]

Because `u_n` is a rational integer, `mathfrak q|(u_n)` is equivalent to
`q|u_n`: contraction of `mathfrak q` to `Z` is `(q)`.  Taking the least
positive `n` in (1.7) proves `f_gamma(mathfrak q)=z(q)`.

Rearranging (1.2) at `z=z(q)` gives

\[
\gamma^z-1=\alpha^{z-1}(\gamma-1)u_z.                    \tag{1.8}
\]

The two algebraic factors preceding `u_z` are `mathfrak q`-adic units.
Since the ramification index over an odd `q` is one, the valuation of the
rational integer `u_z` at `mathfrak q` is exactly `v_q(u_z)`.  This proves
the second equality.  Conjugation sends `gamma` to `gamma^{-1}` and preserves
the valuation of `gamma^z-1`, proving the last assertion in the split case.
\(\square\)

For odd `n`, two further identities retain the `A/B` channel sign:

\[
\alpha^n-1=2A_n\delta^n,
\qquad
\alpha^n+1=2\sqrt2 B_n\delta^n.                           \tag{1.9}
\]

Indeed, expand `alpha^n=delta^(2n)=(A_n+B_n sqrt(2))^2` and use
`A_n^2-2B_n^2=-1`.  All other factors in (1.9) are odd-prime units.  Hence,
for an odd prime `q`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
q^k\mid A_n&\Longleftrightarrow
              \alpha^n\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak q^k},\\
q^k\mid B_n&\Longleftrightarrow
              \alpha^n\equiv-1\pmod{\mathfrak q^k}.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{1.10}
\]

This is the exact higher-power channel test.

## 2. First occurrence equals order stagnation at every prime power

For an integer `M>=1` coprime to the recurrence parameter, write `z(M)` for
the least positive `n` such that `M|u_n`.  The case needed below always
exists by the order interpretation in Proposition 1.2.

### Lemma 2.1 (local order lifting)

Let `R` be the valuation ring of an unramified finite extension of `Q_q`,
where `q` is odd.  Let `x` be a unit, let `t` be its order modulo the maximal
ideal, and put

\[
e=v(x^t-1)\ge1.
\]

Then the order of `x` modulo the `k`-th power of the maximal ideal is

\[
tq^{\max(0,k-e)}.                                         \tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.**  If `x^m=1` modulo the maximal ideal, then `t|m`; write `m=th`.
For `y=x^t`, the binomial expansion in the unramified discrete valuation
ring gives the odd-prime lifting identity

\[
v(y^h-1)=v(y-1)+v_q(h)=e+v_q(h).                          \tag{2.2}
\]

For completeness, if `q` does not divide `h`, the first binomial term
`h(y-1)` has valuation `e` and every later term has larger valuation.  If
`h=q`, the first term has valuation `e+1`; for `j>=2`, the term
`binom(q,j)(y-1)^j` has valuation at least `1+2e>e+1`, and the last term has
valuation `qe>e+1`.  Iteration handles powers of `q`, and the coprime part
does not change the valuation.  Thus `x^m=1 mod mathfrak q^k` exactly when
`v_q(h)>=max(0,k-e)`.  The least such positive `h` is the claimed power of
`q`. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (rank-stagnation tower)

For every odd rational prime `q` and every `k>=1`,

\[
\boxed{z(q^k)=z(q)q^{\max(0,k-e(q))}.}                    \tag{2.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
e(q)\ge2\Longleftrightarrow z(q^2)=z(q),\qquad
e(q)\ge3\Longleftrightarrow z(q^3)=z(q).                 \tag{2.4}
\]

**Proof.**  Apply Lemma 2.1 to `x=gamma` in the completion of `K` at any
`mathfrak q|q`, and use Proposition 1.2.  Equation (1.2) says that
`q^k|u_n` exactly when `gamma^n=1 mod mathfrak q^k`; unramifiedness makes
rational and prime-ideal exponents agree.  Therefore the least such `n` is
the order in (2.1), proving (2.3).  The equivalences (2.4) are immediate.
\(\square\)

At an odd prime index `ell`, every support prime has `z(q)=ell`.  Equations
(1.9)--(1.10) give the channel refinement:

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q^k}(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
\ell q^{\max(0,k-e(q))},&q\mid A_\ell,\\
2\ell q^{\max(0,k-e(q))},&q\mid B_\ell.
\end{cases}                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

Thus a hypothetical squarefull packet consists of four distinct local
orders which do not grow from level one to level two, while one in each
channel also fails to grow from level two to level three.  This is stronger
information than a residual order condition, but it is not a contradiction.

## 3. A coupled first-order congruence for the two channels

Let `ell` be an odd prime and factor

\[
A_\ell=\prod_{q\mid A_\ell}q^{a_q},\qquad
B_\ell=\prod_{r\mid B_\ell}r^{b_r}.                       \tag{3.1}
\]

The channel theorem in the preceding report gives unique integers

\[
q=1+2\ell k_q\quad(q\mid A_\ell),                         \tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
r=s_r+2\ell h_r\quad(r\mid B_\ell),\qquad
s_r=\left(\frac2r\right)\in\{1,-1\}.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

Put `s_ell=(2/ell)`.

### Theorem 3.1 (two-channel quotient ledger)

Define the integers

\[
a=\frac{A_\ell-1}{2\ell},\qquad
b=\frac{s_\ell B_\ell-1}{2\ell}.                        \tag{3.4}
\]

Then

\[
\prod_{r\mid B_\ell}s_r^{b_r}=s_\ell,                   \tag{3.5}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&\equiv\sum_{q\mid A_\ell}a_qk_q\pmod{2\ell},\\
b&\equiv\sum_{r\mid B_\ell}b_rs_rh_r\pmod{2\ell}.     \tag{3.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

The two sums satisfy the coupled congruence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q\mid A_\ell}a_qk_q
\equiv
2\sum_{r\mid B_\ell}b_rs_rh_r\pmod\ell.}               \tag{3.7}
\]

More precisely, the quotient coordinates obey the exact integer identity

\[
a-2b+\ell(a^2-2b^2)=0.                                   \tag{3.8}
\]

**Proof.**  The congruences `A_ell=1 mod 2ell` and
`B_ell=s_ell mod 2ell` prove that (3.4) is integral.  Reducing the second
factorization in (3.1) modulo `2ell` gives

\[
B_\ell\equiv\prod s_r^{b_r}\pmod{2\ell}.
\]

Both sides to be compared in (3.5) belong to `{1,-1}`; two different signs
differ by `2`, which is not divisible by `2ell`.  Hence (3.5) follows.

Modulo `4ell^2`, the binomial theorem gives

\[
(1+2\ell k)^e\equiv1+2\ell ek,                           \tag{3.9}
\]

and, for `s=+/-1`,

\[
(s+2\ell h)^e
\equiv s^e(1+2\ell e s h).                               \tag{3.10}
\]

Multiplying (3.9) over the `A` factorization proves the first congruence in
(3.6).  Multiplying (3.10), then using (3.5), proves the second.

Finally, (3.4) gives

\[
A_\ell=1+2\ell a,\qquad
B_\ell=s_\ell(1+2\ell b).
\]

Substitution into `A_ell^2-2B_ell^2=-1`, cancellation, and division by
`4ell` give (3.8).  Reducing (3.8) modulo `ell` and inserting (3.6) proves
(3.7). \(\square\)

Under the squarefull alternative, the perfect-power classifications add
another exact condition:

\[
\gcd\{a_q:q\mid A_\ell\}=1,
\qquad
\gcd\{b_r:r\mid B_\ell\}=1.                             \tag{3.11}
\]

Indeed, a common divisor `d>=2` of every positive valuation would make the
corresponding factor a perfect `d`-th power.  Cohn excludes this for
`A_ell`; Cohn--Ljunggren excludes it for `B_ell` after the already handled
exception `ell=7`.  Conditions (3.7) and (3.11) are genuine global packet
constraints.  The free quotient variables `k_q,h_r` prevent a contradiction
from the present argument alone.

## 4. The 2026 Fellini--Murty theorem and a prime-rank consequence

This section distinguishes four objects that must not be conflated:
rational primes `q`, prime ideals `mathfrak q`, residue-field norms
`N(mathfrak q)`, and ranks `z(q)`.

### Imported theorem 4.1 (Fellini--Murty, exact printed quantifier)

Let `K` be an algebraic number field and let `x in O_K` be nonzero and not a
root of unity.  Call a prime ideal `mathfrak p` base-`x` super-Wieferich when

\[
x^{N(\mathfrak p)-1}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p^3}.
\]

If there are finitely many base-`x` super-Wieferich prime ideals, then there
are infinitely many base-`x` non-Wieferich prime ideals, meaning

\[
x^{N(\mathfrak p)-1}\not\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p^2}.
\]

This is Theorem 2.3, proved as Theorem 8.1, in Fellini--Murty (JNT 285,
2026).  It is unconditional and uses Siegel's theorem.  It neither assumes
nor proves abc.

The literal theorem does not say that the orders of the resulting prime
ideals are rational primes.  Its Section 8 proof yields that strengthening.

### Proposition 4.2 (repaired prime-order refinement of Section 8)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.1, if the set of base-`x`
super-Wieferich prime ideals is finite, then there are infinitely many pairs
`(ell, mathfrak p)` such that `ell` is an odd rational prime and

\[
 f_x(\mathfrak p)=\ell,\qquad \delta_x(\mathfrak p)=1.    \tag{4.1}
\]

Infinitely many distinct prime values `ell` occur.

**Proof.**  First relate first-occurrence depth to the Wieferich definition.
Let `mathfrak p` be a prime ideal not dividing `(x)`, let `p` be its residue
characteristic, put
`f=f_x(mathfrak p)` and write `N(mathfrak p)-1=fm`.  Since the norm is a
power of `p`, the integer `m` is prime to `p`.  Therefore

\[
 v_{\mathfrak p}(x^{N(\mathfrak p)-1}-1)
 =v_{\mathfrak p}(x^f-1)=\delta_x(\mathfrak p).           \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, depth one, depth two, and depth at least three are exactly the
non-Wieferich, Wieferich-but-not-super, and super-Wieferich cases.

Let `T` be the cofinite set of odd rational primes `ell` satisfying:

1. `ell` is not the order of any super-Wieferich prime ideal;
2. no prime ideal above `ell` divides `(x-1)`.

Each condition deletes only finitely many rational primes.  For
`ell in T`, put `C_ell=(Phi_ell(x))`.  For every prime ideal
`mathfrak p|C_ell`, `Phi_ell(0)=1` shows that `x` is a unit modulo
`mathfrak p`.  The identity

\[
 x^\ell-1=(x-1)\Phi_\ell(x)
\]

shows that its residual order divides the prime `ell`.  If the order were
one, then `x=1 mod mathfrak p` and

\[
 0=\Phi_\ell(x)=1+x+\cdots+x^{\ell-1}=\ell
   \pmod {\mathfrak p}.
\]

Thus the residue characteristic would be `ell`, and `mathfrak p` would be a
prime above `ell` dividing `(x-1)`, contrary to condition 2.  Hence

\[
 f_x(\mathfrak p)=\ell,
 \qquad
 v_{\mathfrak p}(C_\ell)
   =v_{\mathfrak p}(x^\ell-1)=\delta_x(\mathfrak p).      \tag{4.3}
\]

Since \(f_x(\mathfrak p)=\ell>1\), \(\mathfrak p\nmid(x-1)\); hence the
displayed factorization directly gives
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(C_\ell)=\delta_x(\mathfrak p)\).  This is precisely
Fellini--Murty Lemma 6.3 at the minimal order.
Condition 1 excludes every super-Wieferich factor.  Consequently every
factor of `C_ell` has exponent one or two, and every exponent-one factor has
prime order `ell`.

Suppose only finitely many prime-order depth-one ideals existed, say
`mathfrak p_1,...,mathfrak p_r`.  Unique factorization of ideals gives, for
every `ell in T`,

\[
 C_\ell=\mathfrak p_1^{\epsilon_{1,\ell}}\cdots
   \mathfrak p_r^{\epsilon_{r,\ell}}\mathfrak w_\ell^2,
 \qquad \epsilon_{i,\ell}\in\{0,1\}.                    \tag{4.4}
\]

Choose a finite localization `R=O_{K,S}` of class number one.  After
discarding the `mathfrak p_i` which become the unit ideal, write their
extensions as `(pi_i)` and write the extension of `mathfrak w_ell` as
`(omega_ell)`.  Equality of principal ideals in the PID supplies a unit
`eta_ell` with

\[
 x^\ell-1=(x-1)\eta_\ell
   \prod_i\pi_i^{\epsilon_{i,\ell}}\omega_\ell^2.         \tag{4.5}
\]

This remains valid when the localized `C_ell` is the unit ideal; its unit
generator is absorbed into `eta_ell`.  Write the exponents of a fixed basis
of `S`-units modulo two, and write `ell=3n_ell+beta_ell` with
`beta_ell in {0,1,2}`.  Equation (4.5) then gives an `S`-integral point

\[
 X_\ell=x^{n_\ell},\qquad
 Y_\ell=\omega_\ell\prod_j u_j^{a_{j,\ell}}
\]

on one of finitely many curves

\[
                    (x-1)A Y^2=B X^3-1,                 \tag{4.6}
\]

where `A` ranges over finitely many root-of-unity, unit-square-class, and
`pi_i` choices, while `B` is one of `1,x,x^2`.  These are nonsingular affine
genus-one curves: `x` is nonzero and not a root of unity, so in particular
`x!=1`, and every displayed coefficient is nonzero.

Distinct prime indices give distinct points on each fixed curve.  Indeed,
`B=x^beta` fixes `beta`, since `1,x,x^2` are distinct; equality of two
`X`-coordinates would give `x^{n-n'}=1`, hence `n=n'` and then equality of
the indices.  Thus infinitely many indices in `T` yield infinitely many
`S`-integral points on one of a finite family of genus-one curves,
contradicting Siegel's theorem.  This proves infinitely many pairs in (4.1).
For fixed `ell`, all their prime ideals divide the one nonzero ideal `C_ell`,
so only finitely many occur; therefore infinitely many distinct prime orders
occur. \(\square\)

#### Source-proof correction

The preceding proof retains the Section 8 localization-and-Siegel strategy
but deliberately avoids the printed Lemma 6.4 at prime indices.  Three source
issues must be recorded transparently.

* On printed p. 221, the second case writes
  `Phi_(p^i f)(x)=(x^(p^i f)-1)/(x^(p^(i-1)f)-1)`.  This is generally false
  for `f>1`: for `x=2,p=3,f=2,i=1`, the two sides are `Phi_6(2)=3` and
  `(2^6-1)/(2^2-1)=21`.  The correct identity when `p` does not divide `f`
  uses the quotient of `Phi_f(x^(p^i))` by
  `Phi_f(x^(p^(i-1)))`.
* The same binomial estimate includes the endpoint `k=p`, although
  `binom(p,p)=1`.  Separating the last term repairs the estimate because its
  remaining factor has valuation at least `p-1>=2`; only
  `2<=k<=p-1` uses divisibility of the binomial coefficient by `p`.
* Printed p. 226 contains the contradictory phrase that finiteness of the
  non-Wieferich set gives infinitely many non-Wieferich primes.  The proof
  above permits `omega_ell=1` and needs no prior nonunit claim, so it avoids
  that sentence.

These are counterexamples to displayed intermediate steps, not to the
repairable valuation conclusion or to Theorem 2.3.  Proposition 4.2 is
therefore a repaired new refinement, not an unmodified quotation.

We now specialize, taking `x=gamma=17+12sqrt(2)`.  It is an algebraic
integer, is nonzero, and is not a root of unity because its real value is
greater than one.

### Theorem 4.3 (rational balancing alternative)

At least one of the following holds:

1. infinitely many odd rational primes `q` satisfy `e(q)>=3`;
2. infinitely many odd prime indices `ell` admit an odd rational prime `q`
   with

   \[
   z(q)=\ell,\qquad e(q)=1,
   \qquad\text{hence}\qquad q\parallel u_\ell.            \tag{4.7}
   \]

**Proof.**  First suppose there are infinitely many base-`gamma`
super-Wieferich prime ideals.  Remove first the finitely many prime ideals
above the rational prime `2`; the remaining set is still infinite.  For a
prime ideal `mathfrak q|q` in this remaining set, its residue
field has size `q` or `q^2`.  Because `f_gamma(mathfrak q)` divides
`N(mathfrak q)-1`, the multiplier

\[
\frac{N(\mathfrak q)-1}{f_\gamma(\mathfrak q)}
\]

is not divisible by `q`.  The local lifting identity (2.2) therefore shows
that the super-Wieferich condition is equivalent to
`delta_gamma(mathfrak q)>=3`.  Proposition 1.2 turns this into `e(q)>=3`.
There are at most `[K:Q]=2` prime ideals above any rational `q`, so infinitely
many such prime ideals lie above infinitely many rational primes.  All of
these `q` are odd by the initial deletion.  This proves branch 1.

Otherwise the super-Wieferich prime-ideal set is finite.  Proposition 4.2
gives infinitely many `mathfrak q` with prime
`f_gamma(mathfrak q)=ell` and `delta_gamma(mathfrak q)=1`.  Discard first
the finitely many output ideals above `2`.  For each remaining `mathfrak q`,
let `q` be the rational prime below it.  Again at most two prime ideals lie above
one `q`, so the infinitely many prime ideals yield infinitely many rational
`q`.  Proposition 1.2 gives

\[
z(q)=f_\gamma(\mathfrak q)=\ell,
\qquad e(q)=\delta_\gamma(\mathfrak q)=1.
\]

For each fixed `ell`, the nonzero principal ideal
`(Phi_ell(gamma))=(u_ell)` has only finitely many prime-ideal divisors.
Consequently infinitely many such `mathfrak q` also force infinitely many
distinct prime indices `ell`.  Finally `e(q)=1` at rank `ell` is exactly
`q||u_ell`.  This proves branch 2. \(\square\)

The theorem is useful but does not decide every prime index.  In branch 1 it
does not assert that the depth-three ranks are prime, that two primes share a
rank, or that they occupy opposite channels.  In branch 2 it supplies
infinitely many nonsquarefull prime-index terms, rather than all of them.
No stronger conclusion is inferred.

## 5. Three exact submechanisms closed by counterexamples

### 5.1 A simple cyclotomic root does not imply a simple integer divisor

Let `F_n(X)=U_n(X,1)` and let `V_n(X,1)` be the companion Lucas polynomial.
The polynomial identities

\[
(X^2-4)F_n'(X)=nV_n(X)-XF_n(X),                           \tag{5.1}
\]

\[
V_n(X)^2-(X^2-4)F_n(X)^2=4                               \tag{5.2}
\]

follow directly from the two characteristic roots, or by induction.

If `ell,q` are odd primes and `q|F_ell(6)=u_ell`, then `q!=ell` and
(5.1)--(5.2) give

\[
32F_\ell'(6)\equiv\ell V_\ell(6)\not\equiv0\pmod q.      \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every prime divisor at a prime index is a simple root of the Lucas
polynomial as a polynomial in its parameter.  Nevertheless,

\[
F_7(X)=X^6-5X^4+6X^2-1,
\]

\[
F_7(6)=40391=13^2\cdot239,
\qquad F_7'(6)=42408\equiv2\pmod {13}.                    \tag{5.4}
\]

So the fully stated implication

```text
q | F_ell(6) and F'_ell(6) != 0 mod q  =>  q^2 does not divide F_ell(6)
```

is false.  Hensel simplicity says that the residual root has a unique lift
as the parameter varies; it does not say that the fixed integer parameter
`6` misses that lift.

The norm version loses still more information.  From (1.9), for odd `ell`,

\[
N(\alpha^\ell-1)=-4A_\ell^2,
\qquad N(\alpha^\ell+1)=8B_\ell^2.                        \tag{5.5}
\]

Every rational valuation is doubled by these norms.  For example
`239||A_7`, although `239` occurs to exponent two in the first norm.
Therefore norm-square multiplicity cannot distinguish exponent one from
exponent two in the rational channel factor.

### 5.2 Primitive does not mean simple

The prime `13` has rank `7`, does not divide the discriminant `32`, and is a
primitive divisor of `u_7`; yet `13^2||u_7`.  Hence the claim

```text
every primitive divisor of a prime-index Lucas term has valuation one
```

is false.  The Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier theorem supplies primitive divisors; its
statement contains no exponent-one conclusion.  The primitive-divisor route
remains relevant only if supplemented by a genuinely new valuation theorem.

### 5.3 Neither channel nor the minimal residue class forces simplicity

The exact certificates give

\[
13=2\cdot7-1,\quad z(13)=7,\quad 13^2\parallel B_7,       \tag{5.6}

and

\[
1546463=2\cdot773231+1,\quad z(1546463)=773231,

Only these three precise mechanisms are closed.  A derivative argument with

## 6. Exhaustive depth-three search through one hundred million

For each odd prime `3<=q<=100,000,000`, the producer computes

\[
u_{q-(2/q)}\pmod {q^2}                                   \tag{6.1}
\]

using the exact doubling identities

\[
U_{2n}=U_n(2U_{n+1}-6U_n),\qquad
U_{2n+1}=U_{n+1}^2-U_n^2.                                \tag{6.2}
\]

Only a `q^2` hit requires a second computation modulo `q^3`.  This design
keeps the exhaustive stage in 64-bit residues with 128-bit products;
overflow-free 128-bit multiplication is needed only for the three hits.  An
independent Python implementation regenerates the Eratosthenes sieve and
replays all primes with arbitrary-precision integers.  It also independently
recomputes every hit's rank and `A/B` channel.

## 7. What fixed Chebotarev can and cannot see

For a fixed `ell`, the condition `q|u_ell` says that `q` divides the fixed
nonzero algebraic integer `Phi_ell(gamma)`.  Only finitely many rational
primes do so.  A standard Chebotarev theorem distributes unramified primes
in a fixed extension; it cannot select a desired valuation among the finite
prime divisors of one prescribed value.

The depth conditions are diagonal:

\[
\gamma^{z(q)}\equiv1\pmod {\mathfrak q^2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\pmod {\mathfrak q^3},                                   \tag{7.1}
\]

while `mathfrak q` itself varies.  A field obtained by adjoining
`q`-th roots also varies with `q` and is ramified at the very prime whose
depth is being tested.  Ordinary fixed, unramified Chebotarev therefore
does not prove the missing exponent-one divisor.

Fellini--Murty avoid this fixed-field obstruction.  They vary the prime
cyclotomic index and use ideal factorization plus Siegel finiteness.  Their
method produces the global alternative of Theorem 4.3, but its first branch
does not control rank or channel and its second branch is not pointwise in
`ell`.  A uniformly controlled ray-class tower or a new reciprocity law
could still add the missing pointwise information.  No counterexample to
such a precisely formulated stronger theorem is known, so that route also
remains active.

## 9. Remaining live theorem and counterexample targets

A sufficient pointwise theorem is now especially sharp:

> **Opposite-channel depth-three exclusion.** For every odd prime `ell`,
> there do not exist rational primes `p_A|A_ell` and `p_B|B_ell` with
> `e(p_A)>=3`, `e(p_B)>=3`, and
> `z(p_A)=z(p_B)=ell`.

The preceding packet theorem shows that this statement would imply that
every prime-index `u_ell` has an exponent-one prime divisor.  Largest-prime
descent would then exclude every nonunit squarefull balancing number.  The
statement has not been proved, and the finite scan through `10^8` is not a
proof of it.  No counterexample to its full quantifier is known, so it is
not abandoned.

The counterexample direction must do more than find one
balancing-Wieferich prime.  At one prime rank it must simultaneously build

* at least two repeated primes in the `A` channel;
* at least two repeated primes in the `B` channel;
* one depth-three prime in each channel, now necessarily greater than
  `10^8`;
* exponent vectors of gcd one in both channels;
* the residue, sign, and coupled quotient constraints (3.5)--(3.8).

No such packet, and no squarefull prime-index term, was found.  A finite
packet would still be only one exceptional abc point; a rigorous disproof of
standard abc needs an unbounded family.  Both proof and counterexample
routes therefore remain open at the exact boundary stated here.
