\begin{abstract}
We report a multi-route investigation of the $abc$ conjecture which treats
proof and disproof mechanisms symmetrically and does not assume the validity of
any claimed proof.  On the proof side we construct explicit cyclic kernel
sections, isolate exact Tate theta distribution identities, prove good-place
integrality of the determinant complex, identify the Hodge weight and
parabolic slope of the Steinberg packet, and obtain a conductor-plus-level
bound for the root discriminant of the full torsion field.  We also construct
one primitive integral vector which follows all three locally canonical
Legendre boundary directions at their full arithmetic depths.  On the
disproof side we derive the exact unequal-perfect-power gap criterion and prove
that polynomial, fixed-curve, and support-preserving Belyi parametrizations
cannot satisfy its strict exponent budget.  Several tempting global shortcuts
are eliminated by explicit counterexamples: fixed Galois packets cancel,
generic projective selectors reach exactly the cancellation threshold, and
root-discriminant plus finite unitness does not control archimedean packet
height.  The remaining proof-side problem is an explicit arithmetic metric
comparison for a modular Steinberg packet; the remaining disproof-side problem
requires a genuinely sparse, non-parametric close-perfect-power or radical-small
sequence.  No unconditional proof or disproof of $abc$ is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The target and the proof policy}

For pairwise coprime positive integers $a,b,c$ with $a+b=c$, put
\[
  R(a,b,c)=\operatorname{rad}(abc).
\]
The logarithmic $abc$ conjecture states that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there
exists $C_\varepsilon$ such that
\[
  \log c\le (1+\varepsilon)\log R(a,b,c)+C_\varepsilon.
\]
Every theorem below is proved from explicit hypotheses.  A route is abandoned
only after a counterexample or a no-go theorem identifies the failing
mechanism.  Conditional bridge structures are not counted as proofs of the
conjecture.

\section{Power cores forced by a hypothetical counterexample}

For a positive integer $n$, define
\[
  \operatorname{exc}(n)=\frac{n}{\operatorname{rad}(n)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[multiplicity excess]
If $c>R(a,b,c)^{1+\varepsilon}$, then
\[
 \operatorname{exc}(a)\operatorname{exc}(b)
 \operatorname{exc}(c)
 >c^{\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)}.
\]
If $x=\max\{a,b\}$, then
\[
 \max\{\operatorname{exc}(x),\operatorname{exc}(c)\}
 >2^{-1/2}c^{(1+2\varepsilon)/(2(1+\varepsilon))}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pairwise coprimality gives
\[
 \operatorname{exc}(a)\operatorname{exc}(b)
 \operatorname{exc}(c)=\frac{abc}{R(a,b,c)}\ge \frac{c}{R(a,b,c)}.
\]
The assumed inequality gives the first claim.  Since $x\ge c/2$ and
$\operatorname{rad}(x)\operatorname{rad}(c)\le R(a,b,c)$,
\[
 \operatorname{exc}(x)\operatorname{exc}(c)
 \ge \frac{c^2}{2R(a,b,c)},
\]
and the second assertion follows by taking square roots.
\end{proof}

For $k\ge2$, set
\[
 U_k(n)=\prod_p p^{\lfloor v_p(n)/k\rfloor}.
\]
Primewise,
\[
 U_k(n)^k\ge \frac{n}{\operatorname{rad}(n)^{k-1}}.
\]
In particular a prospective counterexample yields both a large square core
and a large cube core, hence representations
\[
 Ax^2+By^2=Cz^2,
 \qquad
 A'x^3+B'y^3=C'z^3
\]
with squarefree or cube-free coefficients and a polynomially large power
coordinate.  These reductions define independent conic and genus-one routes.

\section{The unequal-perfect-power disproof budget}

Suppose fixed integers $m,n\ge2$ admit infinitely many coprime positive
solutions with
\[
 0<y^n-x^m\le (y^n)^\theta.
\]
The associated triple
\[
 a=y^n-x^m,\qquad b=x^m,\qquad c=y^n
\]
satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rad}(abc)\le c^{\theta+1/m+1/n}.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 \theta+\frac1m+\frac1n<1,
\]
then one fixed positive $\varepsilon$ gives infinitely many violations of
$abc$.  For square--cube gaps the exact threshold is $\theta<1/6$.

\subsection{Polynomial and fixed-curve no-go theorems}

\begin{theorem}[polynomial gap barrier]
Let $k$ have characteristic zero and suppose pairwise coprime nonzero
polynomials satisfy
\[
 X^m+A=Y^n.
\]
Writing $D=\max\{m\deg X,n\deg Y\}$, one has
\[
 \deg A\ge D\left(1-\frac1m-\frac1n\right)+1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mason--Stothers gives
\[
 D\le \deg\operatorname{rad}(XAY)-1
 \le \deg X+\deg Y+\deg A-1.
\]
Use $\deg X\le D/m$ and $\deg Y\le D/n$.
\end{proof}

The same argument over the function field of a fixed genus-$g$ curve gives
\[
 h(A)\ge
 D\left(1-\frac1m-\frac1n\right)-(2g-2).
\]
Thus a fixed saving below the threshold forces genus to grow linearly with
$D$.  Polynomial, rational-function, bounded-genus, and fixed-curve
parametrizations cannot disprove $abc$ through close perfect powers.

\section{Riemann--Hurwitz obstruction to algebraic amplification}

Let $f:\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1$ have degree $d$, let
$D=\{0,1,\infty\}$, and let $r=\#f^{-1}(D)$ count distinct geometric points.

\begin{theorem}[boundary count]
One has $r\ge d+2$.  Equality holds precisely when all ramification lies above
$D$.  In particular, if $f^{-1}(D)\subseteq D$, then $d=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ramification contribution above $D$ is $3d-r$.  Riemann--Hurwitz gives
total ramification $2d-2$.  Therefore $3d-r\le2d-2$.
\end{proof}

This excludes support-preserving one-variable Belyi self-amplification.  Any
successful exceptional-set amplifier must use arithmetic common factors,
correspondences, several variables, or input-dependent maps.

\section{Tate cyclic-line energies and exact cancellation}

Let $E_q$ be a split Tate curve and set $L=-\log|q|>0$.  For an odd prime
$\ell$, the sum of the local N\'eron function over the canonical cyclic line is
\[
 A_\ell L,
 \qquad A_\ell=\frac{\ell-1}{12},
\]
while every noncanonical cyclic line contributes
\[
 B_\ell L,
 \qquad B_\ell=-\frac{\ell-1}{12\ell}.
\]
Thus $A_\ell+\ell B_\ell=0$.  The full torsion packet and every fixed
place-independent weighted packet cancel after the complete transitive
projective Galois average.  The generic $1/(\ell+1)$ CRT/Minkowski selector
also reaches exactly the cancellation threshold
\[
 B_\ell+\frac{A_\ell-B_\ell}{\ell+1}=0.
\]
These are strict no-go theorems for linear packets and generic full-orbit
selection, not for nonlinear or globally labelled successors.

\section{Explicit cyclic sections and theta distributions}

For a cyclic subgroup $C\subset E[\ell]$, define
\[
 \psi_C(X)=
 \prod_{\{P,-P\}\subset C\setminus\{O\}}
 (X-x(P)).
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\psi_C(x))
 =\sum_{P\in C\setminus\{O\}}(P)-(\ell-1)(O).
\]
The polynomial descends for a Galois-stable line and is integral at good
places away from $\ell$.  The resulting determinant-of-cohomology torsion and
its dual have norm one at such places.

For the Tate product theta function, if $s^\ell=q$, the exact cyclic
identities are
\[
 \prod_{\zeta^\ell=1}\Theta_q(\zeta u)
 =\frac{C(q)^\ell}{C(q^\ell)}\Theta_{q^\ell}(u^\ell),
\]
and
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Theta_q(s^ju)
 =\frac{C(q)^\ell}{C(s)}
  s^{-\ell(\ell-1)/2}u^{-(\ell-1)}\Theta_s(u).
\]
The automorphy monomial is essential: it produces the negative noncanonical
Bernoulli slope.  Translating from the divisor at $[-1]$ to the identity gives
the analytic trivialization matching the kernel section.

\section{The Steinberg packet and its geometric slope}

Under a Weierstrass scaling $x=u^2x'$, $y=u^3y'$, the cyclic kernel section
has Hodge weight $\ell-1$.  The augmentation packet is therefore naturally
\[
 \mathcal W_\ell=\omega^{\ell-1}\otimes\mathcal{St}_\ell,
\]
where $\mathcal{St}_\ell$ is the augmentation constituent of the finite
permutation local system on $\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_\ell)$.

The finite-monodromy factor is parabolically polystable of degree zero.  On the
level-two modular stack, maximal Kodaira--Spencer gives
\[
 \operatorname{pardeg}(\omega)=\frac12.
\]
Hence
\[
 \mu_{\max}^{\mathrm{par}}(\mathcal W_\ell)
 =\frac{\ell-1}{2}.
\]
The ratio to the canonical Tate coefficient is exactly six:
\[
 \frac{(\ell-1)/2}{(\ell-1)/12}=6.
\]
This determines the geometric leading coefficient but not the arithmetic
metric comparison.

\section{Descent fields and an archimedean obstruction}

Let $K=\mathbf Q(E[\ell])$ for a semistable elliptic curve and a good prime
$\ell\ge5$.  Local different estimates give
\[
 \log\operatorname{rd}(K)
 \le
 \left(1-\frac1\ell\right)
 \log\operatorname{rad}(N_E)+2\log\ell,
\]
up to the fixed local correction required by the chosen Frey model.  Every
subfield has no larger root discriminant.

This bound does not control arbitrary packet height.  In the fixed field
$\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)$, let $u=3+2\sqrt2$ and
\[
 P_n=[u^n:u^{-n}].
\]
Both coordinates are finite-place units, their product is one, and the field
root discriminant is fixed, yet
\[
 h(P_n)=n\log u\to\infty.
\]
Therefore the remaining theorem must use the explicit modular or theta
functional identities; finite unitness and field discriminant alone are
insufficient.

\section{A simultaneous selector for the three Legendre directions}

Let $a+b=c$ and define
\[
 v_{a,b}=(b,-a)\in\mathbf Z^2.
\]
For the three lines
\[
 L_a=\langle(1,0)\rangle,
 \quad L_b=\langle(0,1)\rangle,
 \quad L_c=\langle(1,1)\rangle,
\]
one has
\[
 v_{a,b}\equiv b(1,0)\pmod a,
\quad
 v_{a,b}\equiv-a(0,1)\pmod b,
\quad
 v_{a,b}\equiv-a(1,1)\pmod c.
\]
Thus one primitive global vector follows all three locally canonical boundary
directions at their full prime-power depths.  Moreover
\[
 \frac c2\le\|v_{a,b}\|_\infty<c.
\]
This is stronger than a generic projective selector because it uses the
additive relation $a+b=c$ itself.  The remaining problem is to realize this
adelic subline in the actual metrized theta/determinant packet without
reintroducing the full height through the archimedean norm.

\section{The current decisive theorem}

The proof-side programme has been reduced to an explicit arithmetic statement.
For the normalized Hecke-discriminant or cyclic-theta Steinberg packet
$\Sigma_\ell(E)$, one seeks
\[
 h_\infty(\Sigma_\ell(E))
 \le
 \left(\frac{\ell-1}{2}+o(\ell)\right)
 (\operatorname{Different}(E)+\operatorname{Conductor}(E))
 +O(\ell\log\ell),
\]
with the finite norms fixed by the cyclic distribution formulas and the
integral determinant complex.  Together with the local coefficient
$(\ell-1)/12$ and a quantifier-correct auxiliary prime satisfying
$\log\ell=o(\log c)$, this would imply
\[
 \frac16Q\le(1+o(1))(D+N)+O(\log\ell)
\]
and hence $abc$.

The disproof-side programme requires an infinite sparse sequence not arising
from a fixed algebraic parametrization, for example unequal perfect powers with
a fixed saving below the function-field threshold and independently controlled
radical.  No such sequence is presently constructed.

\section{Formalization ledger}

Lean modules in the repository formalize, among other components, the
quantifier-correct $abc$ and disproof statements, the scalar perfect-power gap
budgets, the Riemann--Hurwitz boundary-count arithmetic, fixed-packet
cancellation, the projective-dimension barrier, the theta automorphy exponent,
the two-place unit-spread scalar identity, the three-boundary logarithmic
semistability inequalities, and the simultaneous selector congruences.  The
algebraic-geometry and arithmetic-metric layers are formalized only after their
mathematical statements and normalizations are fixed; no unresolved source
theorem is introduced as an axiom.

\section{Conclusion}

The $abc$ conjecture remains neither proved nor disproved.  The present work
narrows the proof side to a concrete modular metric inequality and narrows the
disproof side to genuinely sparse, non-parametric arithmetic constructions.
It also supplies explicit local formulae and several rigorous barriers which
prevent known failed mechanisms from being recycled under different names.
